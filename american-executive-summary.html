Executive summary · US · 2025-2026

American Executive Summary

A short plain-English read on the themes that matter most, the subthemes moving fastest, the bottlenecks worth backing, and the business models under the most pressure.

In 2025-2026, demand is still there. What changed is who can actually keep the margin. The winners usually control the customer relationship, handle the paperwork and proof burden, fit the new consumer mood, or own the physical constraint underneath the market.

Top Themes

Industrial / Institutional

Scale, Financialization, and the Owned Economy

More of the economy is being shaped by scale owners, roll-up logic, institutional asset holders, and platform economics that centralize purchasing power, compliance, and capital access

Platform control, roll-ups, and centralized purchasing keep widening the gap.Workflow software, certification, and managed compliance budgets keep deepening.Healthcare operators have to absorb this shift in a market where the US dental market, worth $196 billion in 2026, is consolidating rapidly as reimbursement stagnation and workforce shortages push independent practitioners into DSO networks while technology and aging populations provide modest growth.
Industrial

Physical Reindustrialization and Infrastructure

The physical economy has become strategic again. Tariffs, reshoring, power demand, logistics, and infrastructure needs are repricing materials, locations, and the trades that make modern buildout possible

Domestic capability, logistics resilience, and buildout bottlenecks matter more in valuation.Company mix is already separating, with 1 advantaged examples and 0 exposed examples inside this subtheme set.Manufacturing operators have to absorb this shift in a market where US steel bounced from tariffs in 2025-2026 but faces structural headwinds: global oversupply, flat demand, and the permanent shift to lighter vehicles—making recovery unlikely despite short-term margin gains.
Societal / Industrial

Space, Housing, and Local Friction

Where Americans live, commute, and locate activity is being reorganized by housing lock-in, office impairment, land scarcity near infrastructure, and the uneven value of local place

Adaptive reuse, housing lock-in, and power-adjacent land become recurring decision points.Advisory retainers, project staffing, and portable admin layers gain relevance.Real estate operators have to absorb this shift in a market where the US commercial real estate sector is experiencing a marked but uneven recovery in 2025-2026, driven by industrial and multifamily strength while office properties face ongoing distress and adaptation pressures.
Technological / Industrial

Machine Intelligence and Compute Buildout

AI is not just a software feature cycle. It is simultaneously a workflow-compression story, a compute and power infrastructure story, and a market concentration story around who can afford the stack

Automation budgets, inference demand, and power-ready site competition keep rising.Adaptive reuse, housing lock-in, and power-adjacent land become recurring decision points.Technology & digital operators have to absorb this shift in a market where AI infrastructure demand has turned the colocation industry from a physical space problem into a power-grid problem, making electricity access and regulatory tariffs the new competitive moats.

Fastest-Accelerating Subthemes

  • Automation Becomes Labor Arbitrage: Many automation decisions are no longer primarily about innovation theater. They are responses to unavailable labor, wage pressure, and the need for throughput consistency.
  • Franchise and Platform Governance Matter More Than Local Hustle: Local operators increasingly sit inside systems governed by franchisors, marketplaces, and scaled workflow owners that define the real economics.
  • Functional Consumption Beats Empty Indulgence: Shoppers increasingly want food and drink to do something specific: energize, hydrate, recover, sharpen focus, or support long-term health.
  • The Data-Center Land Rush Creates New Scarcity: AI demand turns power-ready land, colocation capacity, cooling systems, and permitting speed into strategic bottlenecks.
  • AI Seeps Into Boring Industries Through Workflows First: The most durable AI adoption may come from embedding into routine workflows in finance, logistics, healthcare, and regulated admin rather than from flashy standalone consumer products.
  • Asset Owners Keep Gaining Relative Power: The owner of land, rights, infrastructure, or financial rails often captures more durable economics than the visible operator working on top of those assets.

Most Investable Bottlenecks

  • Asset control over visible operation: The owner of the land, rail, catalog, or centralized platform is often capturing more value than the frontline operator.
  • Roll-up system quality: Acquisition discipline, shared services, and financing advantages are concentrating fragmented categories.
  • Power-ready industrial capacity: Power, transmission, cooling, and utility access are re-emerging as industrial selectors.
  • Specified domestic capability: Categories with procurement sensitivity or security logic are rewarding producers with domestic or nearshore reliability.
  • Adaptive reuse and housing lock-in solutions: Mobility friction and obsolete commercial space are reshaping local economics.
  • Utility-linked land and logistics corridors: The right geography is no longer neutral; the scarce site next to the unavoidable flow gets the rent.

How to Use the Stack

Operators

What to do next

  • Be clear about the chokepoint, workflow, or customer trust layer you actually control.
  • Reduce exposure to generic middle positions that only work when demand stays easy.
  • Turn macro change into concrete pricing, staffing, workflow, and channel decisions fast.
Investors

What to underwrite

  • Back scarce rails, mandatory workflows, and physical choke points.
  • Prefer repeatable proof systems over labor-heavy service models.
  • Avoid markets where demand is real but the profit pool sits somewhere else.
