Finance & Insurance

Finance and insurance show deep bifurcation. Auto financing is in crisis—affordability collapse, record subprime delinquencies, and EV lease depreciation are shrinking the market as payment shocks and credit stress ripple through lenders. Prime borrowers (720+ FICO) remain resilient, but subprime is brutal. Credit card processing and money transfer is booming (+11-20% annually, $87-208B market), driven by merchant volume and payment digitalization, but profitability is besieged by regulatory fragmentation, merchant fee revolt, and consolidation pressure (Stripe-PayPal rumors). Property and casualty insurance just returned to strong profitability after weak 2022-2023, but homeowner catastrophe and commercial casualty loss severity remain pressure points. Custody and asset services expand at 3.7% on digitization and alternatives, though fee pressure is intense. Debt collection is harvesting value from record consumer debt and retreating regulation.

The key question is whether the business owns rails, trust, workflow, or simply a shrinking spread.Prefer scaled institutions, data-rich intermediaries, and mandatory workflow layers over regional middlemen without strong moats.

Automobile Insurance

The US auto insurance industry swung from unsustainable losses (2023-2024) into a narrow profit window (95.3 combined ratio in 2025) as rate hikes moderate and AI-powered telematics reshape pricing, but margin compression looms in 2026 as competition returns and repair-cost inflation persists.

Finance & Insurance

Business Insurance

The US business insurance industry is a mature, consolidated market worth $288.3 billion (2025) that posted its strongest underwriting results in a decade, but faces deepening pressure from climate-driven losses, litigation-driven claim inflation, and AI-driven disintermediation threatening the 87% share held by traditional independent agents.

Societal Read

Finance & Insurance is being pushed by broader social and institutional strain through Regulated Software and the Admin State, Aging, Care, and the Assistance Economy, Scale, Financialization, and the Owned Economy, where labor availability, coordination burden, and managed systems increasingly shape the market.

Tensions
  • Regulatory compliance is a rising cost that hurts smaller, less-integrated players more than scaled incumbents, but it is also a cost that cannot be wished away or automated into nothing—it remains a permanent, defensive business.
  • The more firms and regulators rely on software and automation to manage compliance, the higher the stakes if that software fails, is breached, or becomes a single point of concentration risk.
  • Aging demand is volumetrically massive but economically constrained—payer pressure strips pricing power from providers and device makers, so only scale operators and vertically integrated systems survive.
Signals
  • Workflow software, certification, and managed compliance budgets keep deepening.
  • Operators lean harder on pricing architecture, pack-size changes, and cost takeout.
  • Demand tilts toward chronic care, assistance, protection, and age-adapted convenience.
What to do
  • Compliance Itself Becomes a Demand Engine implies that compliance demand can be sticky even when customers dislike paying for it.
  • Identity, Fraud, and Trust Infrastructure Thickens implies that trust infrastructure benefits when fraud methods improve faster than institutions adapt.
  • Reimbursement and Admin Layers Capture Value Around Care implies that in healthcare, admin performance can decide whether demand is monetizable.
What to underwrite
  • Returns concentrate in software and services that automate compliance, verification, and fraud control—niches that are defended by regulatory moats and switching costs.
  • Consulting firms, audit specialists, and compliance-automation platforms capture value as regulation multiplies and the compliance tax rises across every regulated industry.
  • Capital concentrates in vertically integrated care platforms aligned with payer incentives, home-care and monitoring technology, and financial services that price longevity risk.
