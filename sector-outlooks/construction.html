Construction outlook

Construction

Construction is a tale of two economies. Data-center buildout exploded 190% in 2025 starts, driven by AI compute demand, while traditional office construction hit a 10-year low. Residential builders are trapped in a $166–170B flat equilibrium, squeezed by tariff shocks, elevated rates, labor scarcity, and margin compression that is consolidating the industry around top-tier players (D.R. Horton, Lennar, PulteGroup). Remodeling and repair work (painting, insulation retrofit, plumbing supplies) stay stronger than new construction, but materials inflation and labor costs cut both ways. Infrastructure spending supports tunnel, transit, and bridge work, though volatility remains high. The bifurcation is brutal: scale players capture cash flow; smaller operators commoditize.

Follow bottlenecks: labor, permitting, power, and specialized scope matter more than generic backlog bragging.Own scarce trades, infrastructure-linked contractors, and disciplined operators positioned beside unavoidable build cycles.

Societal lens

Societal Read

Construction is being pushed by broader social and institutional strain through Space, Housing, and Local Friction, Work Without the Old Firm, where labor availability, coordination burden, and managed systems increasingly shape the market.

Space, Housing, and Local FrictionWork Without the Old Firm
Tensions
  • Office-heavy cities and regions have stranded assets and weakened tax bases, while places with logistics corridors and power access attract capital; but infrastructure builds slowly, and new buildout faces political resistance and environmental scrutiny.
  • Housing lock-in reduces labor flexibility and pushes up service costs in high-cost areas, but policy options (zoning reform, construction acceleration) remain politically fraught and regionally contested.
  • Companies gain flexibility and margin defense through fractional staffing but lose depth of institutional knowledge and investment in junior development; long-term competitive capability may erode.
Signals
  • Office towers convert to apartments; rural land near power stations attracts investment; logistics parks boom.
  • Consulting, managed service providers, and independent contractor platforms expand rapidly.
  • Advisory retainers, project staffing, and portable admin layers gain relevance.
What to do
  • Office Obsolescence Is a Structural Shift implies that office exposure should be segmented by asset quality and adaptability, not averaged.
  • Adaptive Reuse Becomes a New Development Discipline implies that reuse work requires technical coordination and permitting fluency.
  • Housing Lock-In Distorts Mobility and Spend implies that transaction-light housing environments can still support strong maintenance and retrofit demand.
What to underwrite
  • Value concentrates in logistics land, data-center-ready sites, and adaptive-reuse opportunities in places with strong residential demand; traditional downtown office portfolios depreciate or require capital for repositioning.
  • Infrastructure-linked land (utility corridors, power-transmission proximity, fiber runs) becomes scarce and expensive, capturing rent that flows to landowners and developers positioned ahead of data-center or manufacturing investment.
  • Returns concentrate in platform businesses that coordinate fractional labor and in specialized training and credential providers that serve atomized workers.
