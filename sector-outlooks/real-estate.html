Real Estate outlook

Real Estate

Real estate splits into winners and losers by use case. Housing undersupply and multifamily absorption support development in constrained markets, while industrial and AI-datacenter demand surge. Conversely, secondary office and traditional retail face structural decline. Apartment rents plateau after years of growth, forcing rent-stabilized markets into profitability crises. Conveyancing automates and consolidates, while property management follows rent and occupancy cycles with limited upside.

Read assets through present-day flows of work, housing, logistics, and power rather than old category labels.Prefer logistics, utility-linked land, adaptive reuse capability, and assets aligned with current demand geometry over weak commodity office exposure.

Apartment Rental in the US

Apartment rents have stopped climbing and flat-lined after years of double-digit growth, exposing oversupply in the Sun Belt while rent-controlled markets squeeze landlords between frozen pricing and soaring costs, driving industry consolidation and bankruptcies.

Commercial Real Estate in the US

The US commercial real estate sector is experiencing a marked but uneven recovery in 2025-2026, driven by industrial and multifamily strength while office properties face ongoing distress and adaptation pressures.

Conveyancing Services in the United States

Conveyancing services—property title and settlement work for residential and commercial real estate—is a 26 billion-dollar mature industry being reshaped by high mortgage rates crushing home sales, commercial M&A offsetting losses, and rapid automation adoption reducing closings delays.

Who's advantaged
  • Institutional property managers and REIT platforms with scale to absorb vacant secondary office space and pivot to industrial or residential
  • AI-datacenter developers and power-adjacent land with government and hyperscaler demand outpacing supply
  • Online rental marketplaces (Apartments.com, Zillow Rentals) capturing scale and data leverage in high-velocity transaction markets
Who's exposed
  • Regional mall operators managing obsolete suburban assets with no profitable adaptive-reuse path
  • Multifamily owners in overbuilt Sun Belt markets now competing on price with no rent upside
  • Small independent conveyancing shops and title firms squeezed by automation and consolidation
Societal lens

Societal Read

Real Estate is being pushed by broader social and institutional strain through Space, Housing, and Local Friction, Aging, Care, and the Assistance Economy, where labor availability, coordination burden, and managed systems increasingly shape the market.

Space, Housing, and Local FrictionAging, Care, and the Assistance Economy
Tensions
  • Office-heavy cities and regions have stranded assets and weakened tax bases, while places with logistics corridors and power access attract capital; but infrastructure builds slowly, and new buildout faces political resistance and environmental scrutiny.
  • Housing lock-in reduces labor flexibility and pushes up service costs in high-cost areas, but policy options (zoning reform, construction acceleration) remain politically fraught and regionally contested.
  • Aging demand is volumetrically massive but economically constrained—payer pressure strips pricing power from providers and device makers, so only scale operators and vertically integrated systems survive.
Signals
  • Office towers convert to apartments; rural land near power stations attracts investment; logistics parks boom.
  • Consulting, managed service providers, and independent contractor platforms expand rapidly.
  • Demand tilts toward chronic care, assistance, protection, and age-adapted convenience.
What to do
  • Office Obsolescence Is a Structural Shift implies that office exposure should be segmented by asset quality and adaptability, not averaged.
  • Adaptive Reuse Becomes a New Development Discipline implies that reuse work requires technical coordination and permitting fluency.
  • Housing Lock-In Distorts Mobility and Spend implies that transaction-light housing environments can still support strong maintenance and retrofit demand.
What to underwrite
  • Value concentrates in logistics land, data-center-ready sites, and adaptive-reuse opportunities in places with strong residential demand; traditional downtown office portfolios depreciate or require capital for repositioning.
  • Infrastructure-linked land (utility corridors, power-transmission proximity, fiber runs) becomes scarce and expensive, capturing rent that flows to landowners and developers positioned ahead of data-center or manufacturing investment.
  • Capital concentrates in vertically integrated care platforms aligned with payer incentives, home-care and monitoring technology, and financial services that price longevity risk.
