Energy & Environment

Energy is consolidating around production and infrastructure while small players get wedged. Record 2025 US hydrocarbon output (crude, gas) supported oil field services, but rig counts softened and producer capital discipline constrained revenue. Coal is in terminal decline as utilities flee thermal generation for cheaper gas and renewables; coal wholesalers face lower 2026 demand despite $19B revenue base. Precious-metal mining is booming on gold prices ($5,200+/oz in 2026) and central-bank demand, but production growth is hobbled by labor shortages, aging infrastructure, and tariff-driven costs. Waste and recycling operators earn reliable tipping fees, but lower waste throughput and regulatory complexity pressure margins. The winners are those with long-life infrastructure (geothermal, waste), locked government contracts, or commodity upside (gold, critical minerals).

Energy businesses increasingly sit inside the AI, electrification, and compliance stack, not outside the economy’s main story.Prefer transmission, power-linked infrastructure, and environmental compliance enablers over undifferentiated exposure to commodity swings.

Coal & Natural Gas Power in the US

Coal and natural gas power, which generated over half of US electricity in 2022, now face a pincer movement from rising fuel costs and rapidly growing renewables, even as utilities suddenly pivot to expand natural gas capacity to power AI data centers.

Coal & Ore Wholesaling in the US

The US Coal & Ore Wholesaling industry remains a mature, low-concentration distribution market whose 2025 revenue base is still near $19 billion, but 2026 demand indicators point lower as utility coal consumption retreats while nonfuel mineral demand stays supported by metals, critical-mineral and industrial supply-chain needs.

Energy & Environment sits inside a harder industrial environment through Machine Intelligence and Compute Buildout, Physical Reindustrialization and Infrastructure, where bottlenecks, system ownership, compliance, and infrastructure constraints matter more than broad end-market optimism.

Machine Intelligence and Compute BuildoutPhysical Reindustrialization and Infrastructure
Tensions
  • AI productivity gains benefit capital-rich early adopters and scale players; workers in automation-vulnerable roles face wage pressure or displacement, and smaller firms cannot afford the compute or licensing costs that scale players absorb.
  • Power demand from data centers collides with grid capacity, environmental limits, and local political resistance; buildout is necessary but faces delays and will concentrate infrastructure investment geographically.
  • Scale players and capital-rich firms can afford reshoring and power-adjacent land; smaller manufacturers and regional players face margin pressure from tariffs without the resources to relocate.
Signals
  • Automation budgets, inference demand, and power-ready site competition keep rising.
  • Adaptive reuse, housing lock-in, and power-adjacent land become recurring decision points.
  • Domestic capability, logistics resilience, and buildout bottlenecks matter more in valuation.
What to do
  • The Data-Center Land Rush Creates New Scarcity implies that the AI trade includes real estate, utilities, and heavy construction, not just software.
  • Workflow Compression Rewrites Knowledge Work implies that service firms need a deliberate view on which labor layers remain differentiated.
  • AI Concentrates Capital and Strategic Power implies that infrastructure ownership can matter more than app-level novelty.
What to underwrite
  • Returns concentrate in model-training platforms, data-center infrastructure, power generation, and cooling-system vendors that enable AI buildout.
  • Firms that embed AI into essential workflows and lock in customers also lock in regulatory and switching-cost advantages that compound returns.
  • Returns concentrate in infrastructure, land with utility access, electrical and cooling equipment, and firms that own or operate reshored manufacturing or logistics capacity.
