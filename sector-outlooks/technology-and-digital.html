Technology & Digital

Tech bifurcates into defensible scale and niche specialization. AI, cloud, cybersecurity, and enterprise software command pricing power and growing budgets. Payment processing and fintech infrastructure expand with e-commerce and wallet velocity. But commodity bootcamps, general web design, and consumer-facing content compete toward zero. Data-center power scarcity reprices infrastructure and consolidates operators. Precision agriculture remains adoption-constrained by farm size and capital availability.

AI and software advantage depend on workflow ownership, infrastructure access, and trust, not just feature velocity.Prefer embedded workflow software, compute-adjacent infrastructure, and control layers with recurring necessity.

Societal lens

Societal Read

Technology & Digital is being pushed by broader social and institutional strain through Work Without the Old Firm, Regulated Software and the Admin State, where labor availability, coordination burden, and managed systems increasingly shape the market.

Work Without the Old FirmRegulated Software and the Admin State
Tensions
  • Companies gain flexibility and margin defense through fractional staffing but lose depth of institutional knowledge and investment in junior development; long-term competitive capability may erode.
  • Workers gain autonomy in skill selection and portfolio construction but lose stability, benefits continuity, and access to subsidized training; inequality between credential-holders and non-specialists widens.
  • Regulatory compliance is a rising cost that hurts smaller, less-integrated players more than scaled incumbents, but it is also a cost that cannot be wished away or automated into nothing—it remains a permanent, defensive business.
Signals
  • Advisory retainers, project staffing, and portable admin layers gain relevance.
  • Operators lean harder on pricing architecture, pack-size changes, and cost takeout.
  • Enterprise spending on governance tools, auditors, and compliance staff accelerates.
What to do
  • The Fractional Executive Layer Expands implies that advisory businesses should package expertise into recurring, productized offers.
  • Skills and Credentials Replace Slow Career Ladders implies that training providers need clearer links to wage gain and employability.
  • The Junior Rung Gets Compressed implies that firms may save cost now while weakening their long-term talent pipeline.
What to underwrite
  • Returns concentrate in platform businesses that coordinate fractional labor and in specialized training and credential providers that serve atomized workers.
  • Traditional staffing agencies and large employers' internal talent pipelines face pressure; private equity and platform operators gain leverage in labor-market arbitrage.
  • Returns concentrate in software and services that automate compliance, verification, and fraud control—niches that are defended by regulatory moats and switching costs.

    Industrial lens

    Industrial Read

    Technology & Digital sits inside a harder industrial environment through Machine Intelligence and Compute Buildout, Regulated Software and the Admin State, where bottlenecks, system ownership, compliance, and infrastructure constraints matter more than broad end-market optimism.

    Machine Intelligence and Compute BuildoutRegulated Software and the Admin State
    Tensions
    • AI productivity gains benefit capital-rich early adopters and scale players; workers in automation-vulnerable roles face wage pressure or displacement, and smaller firms cannot afford the compute or licensing costs that scale players absorb.
    • Power demand from data centers collides with grid capacity, environmental limits, and local political resistance; buildout is necessary but faces delays and will concentrate infrastructure investment geographically.
    • Regulatory compliance is a rising cost that hurts smaller, less-integrated players more than scaled incumbents, but it is also a cost that cannot be wished away or automated into nothing—it remains a permanent, defensive business.
    Signals
    • Companies invest heavily in AI tools and compete for data-center real estate near power sources.
    • Office towers convert to apartments; rural land near power stations attracts investment; logistics parks boom.
    • Enterprise spending on governance tools, auditors, and compliance staff accelerates.
    What to do
    • The Data-Center Land Rush Creates New Scarcity implies that the AI trade includes real estate, utilities, and heavy construction, not just software.
    • Workflow Compression Rewrites Knowledge Work implies that service firms need a deliberate view on which labor layers remain differentiated.
    • AI Concentrates Capital and Strategic Power implies that infrastructure ownership can matter more than app-level novelty.
    What to underwrite
    • Returns concentrate in model-training platforms, data-center infrastructure, power generation, and cooling-system vendors that enable AI buildout.
    • Firms that embed AI into essential workflows and lock in customers also lock in regulatory and switching-cost advantages that compound returns.
    • Returns concentrate in software and services that automate compliance, verification, and fraud control—niches that are defended by regulatory moats and switching costs.
