Bottom line: Workflow embeddedness in compute wins; pure distribution or generic software doesn't.

The core tension

You need compute + software synergy to avoid commoditization, but investing in both requires capital discipline and a clear wedge; spreading into too many bets dilutes both and makes you a generalist again.

Who's advantaged
  • Platforms that have woven compute into customer workflows and have high switching costs
  • Infrastructure leaders with distribution moats and expansion into higher-margin software/services
Who's exposed
  • Pure-play connectivity providers without differentiated software or workflow integration
  • Legacy software vendors clinging to older deployment models and unable to evolve into cloud-native offerings
Tensions

Workflow, compute, and installed-base advantage compound across large customer systems for software leaders.

Societal Read

Software and Network Platform is being shaped socially by Regulated Software and the Admin State, Scale, Financialization, and the Owned Economy, Space, Housing, and Local Friction, where labor formation, institutional burden, demographic strain, and coordination complexity change which operators can actually scale.

Tensions
  • Regulatory compliance is a rising cost that hurts smaller, less-integrated players more than scaled incumbents, but it is also a cost that cannot be wished away or automated into nothing—it remains a permanent, defensive business.
  • The more firms and regulators rely on software and automation to manage compliance, the higher the stakes if that software fails, is breached, or becomes a single point of concentration risk.
  • Consolidated players can absorb compliance costs and rate risk that squeeze independents; but consolidation also means fewer competitors, tighter coordination, and systemic concentration risk if anchor players fail.
Signals
  • Workflow software, certification, and managed compliance budgets keep deepening.
  • Operators lean harder on pricing architecture, pack-size changes, and cost takeout.
  • Business services operators have to absorb this shift in a market where the US environmental consulting market is worth $27.4 billion (2025) and growing modestly at 1.6%, powered by stricter EPA rules and corporate climate targets, but held back by acute engineer shortages, tariff supply-chain headwinds, and consolidation among the Big Four firms.
