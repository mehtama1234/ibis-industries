Industrial memo

Physical Reindustrialization and Infrastructure

Operator angle: Nearshore positioning, electrician and trades capacity, and power-centric site control are now strategic assets. Tariffs and AI infrastructure demand make materials expensive and supply chains political—move fast on location and labor, or lock in with tied supply relationships.

Investor angle: Back infrastructure bets (power, cooling, trades labor), nearshore reshoring of specification goods, and sites near data centers or AI buildout. Avoid distant pure-export manufacturing and logistics-only plays that depend on old cost arbitrage.

Deep theme read

For decades, the US economy could treat physical stuff as a solved problem: cheap imports, just-in-time supply chains, and geographic arbitrage let capital chase cheaper labor overseas. That era is ending. Tariffs, reshoring pressure, AI's insatiable demand for power and cooling, and infrastructure needs for everything from utilities to data centers have made atoms matter again. The trades, materials, land with grid access, and logistics capacity are now binding constraints, not commodities. Whoever controls these flows—and the political will to move them—wins a once-in-a-generation repricing.

Bottom line: Physical proximity and power access now determine competitive advantage.

Electrical and Cooling Trades Become Picks and Shovels

Ai infrastructure, building retrofits, and electrification sharing the same labor pool and cooling becoming central to industrial and digital buildout are no longer isolated category quirks. They are showing up across business services, construction, and adjacent sectors, where the compute super-cycle and the labor squeeze are changing the operating baseline. That pushes the category toward a world where trade bottlenecks can be more investable than the headline megaprojects they support. The pattern is already visible in example operators such as Eaton, Emerson Electric Co, and MasTec.

Pressure points
  • Business services operators have to absorb this shift in a market where the US electricians industry is being pulled between a dead residential market and explosive demand from AI/EV/grid infrastructure, creating labor shortages, wage pressure, and consolidation among the 269,000 small contractors.
  • Construction operators have to absorb this shift in a market where the US HVAC industry is a $159 billion mature market (2026) being reshaped by residential weakness, commercial surge, regulatory refrigerant shifts, heat pump electrification, and a severe technician labor shortage that is pushing wages up 8% annually.
  • Throughput and quality become harder to defend when skilled labor is the bottleneck.
