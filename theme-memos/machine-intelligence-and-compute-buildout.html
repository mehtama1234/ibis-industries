Technological / Industrial memo

Machine Intelligence and Compute Buildout

Operator angle: Workflow embedding and infrastructure positioning are both durable—become the software that replaces clerical and analytical work, or own cooling, power, and permitting assets near data centers. Avoid consumer-facing AI features without durable workflow adoption or moat.

Investor angle: Bet on infrastructure winners (power, cooling, colocation, permitting), workflow-compression software in boring industries (finance, logistics, healthcare), and platforms that can bear massive capex. Avoid consumer-facing AI feature plays without network effects or scale efficiencies.

Deep theme read

AI is not a software story; it is simultaneously an infrastructure story, a labor-displacement story, and a concentration-of-power story. Training and running large models demands enormous electricity, cooling, and data-center capacity—so AI success depends on whether you have access to power and land near the grid. It also compresses workflows across finance, design, writing, analysis, and customer support, which reduces the number of people needed to do the same work. That compression benefits firms that can afford to deploy and integrate AI; it threatens workers in occupations that look like replaceable routines. The firms that can afford to build or access frontier models are a tiny set of capital-rich platforms and infrastructure players. Everyone else either buys from them, or falls behind.

Bottom line: AI is infrastructure and workflow compression—not a consumer feature story.
