Agriculture

US agriculture is caught between structural collapse and margin divergence. The cattle herd hit a 75-year low, reshaping beef supply and creating wild price swings, while corn prices have crashed to $4.15/bushel after record 2025 production, leaving most grain farmers with $180 per-acre losses. Vertical integration by major processors is accelerating, squeezing wholesalers and independent operators. Beekeeping is imploding—55.6% annual colony losses from varroa viruses—yet pollination fees spike 15% because California almonds have no alternative. Meanwhile, tariffs add $200+ million annually to nursery and floriculture supply chains, collapsing traditional wholesale margins across flower, nursery, and produce distribution.

        Business Services

        Business services is bifurcating between obsolescence and resilience. Copier wholesaling is in freefall (−9.8% in 2025), squeezed by digitalization, remote work, OEM direct-to-client shift, and tariff headwinds. Traditional office-equipment distribution has no future. Janitorial services, logistics consulting, and credit repair grow in absolute dollars, but document preparation stalls (AI transcription now 95%+ accurate) and private detective work softens despite post-pandemic recovery. Consolidation is relentless: small operators close or merge. The winning positions are highly specialized—recycling automation ($27.7B at 6-7% CAGR), managed print services inside OEM ecosystems, and fragmented labor-intensive services (maids, cleaners) where scale and brand matter.

        Construction

        Construction is a tale of two economies. Data-center buildout exploded 190% in 2025 starts, driven by AI compute demand, while traditional office construction hit a 10-year low. Residential builders are trapped in a $166–170B flat equilibrium, squeezed by tariff shocks, elevated rates, labor scarcity, and margin compression that is consolidating the industry around top-tier players (D.R. Horton, Lennar, PulteGroup). Remodeling and repair work (painting, insulation retrofit, plumbing supplies) stay stronger than new construction, but materials inflation and labor costs cut both ways. Infrastructure spending supports tunnel, transit, and bridge work, though volatility remains high. The bifurcation is brutal: scale players capture cash flow; smaller operators commoditize.

        Consumer Services

        Consumer Services

        Consumer services are splitting by discretion and defensibility. Golf is reversing a 20-year decline, attracting under-35 players and women, yet operators are margin-squeezed between pricing power constraints and rising labor-maintenance costs. Tutoring and driving schools gain on learning-loss and driver-safety demand but face digital price competition. For-profit universities struggle against falling enrollment (−2% in fall 2025) and federal earnings rules. The real resilience is in essentials—maids, nannies, and gardeners serving high-income households; childcare franchises growing despite tuition hikes and declining births. Funeral services are flat and consolidating (three national players), losing volume to cremation (63%+ in 2025). Most discretionary services are exposed to consumer retrenchment and can only defend margins through scale or specialization.

        Energy & Environment

        Energy & Environment

        Energy is consolidating around production and infrastructure while small players get wedged. Record 2025 US hydrocarbon output (crude, gas) supported oil field services, but rig counts softened and producer capital discipline constrained revenue. Coal is in terminal decline as utilities flee thermal generation for cheaper gas and renewables; coal wholesalers face lower 2026 demand despite $19B revenue base. Precious-metal mining is booming on gold prices ($5,200+/oz in 2026) and central-bank demand, but production growth is hobbled by labor shortages, aging infrastructure, and tariff-driven costs. Waste and recycling operators earn reliable tipping fees, but lower waste throughput and regulatory complexity pressure margins. The winners are those with long-life infrastructure (geothermal, waste), locked government contracts, or commodity upside (gold, critical minerals).

Custody and asset services expand at 3.7% on digitization and alternatives, though fee pressure is intense. Debt collection is harvesting value from record consumer debt and retreating regulation.

        Food & Drink

        Food & Drink

        Food and drink is contracting under multiple pressures: spirits are in sharp contraction (−2.2% total sales, bourbon −4.9% volume) after two decades of premiumization, hit by overcapacity, tariffs, and the 2026 bankruptcy of RNDC (second-largest distributor). Beer production dropped 5.7% in 2025; craft breweries fell to 9,344 in June 2026 with 481 closures; consumers are shifting to premium and non-alcoholic. Fast food is a mature $424B industry growing below 1% as consumer traffic deflates from GLP-1 adoption and retrenchment, while labor costs (California $20/hour minimum now standard) and tariff-driven food inflation relentlessly compress margins. Independent food trucks face saturation, rising costs, and fragmented local regulation. The only bright spots are convenience (spirits RTDs up 16.4%, smoothie bowls growing), premium positioning, and efficiency plays (automation, waste reduction). The industry is consolidating toward scale players with automation and brands; indies are commoditizing.

        Healthcare

        Healthcare bifurcates sharply: high-acuity inpatient and specialty care are consolidating around scale and automation, while primary and outpatient operators compete in a fragmented margin squeeze. Telehealth erodes geography-protected practices, private equity reshapes ownership, and labor scarcity forces wage inflation across clinics and hospitals. The rehab and mental-health sectors face existential pressure—Medicaid disenrollments evaporate patient volume, overdose rates plateau, and digital alternatives commoditize simpler cases, leaving only integrated networks and PE-backed consolidators standing.

Legacy capital-intensive sectors (brakes, glass, brick) face secular decline as substitutes and EVs reduce demand, while tariff-vulnerable importers scramble to find domestic alternatives or give up. Niche high-tech equipment (air purification, diagnostics, RFID systems) and defense-tied armored vehicles grow steadily, but small players suffocate under tariff passes-through and labor costs.
