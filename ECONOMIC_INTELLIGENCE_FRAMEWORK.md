# Economic Intelligence Framework

This repo now has a complete `1,491`-industry base layer. The next layer is not more raw briefs. It is interpretation: a structured way to read industrial, consumer, cultural, social, societal, and operator change through the US economy in `2025-2026`.

## End State

The end state is a usable intelligence system with five connected layers:

1. `Industry layer`
   - Every US IBIS industry has a current brief.
   - Each brief provides the factual substrate: size, growth, economics, major players, current dynamics, and dated developments.

2. `Force layer`
   - Recurrent patterns are grouped into cross-industry forces.
   - Each force is supported by evidence industries and sub-force writeups.

3. `Theme layer`
   - Forces are reorganized into broader domains: industrial, consumer, cultural/social, societal/institutional, and technological.
   - This is where the repo becomes a readable map of the economy rather than a list of sectors.

4. `Operator layer`
   - Themes are translated into business-model and operator implications.
   - The question shifts from "what is happening?" to "what does this mean for a distributor, clinic, contractor, retailer, manufacturer, lender, franchisee, or software vendor?"

5. `Company layer`
   - Individual company/business writeups can be grounded in the industry and force context.
   - This is the final applied layer: company pages are not standalone profiles; they are interpreted against the underlying structure.

## Core Taxonomy

### 1. Industrial Trends

Industrial trends describe how the physical economy is being reorganized.

Current force coverage in the repo already points to the main buckets:

- `Atoms Strike Back`
  - tariffs
  - nearshoring/reshoring
  - physical supply-chain repricing
  - import exposure
- `The Compute Super-Cycle`
  - data-center buildout
  - power scarcity
  - grid bottlenecks
  - electrical and cooling trades
- `The Great Consolidation`
  - PE rollups
  - franchise scaling
  - asset ownership concentration
  - services consolidation
- `The Real-Estate Reckoning`
  - office impairment
  - logistics/data-center rotation
  - adaptive reuse
  - housing/rate pressure
- `Commodity Whiplash`
  - fuels
  - metals
  - fertilizer/input volatility
  - agricultural cycle exposure
- `The Labor Squeeze`
  - skilled-trades shortages
  - wage pass-through pressure
  - care labor scarcity
  - automation as margin defense
- `The Electrification`
  - EV transition
  - batteries/copper/electrical content
  - aftermarket and insurance changes

Industrial reading frame:

- where capex is flowing
- where labor is the bottleneck
- where energy and infrastructure constrain growth
- where tariffs and politics alter cost structure
- where scale matters more than craftsmanship or local fragmentation

### 2. Consumer Trends

Consumer trends describe how household spending is being repriced, redirected, or culturally re-ranked.

Current repo forces already cover major consumer arcs:

- `The Hollow Middle`
  - premiumization vs value
  - mid-market collapse
  - dupe/private-label pressure
- `The Channel Shift`
  - e-commerce/platform migration
  - omnichannel survival
  - experiential retail as one of the last physical moats
- `The Health Reckoning`
  - GLP-1 effects
  - sugar and alcohol pressure
  - functional/better-for-you growth
- `The Experience Economy`
  - spending rotating from goods to experiences
  - live/event/venue resilience
  - travel/play/status-through-experience
- `The Margin Vise`
  - cost inflation vs consumer willingness to pay
  - price resistance
  - premium as shield

Consumer reading frame:

- where households trade down
- where they still pay up
- which categories are volume stories vs mix stories
- which consumer behaviors are structural rather than cyclical
- how health, convenience, and status reshape demand

### 3. Cultural and Social Trends

This layer is broader than consumer demand. It tracks changing norms, behaviors, identities, and work patterns.

The current force set already implies several strong cultural/social lines:

- `The Fractional Worker`
  - work unbundling
  - gig/fractional expert models
  - thinner firms
  - benefits instability shifted to workers
- `The Experience Economy`
  - social value moving from ownership to participation
  - status moving from goods to live/shared experiences
- `The Health Reckoning`
  - sober-curious behavior
  - wellness as identity
  - food/drink categories being culturally reclassified
- `The Channel Shift`
  - platform-native shopping behavior
  - the store becoming showroom, pickup point, or experience venue
- `The AI Rewiring`
  - changing ideas of knowledge work
  - junior labor compression
  - the human-premium vs automation split

Cultural/social reading frame:

- how work identity is changing
- how status consumption is changing
- how health behaviors are shifting from niche to mainstream
- how digital mediation changes what people buy and how they decide
- how younger and older cohorts diverge in behavior

### 4. Societal and Institutional Trends

This layer tracks demographic, regulatory, public-finance, and system-capacity changes.

Current repo forces point to the main societal/institutional buckets:

- `The Graying Market`
  - aging demand wave
  - senior care, chronic care, longevity finance
  - caregiver scarcity
- `The Immigration Squeeze`
  - labor supply effects in care, trades, fields, and plants
  - enforcement complexity
- `The Compliance Tax`
  - regulation creating demand for software, consulting, testing, and admin
  - compliance load favoring scale
- `The Pricing Power Collapse`
  - payer and reimbursement pressure
  - public/private price controls in healthcare-adjacent systems
- `Money Gets Unbundled`
  - shifts in banking, payments, credit, and financial intermediation
  - institutional winners changing under rate and regulatory stress
- `The Breach Economy`
  - trust/safety/security overhead as a structural cost of digital systems

Societal/institutional reading frame:

- where demographics create inevitable demand
- where regulation creates both drag and defensibility
- where public funding or reimbursement determines profitability
- where labor supply is a policy problem, not just a wage problem
- where institutional trust and risk management become their own industries

### 5. Operator and Business-Model Lenses

This is the translation layer from economic pattern to practical business implication.

The current repo already defines initial operator lenses:

- `The Local Services Rollup`
- `The Specialty Manufacturer`
- `The Health Services Operator`
- `The Experiential Venue`
- `The Food Production Niche`
- `The Regulated Admin Stack`
- `The Distribution Middleman`
- `Care & Family Infrastructure`

These should expand into a broader operator taxonomy:

- local labor-intensive services
- fragmented roll-up candidates
- reimbursement-driven healthcare operators
- physical distributors and credit-bearing intermediaries
- experience-led consumer venues
- regulated software/admin infrastructure
- niche manufacturers with specification moats
- asset-heavy owners exposed to rates, utilization, or regulatory load

Operator reading frame:

- what determines margin durability
- whether labor, capital, regulation, or demand is the binding constraint
- whether scale helps because of procurement, compliance, staffing, distribution, or branding
- whether the business is cyclical, demographic, policy-driven, or structurally advantaged

## Standard Questions This System Should Answer

At maturity, the system should answer:

- What is changing?
- Why is it changing?
- Which industries show the evidence?
- Is the change cyclical, structural, demographic, political, regulatory, technological, or cultural?
- Who benefits?
- Who gets squeezed?
- Which business models are advantaged?
- What should an operator, investor, acquirer, or builder do with that information?

## Working Thesis

The US economy in `2025-2026` is being reorganized by a few overlapping forces:

- labor scarcity
- AI and compute buildout
- regulation and compliance load
- demographic aging
- consumer bifurcation
- channel migration to platforms
- higher cost of capital and property repricing
- consolidation and financialization
- health behavior change
- politicized supply chains and industrial policy

The job of this repo is to turn those forces from scattered observations into a coherent system of evidence, interpretation, and operator guidance.
