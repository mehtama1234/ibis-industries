A force from the data · Industrial · 2025–2026

Atoms Strike Back

Trade war and tariffs force reshoring, nearshoring, and inventory hoarding — physical supply chains get expensive and political, and whoever moves fastest to nearshore wins.
How we found this This isn't a hunch — it's what fell out of reading 221 US industries side by side. The same force kept reappearing. Each page below is one corner of the economy where it shows up, with the real 2025–2026 numbers as proof.
The one idea

Tariffs forced reshoring and nearshoring, but only companies with capital and political access survived; thin-margin importers, small suppliers, and commodity-dependent industries are being consolidated or eliminated.

The auto reordering

The auto tariff shock
25% tariffs on cars and parts in 2025 forced the auto supply chain to abandon the US and pivot toward Mexico, killing a century of manufacturing geography overnight.
The EV transition & legacy parts
Electrification voids some parts and creates others; suppliers must pick a side — and tariffs force the choice fast.
B

Heavy materials & metals

Metals behind the tariff wall
Steel and aluminum tariffs ripple into every downstream builder and maker.
The materials of everything
Tariffs and energy costs repriced basic materials—cement, chemicals, glass, rubber—making nearshore sourcing the only math that works.
C

Reshoring the factory

Reshoring advanced manufacturing
Defense mandates and tariff uncertainty pull chip fabs, 3D printers, and electrical equipment back home—but at the cost of margin and speed.
Appliances & durable goods
A 50% tariff on imported appliances in mid-2025 crushed margins and forced US makers to nearshore fast—or absorb billions in costs.
D

Food & the retail edge

Food's tariff exposure
Tariffs and commodity shocks are reshaping meat, coffee, and soy processing—winners are those fast enough to nearshore or consolidate.
The retail import shock
Tariffs on clothing and furniture hit thin margins hard, collapsing brick-and-mortar chains while fast movers pivot to online and nearshore supply.

The big picture

Atoms strike back
Trade war tariffs make foreign supply chains expensive and risky; nearshoring wins, thin-margin importers lose.
