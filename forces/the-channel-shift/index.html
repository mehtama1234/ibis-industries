A force from the data · Cultural · 2025–2026

The Channel Shift

The store stopped being where you buy — e-commerce, marketplaces, and direct-to-consumer hollow out physical retail, and only omnichannel or the experiential niche survives.
How we found this This isn't a hunch — it's what fell out of reading 543 US industries side by side. The same force kept reappearing. Each page below is one corner of the economy where it shows up, with the real 2025–2026 numbers as proof.
The one idea

E-commerce won the sale, but omnichannel at scale and platforms with logistics won the customer; everyone else is closing stores and cutting jobs.

The categories that went online first

Electronics go online
Commodity electronics led the retail exodus — tariffs, pricing power, and the smartphone gutted specialty stores, leaving only online retailers and the smallest experiential niches.
Media & hobby retail
The store stopped being where you buy — Amazon and marketplaces hollowed out these categories, and only omnichannel giants and experential stores survive.
Apparel & the try-before-you-buy problem
Clothing shifted online despite fit friction; stores became showrooms, then closed.
B

The marketplace machine

The marketplace eats the middle
The platform takes a cut of everything and sets the terms of survival for sellers.
Direct-to-consumer brands
Small brands selling direct online are crushed by platforms and tariffs; only scaled omnichannel survives.
C

Who's holding on

Omnichannel or die
Big-box retailers survive the channel shift only by fusing online and store — ship-from-store, buy-online-pickup-in-store — or they fold.
The experiential survivor
Expertise, immediacy, and in-person experience are the moats physical retail has left.
D

The tensions

Tariffs & the de-minimis end
When the tariff loophole closed in August 2025, the price war moved offline—mass-market stores couldn't compete with Shein and Temu on cost anymore.
The closure wave
Thousands of stores close each year; the real-estate and jobs fallout compounds.

The big picture

The channel shift
The point of sale moved online; platforms and omnichannel scale win, standalone mid-market stores lose.
