A force from the data · Economic · 2025–2026

The Pricing-Power Collapse

Medicare cuts and payer power strip pricing from providers and device makers — volume rises with aging, but the price per unit keeps falling, and only scale survives.
How we found this This isn't a hunch — it's what fell out of reading 543 US industries side by side. The same force kept reappearing. Each page below is one corner of the economy where it shows up, with the real 2025–2026 numbers as proof.
The one idea

Medicare cuts reimbursement 1–2% yearly while demand grows 3–4%, making pricing scarce but volume abundant—only consolidators and proprietary innovators survive, and everyone else either exits, merges, or gets bought.

The providers get cut

Death by reimbursement cut
Medicare trims rates every year while labor costs rise — providers and device makers watch margins collapse even as volume grows.
The specialist squeeze
Medicare cuts and administrative burden push solo practitioners into consolidation and PE deals.
B

The makers get squeezed

The device price grind
Device makers face 1-2% annual price cuts as Medicare freezes reimbursement and hospitals consolidate their buying power.
Generic-drug deflation
Medicare-driven price cuts and PBM dominance compress margins and force smaller makers out of commodity generics.
C

The payers hold the power

Who holds the purse
Insurers are regaining pricing power through premiums and audits, while Medicare Advantage collapses and squeezed providers have nowhere to go.
The independent-practice exodus
Solo dentists and mental health clinics sell to networks and PE to escape the pricing squeeze.
D

The tensions

Volume up, price down
Aging brings more patients, but Medicare's 82-87 cent payment per dollar of care means volume alone can't save anyone from margin collapse.
The value-based bet
Providers and insurers are racing to move surgery and complex care out of hospitals and into cheaper sites — but reimbursement lags behind the costs of making it work.

The big picture

The pricing-power collapse
Medicare cuts and payer power strip pricing from providers and device makers — volume rises with aging, but the price per unit keeps falling, and only scale survives.
