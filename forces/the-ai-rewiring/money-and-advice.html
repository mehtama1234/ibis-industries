Money & advice

AI makes financial advice routine. Winners own scale, data, and the judgment machines can't yet replicate.

AI is automating financial advice and credit judgment. Passive funds are eating active management. Robo-advisors and AI credit scoring are replacing human gatekeepers. The result: margins compress for traditional advisors while winners consolidate into giant platforms or specialized boutiques.

What changed

AI made routine financial advice cheap and scalable. A robo-advisor can now manage a $10 million portfolio for 66 basis points—down from 75 basis points just two years ago. Fintech platforms like Wealthfront and Betterment won 42% of under-40 clients by offering transparent, algorithm-driven portfolios without the white-glove service. At the same time, AI credit scoring is displacing traditional models: Zest AI's system can approve 25% more loans while holding risk constant, and FICO's new Score 11 can score 9 million previously invisible consumers using rent and cash-flow data instead of credit history alone.

This killed the comfortable middle. Active mutual fund managers lost $1+ trillion in outflows in 2025 alone—the steepest year in an 11-year exodus—because only 38% of them beat their passive peer funds after fees. Traditional wealth advisors now compete on technology and scale, not on stock-picking skill. The advisory model is shifting: large firms are moving to W-2 advisor employment and technology-centric operations rather than asset aggregation. Consolidation hit 466 M&A deals in 2025, up 27% from 2024.

The evidence

  • Portfolio Management (Finance & Insurance): passive index funds and ETFs captured 55% of US mutual fund assets by 2025, while active mutual funds faced $1+ trillion in net outflows in 2025
  • Financial Advice (Finance & Insurance): wealth management M&A hit 466 deals in 2025 (up 27% from 2024), with Q1 2026 reaching 109 deals in a single quarter—highest in 8 quarters
  • Credit Scoring & Rating (Finance & Insurance): Zest AI's model approves 25% more loans while holding risk constant; FICO Score 11 (early 2026) brings 9 million previously credit-invisible consumers into the system
  • Gen Z & Millennials (Finance & Insurance): 42% of under-40 fintech users choose robo-advisors over traditional firms; Gen Z and millennials hold $3.1 trillion in equities as of August 2026, a record

Who rides it

Winners are consolidators with scale and data. BlackRock is buying data dominance (Preqin for £2.5B in March 2025; HPS Investment Partners for $12B in December 2024) and moving into illiquid alternatives—which generate over half the industry's projected revenue by 2030. Firms that own platforms and AI tooling win younger clients: fintech platforms grabbed 42% of under-40 users. Credit bureaus with proprietary alternative data (rent payment history, cash flow) and machine learning gain market share. Boutique specialists who focus on niche clients or complex strategies survive by offering judgment machines can't replicate yet—tax optimization, family office strategy, illiquid deal sourcing.

Who's squeezed

Squeezed are solo and small-firm advisors. Advisor fees fell from 75 basis points to 66 basis points in just two years as clients shop for cheaper alternatives. Active mutual fund managers are becoming extinct: only 38% of them beat passive peers after fees, and $1+ trillion flowed out in 2025 alone. Mid-size advisory firms that can't afford the technology stack or data moat are being acquired by larger consolidators. Traditional rating agencies and credit bureaus face disruption: Zest AI's model and alternative scoring systems are eating their moat by offering better loan approval rates. Cybersecurity breaches (TransUnion exposed 4.4M Americans in August 2025) have eroded consumer trust and forced defensive spending on security rather than innovation.

The main worry

The single biggest risk

The single biggest risk is that AI credit scoring locks in bias at scale. Zest AI's model works because it optimizes for lender revenue and risk, not fairness. If alternative scoring systems replace traditional bureaus before regulators impose guardrails, millions of credit-invisible consumers could be scored on proxy variables (rent, cash flow, phone bills) that correlate with race or ZIP code—automating discrimination at a speed humans can't police. The SEC is moving to overhaul rating agency rules, but consumer credit regulations lag behind AI deployment. A data breach at a major credit bureau could trigger a crisis: TransUnion's 4.4M-person breach in 2025 already raised consumer awareness and forced firms to invest in security. A breach of a robo-advisor or fintech platform holding retirement accounts could trigger a run on assets.

In one sentence
