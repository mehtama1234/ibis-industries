D · The new scarcities

The human premium

AI is making taste, judgment, and accountability scarce — and whoever can prove a person stands behind the work is winning.

The middle of every service industry is hollowing out. On one side, AI handles the routine: the boilerplate contracts, the commodity websites, the junior consultant hours. On the other side, the premium tier — work that requires taste, judgment, or accountability — is pulling away. The gap between cheap-and-AI and personally-guaranteed is widening fast.

What changed

AI made two things scarce at once: the commodity work (now cheap; anyone with a tool can do it) and the human relationship (now rare; less of it happens). When AI handles the routine, the real value concentrates at the top and bottom — either you need genuine judgment, or you need to trust someone's name is on it. Everything in the middle gets compressed.

Clients no longer pay for hours. They pay for outcomes, or they buy the robot. That shifts who wins. Design firms with strong brands and personalization are commanding 55-80% margins. Legal work is consolidating into bigger firms that can afford the AI infrastructure and handle mega-deals. Consulting is splitting into high-end strategy (outcomes-based pricing) and algorithmic automation — entry-level consultant roles are vanishing, replaced by AI specialists paid 56% more. The judgment work is becoming a luxury product. The labor is disappearing.

The evidence

  • Web design services: AI website builders market hit $6.3B in 2026 (26% YoY growth), with 60% of new websites now built partly with AI, compressing mid-market design labor by 20-40%, while top-tier agencies earning 55-80% margins by adding strategy and personalization.
  • Law firms: US legal services posted $426.7B revenue in 2025 on rate hikes (fees per lawyer up 16.8%) despite demand growth of only 1.9%, forcing 59 mergers in 2025 (up 18% YoY) and driving tech spending up 9.7-11% annually — yet 59% of corporate clients see no clear savings from AI tools.
  • Consulting: AI roles now outnumber entry-level consultants at top firms (a reversal from 2015); Accenture cut 11,000 roles in April 2025 while doubling AI specialist headcount to 80,000; federal consulting spend dropped $5B in early 2025, forcing industry consolidation.
  • Legal market 2026: Milbank's new salary scale ($235K starting associates; $455K for 8-year associates, effective July 2026) and 16+ announced mergers signal talent pressure at the premium tier, even as mid-market firms face structural weakness on expense-demand mismatch (expenses up 9.1% vs. demand up 1.9%).

Who rides it

The winners are the branded firms that moved upmarket. Top design agencies commanding 55-80% margins by adding strategy and personalization. Large law firms merging to afford AI infrastructure and land mega-deals (Hogan Lovells-Cadwalader combination = $3.6B revenue). Consulting's Big Four keeping federal access and outcome-based pricing power. And AI specialists themselves, earning 56% wage premiums. Anyone who owns the client relationship and can say 'a person stands behind this work' is winning.

Who's squeezed

The mid-market is squeezed. Mid-tier design shops competing globally against freelancers and AI builders. Regional law firms can't afford the AI stack or the merger premium. Junior and mid-level consultants are being replaced by AI automation; their hiring is down 40% since 2023. The commodity tier of every service is getting cheaper and smaller. And in-house clients are left confused — they're paying more for outside counsel while being told the AI should save them money.

The main worry

The single biggest risk

The biggest risk is that AI investment doesn't deliver the promised ROI, but firms keep spending anyway. In law, 59% of corporate clients say they see no clear savings from AI, yet firms are hiking tech budgets 9.7-11% annually. Law firms are burning cash on technology infrastructure while demand growth is stuck at 1.9% and expenses are up 9.1%. If firms can't show measurable returns on these bets, the margin compression becomes permanent — and the talent market at the top inverts.

In one sentence

The human premium in a nutshell

AI made routine cognitive work cheap and automated, pushing value to whoever controls the client relationship, owns the data, or proves a person stands behind the judgment — and hollowing out the middle tier entirely.
