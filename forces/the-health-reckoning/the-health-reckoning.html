The Health Reckoning

GLP-1 drugs and the sober-curious wave are shrinking demand across food and drink; only premium and health-aligned businesses survive.

Three converging forces are rewriting consumer spending on food and drink. GLP-1 medications reached 18% of U.S. adults by 2026, pharmaceutical weight loss now outpaces behavioral coaching, and Gen Z is drinking 30% less than Millennials at the same age. Businesses that rode volume growth for decades are now in structural decline—and only those positioned for premium, health-conscious, or high-margin customers are staying upright.

What changed

The retreat from traditional consumption is reshaping entire industries. Weight loss services, once a $3.8 billion industry in 2022, contracted to $2.6 billion by 2026 as GLP-1 adoption accelerated from 5.8% of adults in February 2024 to 18% by 2026. This wasn't a gradual shift—it was seismic. Weight Watchers, the 60-year-old icon of diet culture, filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy on May 6, 2025, and emerged as a private company in June 2025 after cutting $1.15 billion in debt. Traditional coaching and accountability models became obsolete almost overnight. The industry consolidated around telehealth GLP-1 platforms like Hims, Ro, and Noom, which now function as prescription delivery networks rather than lifestyle programs. Obesity rates fell from 39.9% in 2022 to 36.4% by 2026, shrinking the addressable market for legacy diet services and forcing smaller regional clinics into margin pressure or shutdown.

Wine and beer faced a different structural wall: Americans simply stopped drinking. The U.S. adult drinking rate hit 54% participation in August 2025—the lowest in 90 years. Gen Z monthly wine occasions collapsed 34% since 2019, with 71% citing health and wellness concerns. GLP-1 drugs compounded this by reducing on-premise alcohol spending by 30% and home consumption by 22% among users. Wine bars, a $3.4 billion industry, barely grew (−0.7% in 2026) and bifurcated sharply: private equity-backed, experiential wine bars scaled 6.8% CAGR with premium positioning and staff expertise, while traditional independents faced margin compression. The 2025 Dietary Guidelines removed explicit daily drink limits (seemingly a win for the industry), but the U.S. Surgeon General simultaneously warned that alcohol linked to nearly 100,000 annual cancer cases. On-premise bar visits dropped 3.4% in 2025 versus 2024. The affluent, older drinker (55+) held loyalty, but that demographic is aging and numerically shrinking.

Fast food and energy drinks split into opposite trajectories, both driven by health and volume dynamics. Fast food entered freefall—traffic declined 0.3% in 2025, accelerating to −2% in Q4 2025 versus Q4 2024. Households with a GLP-1 taker cut restaurant spending by an average 5.3%; dinner spending at fast food dropped 6% among regular users. The $423.7 billion industry (2026) is growing below 1% annually, squeezed by labor costs (California's $20/hour minimum wage in April 2024 forced price hikes of 8–12%), tariff-driven food inflation, and disappearing customer frequency. BurgerFi, TGI Fridays, and Uncle Julio's filed bankruptcy or sold in foreclosure within three months of each other in late 2024, victims of structural demand collapse. Energy drinks, by contrast, exploded to $23.9 billion by 2025, up from $11.7 billion in 2022—a 104% increase. But the category underwent radical transformation: sugar-free surpassed full-sugar in dollar sales for the first time as 70% of consumers sought to limit sugar intake. Consolidation wars erupted as Celsius paid $1.8 billion for Alani Nu (April 2025) to capture younger, health-conscious women, and then acquired Rockstar through a PepsiCo distribution deal (September 2025). Red Bull launched Red Bull Zero in November 2024. The winners were companies that moved fast to offer zero-sugar, performance-oriented products; the losers were traditional, sugar-laden competitors left behind.

What these industries share is that volume is no longer the business model. Consumers are eating less, drinking less, and when they spend, they're trading down to premium or health-aligned alternatives. Fast food chains couldn't offset traffic loss with pricing—customers just stopped coming. Wine bars couldn't rely on Gen Z to replace aging Boomers. Traditional weight loss coaching died overnight because GLP-1s work better and cost less in the long run. Energy drinks that didn't reformulate to zero-sugar faced category extinction. The health reckoning isn't gradual disruption; it's demand collapse followed by margin compression for everyone except the premium and the purpose-built.

The evidence

  • Weight loss services: Market contracted 31% (2022–2026) as GLP-1 adoption hit 18% of adults and Weight Watchers bankruptcy marked the end of 60-year diet-program era (2025)
  • Wine bars: U.S. adult drinking hit 54% participation in August 2025—lowest in 90 years—while Gen Z wine occasions fell 34% since 2019, forcing industry bifurcation toward premium and away from volume (2025)
  • Energy drinks: Category doubled to $23.9B by 2025 (from $11.7B in 2022) as sugar-free surpassed full-sugar in dollar sales for the first time, with Celsius paying $1.8B for Alani Nu to capture health-conscious women (2025)
  • Fast food: Traffic declined 0.3% in 2025, accelerating to −2% in Q4 2025 vs. Q4 2024, as GLP-1 adoption reduced spending by 5.3% among user households and California's $20/hour minimum wage (April 2024) forced price hikes of 8–12% (2025–2026)
  • Telehealth GLP-1 platforms: Consolidation accelerated as Hims acquired Zava (June 2025) and Celsius expanded via PepsiCo partnership (September 2025), favoring large, well-capitalized providers over regional clinics and legacy services (2025–2026)

Who rides it

Premium and health-aligned businesses. Experiential wine bars scaled by private equity grew 6.8% CAGR with 22% higher retention than traditional formats. Zero-sugar energy drinks, led by Celsius and Red Bull, doubled market size while driving consolidation and acquisition wars. Telehealth GLP-1 platforms like Hims and Ro grew vertically integrated supply chains and became the default weight-loss solution. Restaurants and chains that embraced smaller portions, healthier options, and premium positioning—rather than relying on volume and value—maintained margins. The formula is clear: premium pricing, health messaging, and experiential elements beat volume plays.

Who's squeezed

Volume-dependent, mass-market businesses. Traditional weight loss coaching and diet programs are extinct—Weight Watchers' bankruptcy was just the symbolic capstone. Fast food chains dependent on frequent, high-volume traffic face structural traffic collapse as GLP-1 adoption reduces appetite and frequency. Wine and beer suppliers targeting Gen Z or mid-market drinkers lost 30% of on-premise spending per GLP-1 user and faced a generational defection (Gen Z drinks 30% less than Millennials). Full-sugar energy drink makers and suppliers got stranded as the category pivoted to zero-sugar overnight. Regional weight loss clinics, independent wine bars without experiential positioning, and fast-food franchises without brand power or premium tiers all face margin compression and closure.

The main worry

The single biggest risk

The biggest risk is the floor beneath fast food and casual dining collapses faster than replacement demand materializes. Traffic is already down 2% year-over-year in Q4 2025. If GLP-1 adoption continues climbing (current trajectory suggests 25–30% of adults by 2027–2028), fast food may lose not just volume but consumer habit entirely—the customer stops visiting, finds alternatives, and never returns. Chains can't raise prices indefinitely without killing traffic further. Labor costs and tariff inflation are structural, not cyclical, so they can't cut their way to profitability. The bankruptcy wave (BurgerFi, TGI Fridays, Uncle Julio's in late 2024) may not be over; it may be the opening chapter. Regional fast-food and casual-dining operators—the 50,000+ independent franchises and small chains that lack scale to invest in automation or brand transformation—face wipeout.
