C · The health-aligned winners

When diet meets convenience

The health-conscious shift squeezes subscription fatigue from meal kits while forcing frozen-food makers to compete on speed, not nutrition—and tariffs are locking in higher costs for years.

GLP-1 drugs, the sober-curious wave, and the pull toward whole foods are thinning out the middle of packaged eating. What survives: either deeply convenient (frozen, ready-to-eat) or genuinely premium and health-forward (fresh meal kits, organic prepared). The subscriber model is collapsing under churn, and tariffs are making everything more expensive—especially the healthy options hardest to import.

What changed

Meal kit delivery, which promised the perfect marriage of health and convenience, is suffocating under its own subscription model. HelloFresh owned three-quarters of the US market in 2025, yet its revenue fell 9% that year because total orders dropped 12.3%—customers are simply exhausted. Monthly churn sits at a brutal 10.8% (73.6% annually), driven by meal fatigue, menu repetition (33% of cancellations cite boredom), and the simple fact that grocery apps like Instacart are growing 14% year-over-year and offer cheaper alternatives. Blue Apron, no longer even public, abandoned subscriptions entirely in 2026 and pivoted to à la carte ordering. HelloFresh itself is pivoting hard into ready-to-eat meals through its Factor brand, which now captures over 50% of the US RTE market. The message is clear: health-conscious customers still exist, but they don't want to be locked into a commitment or stuck cooking the same eight meals on repeat.

What is actually winning, then, is convenience in its rawest form—especially frozen. The frozen food industry grew 4.0% compound annually from 2021 to 2026 and now sits at $50.7 billion. But here is the shift: convenience has overtaken health as the primary reason people buy frozen (61% of shoppers in 2026 vs. 52% in 2025). Frozen meals are no longer sold as nutritious alternatives. They are sold as time-savers, period. Packaged brands are doubling down on new flavors and formats—Conagra alone launched over 50 new frozen products in June 2025—precisely because private-label frozen food hit an all-time high of 23.8% market share in the first half of 2026, up from 23.5% in 2025. The conversation has moved from "is this healthy?" to "can I eat in 8 minutes?"

This pivot comes as tariffs are crushing the cost structure of both industries. Steel and aluminum tariffs surged from $1.6 billion in 2024 to $7.79 billion in 2025—all of it flowing into packaging costs for frozen pizzas and packaged meals. Imported seafood, frozen fries, and vegetables face new duties spreading 12 to 18 months through the supply chain, meaning the real retail pain hits in late 2026 and into 2027. Meanwhile, labor scarcity is forcing automation: cobots at $25,000 to $40,000 with 18-month ROI are now standard in large plants, and 40% of frozen-food plants are running digital twins to optimize throughput. This means two things for the consumer. First, prices for convenient frozen meals will rise sharply over the next 18 months. Second, only the companies with scale (to absorb tariff costs and automate aggressively) and brand power (to justify higher retail prices) will survive the shakeout.

The evidence

  • Meal-kit market in terminal decline: HelloFresh revenue fell 9% in constant currency in 2025 while total orders dropped 12.3%, as monthly churn hit 10.8% and customers defected to cheaper grocery apps like Instacart (14% GMV growth in 2026).
  • Frozen food convenience dominates: convenience overtook health as primary shopping motivation (61% in 2026 vs. 52% in 2025), driving demand for ready-heat meals rather than fresh-meal-kit subscriptions.
  • Tariff shock hitting hard: steel and aluminum tariffs surged from $1.6B in 2024 to $7.79B in 2025, with frozen-food packaging costs and imported ingredients facing 12-18 month supply-chain delays, peaking in late 2026.
  • Private label stealing shelf space: private-label frozen food hit 23.8% market share in H1 2026 (all-time high), squeezing branded manufacturers as price pressure mounts; Conagra launched 50+ new frozen products in June 2025 to fight back.
  • Ready-to-eat meals emerging as growth vector: Factor (HelloFresh's RTE brand) captured 50%+ US market share; HelloFresh opened a 900-job Factor production facility in Germany in Q1 2026, abandoning cook-at-home as its core business model.

Who rides it

Ready-to-eat meal brands (especially HelloFresh's Factor) are winning big by moving away from the subscription trap. Conagra, Nestlé, and other scaled frozen-food makers with brand equity and automation capacity are holding margin and shelf space. Grocery delivery apps like Instacart and DoorDash are the real victors—growing 14%+ by offering cheaper, more flexible alternatives to subscriptions. Retailers benefit from all this: convenience drives traffic, and private-label frozen meals are taking share precisely because they undercut branded competitors on price.

Who's squeezed

The meal-kit subscription model is hemorrhaging customers and is basically dead for cook-at-home. Blue Apron, which fought to stay public, gave up and switched to à la carte. Mid-market frozen and packaged brands without scale or automation are getting squeezed from both sides: tariffs are raising their input costs, private label is eating their margin, and they lack the brand power to pass those costs to the consumer. Smaller, newer health-forward brands (plant-based frozen, specialized diet meals) are stalling—plant-based frozen products in particular are not moving units. The customer willing to pay a premium for tailored, fresh, health-conscious meals exists, but she is not subscribing to a box.

The main worry

The single biggest risk

Tariff costs are locked in for 12 to 18 months and will peak in late 2026 and into 2027. Consumers are not going to accept a 15-20% price hike on frozen dinners, so the real squeeze is on margin, not volume. Smaller manufacturers will be forced to cut corners (cheaper proteins, fewer vegetables, thinner packaging) or exit. The bigger risk: if tariffs persist or ratchet higher, the supply chain for frozen seafood, fries, and vegetables will hollow out, forcing more domestic sourcing at higher cost. By 2027, the frozen-food aisle will be dominated by two tiers—private label (cheap, basic) and premium branded (Conagra, Nestlé, premium RTE). The middle, which is most of the market today, is going to vanish.

In one sentence

When diet meets convenience in a nutshell

The health-conscious shift kills meal-kit subscriptions but props up convenience-first frozen food—until tariffs price all but the cheapest and the premium out of reach.
