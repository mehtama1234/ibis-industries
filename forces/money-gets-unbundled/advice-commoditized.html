C · Advice & the retail saver

Advice gets commoditized

Passive funds and robo-advisors compress fees; scale and trust win.

Money management used to be personal. A financial advisor took your call, knew your goals, and charged you accordingly. Now the machine is eating the business. Passive index funds and robo-advisors are pulling assets away from active managers while crushing the fees advisors can charge. The industry is consolidating fast—because scale is the only way to survive.

What changed

Ten years ago, active management was the norm and passive index funds were the cheap alternative. That's flipped. By 2025, passive index funds and ETFs held 55% of all mutual fund and ETF assets in the US, up from just 34% a decade earlier. At the same time, active mutual funds bled $1+ trillion in net outflows in 2025—the steepest year of an 11-year exodus. Only 38% of active fund managers beat their passive peers after fees in 2025, down from 42% the year before. Clients noticed. They left.

The fee compression is punishing. In 2024, advisors managing a $10 million portfolio charged around 75 basis points. By 2026, that fee had dropped to 66 basis points—a 12% cut in a single year. Institutional clients demand even lower rates. The portfolio management industry still generated $340 billion in revenue in 2025, but margins shrank to 32-36%, down from 36.1% in 2022. The industry has 75,000 establishments and 580,000+ employees, but the work itself is becoming commoditized: algorithm-driven advice, index tracking, and low-touch platforms can do what used to require a human.

The winner is scale. BlackRock's strategy tells the story. In December 2024, it bought HPS Investment Partners for $12 billion, expanding its private credit footprint by 40%. Three months later, in March 2025, BlackRock bought Preqin for £2.5 billion to lock in data dominance in private markets. Meanwhile, robo-advisors and hybrid advisory services—human plus algorithm—now manage $1 trillion in US assets as of 2026. Vanguard Personal Advisor alone crossed $300 billion. These are not boutique operators. They are platforms that can serve millions at once, lower their cost per client, and still make money.

The industry is consolidating at a historic pace. In 2025, there were 466 M&A deals in wealth management, up 27% from 2024. By 2029, the industry could see 1,500+ deals as scale becomes non-negotiable. Younger clients—42% of under-40 fintech users versus 11% at traditional firms—prefer robo-advisors and apps like Wealthfront and Betterment to phone calls with advisors. Gen Z and millennials now hold $3.1 trillion in equity holdings, a record as of August 2026, and they are shifting wealth toward stocks and digital platforms, not real estate or traditional advisors. The 148,000+ independent RIAs and financial planning firms that existed in 2022 are shrinking and consolidating. Those who survive will do so by adopting AI-driven prospecting, automation, and hybrid models—or by being absorbed into larger firms.

The evidence

  • Portfolio Management: Passive funds now hold 55% of US mutual fund and ETF assets by 2025, up from 34% a decade ago (2025).
  • Financial Planning: Advisors' fees fell from ~75 basis points to 66 basis points on a $10M portfolio between 2024 and 2026 (2026).
  • Robo-Advisory: Robo-advisors and hybrid advisory services manage $1 trillion in US assets, with Vanguard Personal Advisor at $300 billion (2026).
  • Wealth Management M&A: 466 M&A deals in wealth management in 2025, up 27% from 2024, with potential for 1,500+ deals by 2029 (2025).
  • Generational Shift: Gen Z and millennials hold $3.1 trillion in equity, a record, shifting wealth to digital platforms and away from traditional advisors (August 2026).

Who rides it

BlackRock and other consolidators with scale, data, and technology win. Robo-advisory platforms and fintech apps like Wealthfront and Betterment win by serving younger clients efficiently at lower cost. Any advisor or firm willing to adopt hybrid models—AI-driven prospecting, automation, and algorithm-assisted advice—survives. The winners are those who can process millions of clients with minimal human touch and still deliver results.

Who's squeezed

Independent financial advisors and boutique RIAs get squeezed. The 148,000+ independent firms and RIAs from 2022 are consolidating rapidly. A solo advisor or small team can no longer compete on price or technology. Advisors at traditional firms face pressure to adopt AI tools or risk being replaced. Regional wealth managers and mid-market players who can't reach scale disappear into acquisitions. Active fund managers who underperform passive benchmarks lose assets and shut down or merge. The fee compression means revenue per client drops every year—only scale and technology can offset that.

The main worry

The single biggest risk

The biggest risk is that advice itself becomes a loss leader. If passive funds are truly indistinguishable and robo-advisors can serve millions, then charging any advisory fee becomes harder. Firms may give up charging for advice and instead make money on execution, insurance, lending, and other ancillary services. That leaves truly independent advisors with nowhere to stand. Additionally, as older clients die and younger clients never use human advisors, the talent pipeline dries up—it becomes harder to recruit and retain advisors when the job is shrinking and the fees are declining. Regulatory changes, like the SEC's recent Regulation S-P compliance deadlines in June 2026, also raise operational costs for smaller firms that can't spread the burden across millions of clients.

In one sentence

Advice gets commoditized in a nutshell

Passive index funds and robo-advisors are commoditizing financial advice, crushing fees, and forcing the industry to consolidate around scale and technology—leaving independent advisors behind.
