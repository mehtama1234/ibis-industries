Food & Drink

Food and drink is contracting under multiple pressures: spirits are in sharp contraction (−2.2% total sales, bourbon −4.9% volume) after two decades of premiumization, hit by overcapacity, tariffs, and the 2026 bankruptcy of RNDC (second-largest distributor). Beer production dropped 5.7% in 2025; craft breweries fell to 9,344 in June 2026 with 481 closures; consumers are shifting to premium and non-alcoholic. Fast food is a mature $424B industry growing below 1% as consumer traffic deflates from GLP-1 adoption and retrenchment, while labor costs (California $20/hour minimum now standard) and tariff-driven food inflation relentlessly compress margins. Independent food trucks face saturation, rising costs, and fragmented local regulation. The only bright spots are convenience (spirits RTDs up 16.4%, smoothie bowls growing), premium positioning, and efficiency plays (automation, waste reduction). The industry is consolidating toward scale players with automation and brands; indies are commoditizing.

Breweries in the US

The US beer industry is contracting sharply—production down 5.7% in 2025, craft breweries down to 9,344 in June 2026, and 481 closures in 2025—while large producers consolidate, shift to premium and non-alcoholic products, and face tariff-driven cost inflation as consumers increasingly choose alternative beverages.

Tensions
  • Traditional indulgence categories face volume loss they cannot offset with pricing, while functional and premium alternatives capture margin and cultural prestige.
  • The line between medical, cosmetic, and wellness spending is disappearing, creating overlap that benefits integrated providers but destabilizes category silos.
  • Value leaders win on throughput and scale but face razor margins, while premium players command pricing power but serve smaller, more fickle audiences.
  • Mid-market retailers are caught between squeezed labor costs and shoppers who now treat their goods as interchangeable, forcing them to either transform into experiential destinations or exit the category altogether.
Signals
  • Protein, low-sugar, sober-curious, and health-tracking language becomes more central.
  • Operators lean harder on pricing architecture, pack-size changes, and cost takeout.
  • Mix shifts toward either obvious value packs or visibly better premium tiers.
  • Traffic, basket construction, and repeat behavior move toward low-friction channels.

Barbelled Consumer America

Premium Refuges Still Hold Price

Premium as self-permission rather than simple indulgence and quality signaling through brand, fit, and finish are no longer isolated category quirks. They are showing up across retail and food & drink, where the hollow middle and the margin vise are changing the operating baseline. That pushes the category toward a world where premium only works when the customer can articulate why the product is better. The pattern is already visible in example operators such as Ulta Beauty, Williams-Sonoma, and Lululemon.

Pressure points
  • Retail operators have to absorb this shift in a market where the US brick-and-mortar jewelry stores industry contracted sharply in 2025-2026 (12.1% and 2.2% respectively) as tariffs, high gold prices, and e-commerce competition accelerated store closures, even as lab-grown diamonds and wealthy consumers sustained pockets of resilience.
  • Food & drink operators have to absorb this shift in a market where in 2026, the U.S. wine industry remains a major economic force but faces weaker domestic demand, climate volatility, and a sharp Canada export shock.
