Advantaged setups
  • Businesses positioned on the right side of Physical Reindustrialization and Infrastructure.

Wellness Recodes Daily Life

GLP-1 Resets Appetite and Portion Economics

Smaller portion logic reaching mainstream households and traditional weight-loss services being rewritten by pharma are no longer isolated category quirks. They are showing up across consumer services, food & drink, and adjacent sectors, where the health reckoning and the margin vise are changing the operating baseline. That pushes the category toward a world where volume assumptions based on old appetite patterns are less reliable. The pattern is already visible in example operators such as Jenny Craig, McDonald's, and The Coca-Cola Company.

The Health ReckoningThe Margin Vise
Pressure points
  • Consumer services operators have to absorb this shift in a market where the US weight loss services industry has shifted from center-based coaching and branded diet plans toward hybrid behavioral, clinical and subscription models as GLP-1 medications pull demand away from legacy programs while expanding the addressable market for medically supervised weight management.
  • Food & drink operators have to absorb this shift in a market where a mature, fragmented $424B industry growing below 1% in 2026 as traffic deflates from consumer retrenchment and GLP-1 adoption, labor costs rise structurally (California $20/hour minimum now core cost floor), tariffs push food prices relentlessly higher, and chains make desperate bets on AI automation to replace payroll.
Signals and consequences
  • Protein, low-sugar, sober-curious, and health-tracking language becomes more central.
  • Operators lean harder on pricing architecture, pack-size changes, and cost takeout.
  • Volume assumptions based on old appetite patterns are less reliable.
  • Protein, satiety, and better-for-you positioning become more strategic.

Functional Consumption Beats Empty Indulgence

Energy and hydration as daily performance categories and supplement-like branding migrating into mainstream beverages are no longer isolated category quirks. They are showing up across manufacturing, food & drink, and adjacent sectors, where the health reckoning and the hollow middle are changing the operating baseline. That pushes the category toward a world where function can justify premium pricing if the use case is legible. The pattern is already visible in example operators such as The Coca-Cola Company, Haleon, and Abbott Laboratories.

The Health ReckoningThe Hollow Middle
Pressure points
  • Manufacturing operators have to absorb this shift in a market where the industry manufactures and markets caffeinated ready-to-drink beverages, shots and mixes, with 2025 demand supported by strong retail velocity, flavor innovation, expanded distribution and consumer substitution away from soda and some coffee occasions.
  • Food & drink operators have to absorb this shift in a market where a maturing $13.6 billion industry facing structural demand collapse—consumers no longer believe juice is healthy—while hit by tariffs, regulatory pressure on sugar, and Florida's decimated citrus harvest.
Signals and consequences
  • Protein, low-sugar, sober-curious, and health-tracking language becomes more central.
  • Mix shifts toward either obvious value packs or visibly better premium tiers.
  • Function can justify premium pricing if the use case is legible.
  • Packaging and merchandising need to tell a utility story quickly.
Aging, Care, and the Assistance Economy

Chronic Care Becomes an Industrial Service Chain

Chronic disease producing recurring procedure and device demand and outpatient settings capturing more volume are no longer isolated category quirks. They are showing up across healthcare and manufacturing, where the graying market and the pricing-power collapse are changing the operating baseline. That pushes the category toward a world where these are process businesses as much as clinical ones. The pattern is already visible in example operators such as Zimmer Biomet, Abbott Laboratories, and Labcorp.

The Graying MarketThe Pricing-Power Collapse
Pressure points
  • Healthcare operators have to absorb this shift in a market where the US dialysis centers industry treated roughly 516,837 patients on dialysis in 2025, generated $36 billion in revenue with a 3.5% annual growth rate, and faced mounting wage pressures and regulatory reimbursement limits despite demographic tailwinds from an aging, diabetic population.
  • Manufacturing operators have to absorb this shift in a market where the industry is growing slowly but steadily in 2025, supported by aging demographics, outpatient orthopedic procedures and robotics-enabled implants, while import competition, hospital price pressure and 2025-2026 tariff uncertainty keep margins tight.
Signals and consequences
  • Demand tilts toward chronic care, assistance, protection, and age-adapted convenience.
  • Margin performance diverges more from volume growth in payer-shaped sectors.
  • These are process businesses as much as clinical ones.
  • Scale in procurement, scheduling, and documentation becomes central.
Work Without the Old Firm

Automation Becomes Labor Arbitrage

Robots and software filling labor gaps before they chase full transformation and automation prioritized where turnover is chronic are no longer isolated category quirks. They are showing up across manufacturing, retail, and adjacent sectors, where the labor squeeze and the compute super-cycle are changing the operating baseline. That pushes the category toward a world where automation budgets should be framed against labor reliability, not only labor cost. The pattern is already visible in example operators such as Amazon, Sysco, and Microsoft.

The Labor SqueezeThe Compute Super-Cycle
Pressure points
  • Manufacturing operators have to absorb this shift in a market where a $387.7B (2026) mature industry led by four consolidated firms, racing to automate away chronic labor shortages while navigating tariff shocks and China's lockout-and-opening cycle on exports.
  • Retail operators have to absorb this shift in a market where uS vending machine operators remain a large unattended retail channel, with 2025 BLS sectoral output of $11.027 billion, but low concentration, fewer operators, healthier product demand and micro-market substitution keep the core category structurally pressured.
Signals and consequences
  • Automation, scheduling discipline, retention tactics, and selective simplification accelerate.
  • Automation budgets, inference demand, and power-ready site competition keep rising.
  • Automation budgets should be framed against labor reliability, not only labor cost.
  • Partial automation can create better payback than ambitious full-stack reinvention.

The Compute Super-CycleThe Labor Squeeze

Signals and consequences
