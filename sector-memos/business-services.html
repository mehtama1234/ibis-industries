Business Services

Operator angle: The best businesses convert complexity into recurring workflows instead of selling hours alone.

Investor angle: Prefer firms with embedded compliance, specialized expertise, or software-led delivery over generic people-heavy service models.

Business Services in 2025-2026 is being reorganized by Work Without the Old Firm, Space, Housing, and Local Friction, Regulated Software and the Admin State, with the economics increasingly determined by who can absorb complexity and sit on the right side of sector bottlenecks.

Advantaged setups
  • Businesses positioned on the right side of Work Without the Old Firm.
  • Operators with real system leverage: scale, workflow, procurement, or distribution control.
  • Formats with visible differentiation rather than generic middle positioning.
  • Embedded workflow and trust infrastructure with recurring necessity.
Exposed setups
  • Undifferentiated middle-market operators without pricing power.
  • Businesses carrying rising complexity without enough scale or workflow leverage.
  • Models exposed to secular behavior change but still priced as if old demand patterns hold.
  • Middlemen and service layers that can be compressed by software or platform control.
Diligence questions
  • Which of the dominant themes in Business Services are structural rather than cyclical?
  • Where does margin actually get lost: labor, procurement, compliance, capital, or channel access?
  • Does the operator own a bottleneck, or is it merely exposed to one?
  • What lets this operator keep relevance as automation, software, and consolidation spread through the sector?
