US agriculture is caught between structural collapse and margin divergence. The cattle herd hit a 75-year low, reshaping beef supply and creating wild price swings, while corn prices have crashed to $4.15/bushel after record 2025 production, leaving most grain farmers with $180 per-acre losses. Vertical integration by major processors is accelerating, squeezing wholesalers and independent operators. Operator angle: Treat volatility, biology, and trade exposure as core economics rather than background noise.

Investor angle: Prefer sectors with distribution leverage, controlled-environment advantages, or strong ties to processing and infrastructure rather than pure commodity exposure.

US agriculture is caught between structural collapse and margin divergence. The cattle herd hit a 75-year low, reshaping beef supply and creating wild price swings, while corn prices have crashed to $4.15/bushel after record 2025 production, leaving most grain farmers with $180 per-acre losses. Vertical integration by major processors is accelerating, squeezing wholesalers and independent operators. Beekeeping is imploding—55.6% annual colony losses from varroa viruses—yet pollination fees spike 15% because California almonds have no alternative. Meanwhile, tariffs add $200+ million annually to nursery and floriculture supply chains, collapsing traditional wholesale margins across flower, nursery, and produce distribution.

What to do
  • Vertical integrators and major processors (Tyson, JBS, Smithfield) capturing beef supply chain consolidation
  • Precision livestock and beekeeping automation tech ($7.94B market, 8.8% CAGR to $12.13B by 2030)
  • Direct-to-consumer models and alternative sales channels bypassing wholesale
What to underwrite
  • Is this producer price-locked or riding commodity spot prices, and what's the lock duration?
  • For wholesalers, how exposed are margins to processor vertical integration and direct sourcing?
  • What tariff scenarios are embedded in working capital and 2027 growth forecasts?
