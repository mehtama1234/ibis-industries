Healthcare

Operator angle: Demand is rarely the problem. Labor, reimbursement, and administrative execution are the real constraints.

Investor angle: Prefer enabling infrastructure, chronic-care systems, and reimbursement-fluent operators over labor-heavy exposure without pricing control.

Advantaged setups
  • Businesses positioned on the right side of Aging, Care, and the Assistance Economy.
  • Operators with real system leverage: scale, workflow, procurement, or distribution control.
  • Formats with visible differentiation rather than generic middle positioning.
  • Demand systems with strong retention, coordination, or habit loops.
Exposed setups
  • Undifferentiated middle-market operators without pricing power.
  • Businesses carrying rising complexity without enough scale or workflow leverage.
  • Models exposed to secular behavior change but still priced as if old demand patterns hold.
  • Asset- or labor-heavy operators exposed to input volatility without pass-through rights.
Diligence questions
  • Which of the dominant themes in Healthcare are structural rather than cyclical?
  • Where does margin actually get lost: labor, procurement, compliance, capital, or channel access?
  • Does the operator own a bottleneck, or is it merely exposed to one?
  • How much of the economics are governed by reimbursement, regulation, fraud control, or trust infrastructure?

Adult Day Care

The industry is a fragmented, Medicaid-heavy community-care market benefiting from population aging and the shift away from nursing homes, but margins are constrained by staffing costs, state reimbursement rates and tighter HCBS oversight.

Allergists

Allergists are consolidating from solo/small group practices into multispecialty networks, facing Medicare reimbursement cuts, at-home testing competition, and margin pressure while the overall allergy care market grows 8-9% annually.
