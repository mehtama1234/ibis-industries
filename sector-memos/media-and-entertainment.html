Operator angle: Attention is abundant, but monetizable scarcity is not.

Tensions
  • Physical retail venues are becoming economic moats for operators who can program them as discovery environments and brand theaters, while purely transactional retail continues to erode.
  • Experience-driven spending concentrates wealth and access—not all communities or demographics have equal access to well-programmed venues or travel infrastructure, potentially widening lived inequality.
  • Consolidated players can absorb compliance costs and rate risk that squeeze independents; but consolidation also means fewer competitors, tighter coordination, and systemic concentration risk if anchor players fail.
  • Asset owners (landlords, infrastructure owners, platform operators) keep compounding returns while workers and operators inside the system compete for thinner margins.

Experience, Status, and Community

Fandom and Affiliation Become Spend Infrastructure

Shared affiliation deepening willingness to spend and communities monetized across merchandise, events, and media are no longer isolated category quirks. They are showing up across retail and media & entertainment, where the channel shift is changing the operating baseline. That pushes the category toward a world where affiliation businesses need ongoing ritual and refresh, not just a one-time sale. The pattern is already visible in example operators such as DraftKings, GameStop, and Mattel.

The Channel Shift
Pressure points
  • Retail operators have to absorb this shift in a market where the US sporting goods retail industry is consolidating rapidly (led by Dick's $2.5 billion Foot Locker acquisition), but is constrained by weakening consumer discretionary spending, tariff cost headwinds, and structural pressure from e-commerce and brand direct-to-consumer strategies.
  • Media & entertainment operators have to absorb this shift in a market where the U.S. video games industry generated about $60.8 billion in consumer spending in 2025, up 1.4% from 2024, with 2026 spending forecast near $62.8 billion as Nintendo Switch 2 momentum, subscriptions and major releases offset weaker legacy console cycles.
Signals and consequences
  • Volume and loyalty metrics show clear preference for mobile apps, social commerce, and same-day delivery.
  • Company mix is already separating, with 2 advantaged examples and 0 exposed examples inside this subtheme set.
  • Affiliation businesses need ongoing ritual and refresh, not just a one-time sale.
  • Identity-rich categories can withstand generic commoditization longer.
Scale, Financialization, and the Owned Economy

Asset Owners Keep Gaining Relative Power

Ground ownership and lease control separating from operations and rights catalogs and infrastructure assets treated as yield-bearing instruments are no longer isolated category quirks. They are showing up across real estate, media & entertainment, and adjacent sectors, where the great consolidation and the real-estate reckoning are changing the operating baseline. That pushes the category toward a world where owning the constraint often beats operating on top of it. The pattern is already visible in example operators such as Prologis, CBRE Group, and Google.

The Great ConsolidationThe Real-Estate Reckoning
Pressure points
  • Real estate operators have to absorb this shift in a market where a steady, consolidating landlord business serving price-constrained renters, now accelerating because housing is unaffordable and supply is stuck.
  • Real estate operators have to absorb this shift in a market where the US commercial real estate sector is experiencing a marked but uneven recovery in 2025-2026, driven by industrial and multifamily strength while office properties face ongoing distress and adaptation pressures.
Signals and consequences
  • Survivors cluster in niches too small to roll up; generalists get gobbled into platforms.
  • Real estate decisions increasingly hinge on proximity to electricity, broadband, and supply chain nodes.
  • Owning the constraint often beats operating on top of it.
  • Investors should distinguish asset control from operating revenue noise.
