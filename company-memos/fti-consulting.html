Advantaged
What's happening

The US consulting industry is growing but hollowing: AI is replacing junior work, federal spending is collapsing, and clients are demanding outcomes. Firms are buying narrower slices of expertise, creating demand for fractional leadership and specialized expert networks. Restructuring demand remains tied to economic cycles and M&A activity. Litigation consulting and valuation expertise are tied to corporate activity, regulatory change, and tax matters.

The investor question

FTI's competitive position is anchored by specialized expertise (litigation, valuation, restructuring) and brand in specific sectors. The question is whether FTI can maintain premium pricing for specialist work as larger consulting firms acquire similar capabilities.

The operator playbook

Management must defend specialist positioning and cross-sell capabilities across practices while resisting commoditization pressure. Talent concentration and client concentration are binding; growth is limited by specialist bench capacity.

The core tension

If FTI looks like a specialist practice being acquired or displaced by larger consulting firms, margins compress and the independent specialist model breaks. M&A activity and economic cycles create volatility.

Bottom line: FTI survives by being the leading specialist in litigation, valuation, and restructuring; if larger firms duplicate this capability, the specialist premium erodes.
