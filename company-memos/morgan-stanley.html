Morgan Stanley

Advantaged
What's happening

M&A hit $4.9 trillion in 2025, lifting investment banking revenues over $100 billion. Morgan Stanley sits in the capital markets flow—equity underwriting, commodity trading, and merchant banking are all strong. But margin compression looms: AI compliance costs are rising, employment rationalization is incomplete, and fintech and electronic trading are eating trade commissions. Private banking is doing well (client portfolios grew), but consumer banking and traditional retail insurance are hollowing out.

The investor question

The bet is whether Morgan Stanley can grow underwriting and advisory fees fast enough to offset margin pressure from automation and regulatory costs. Deal pipelines are strengthening for 2026, but the durability of deal volumes and the fee pools available to winners remain uncertain.

The operator playbook

Lean into high-touch advisory, merchant banking, and capital-intensive businesses where scale and credit discipline separate winners. Rationalize consumer-banking footprint and automate compliance workflows. Defend private banking economics and affluent-client relationships.

The core tension

Capital markets tailwinds are real, but AI and electronic trading are permanently shrinking trading margins. If deal activity normalizes or rates fall sharply, Morgan Stanley's investment-banking revenue could evaporate faster than cost cuts can follow.

Bottom line: A fee-income beneficiary of deal flow that must race automation to defend profitability.
