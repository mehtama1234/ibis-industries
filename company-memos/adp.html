ADP

Contested Middle
What's happening

HR consulting is a $39.4 billion US market growing just 1.4% in 2026, under pressure from AI software disruption and consolidation. Payroll and benefits administration are consolidating around cloud platforms as employers demand integrated HCM stacks rather than loose point solutions. The shift from manual to AI-assisted employee workflows is accelerating, but that same AI is eroding ADP's traditional consulting-labor moat.

The investor question

The question is whether ADP can translate payroll ownership into a thicker, stickier software platform before AI commoditizes expertise and compliance becomes a solved feature. If it does, subscription and platform economics compound. If not, it stays trapped in a slow-growth advisory business where talent costs and competition erode margins relentlessly.

The operator playbook

The operator must treat talent concentration as the binding constraint and invest relentlessly in automation that makes fewer humans more productive. Anything that requires humans to scale doesn't scale.

The core tension

If ADP becomes a commodity outsourcer, it competes on labor cost and loses. If it becomes pure software, it faces the full fury of AI-native competitors and margin-compressing fintech platforms that bundle payroll with cash management and accounting.

Bottom line: ADP's survival depends on making itself so deep and integrated that swapping it out is organizational trauma.
