What's happening

The US business information reseller industry is a mature, consolidating reseller business aggregating market data and research for subscriptions, now competing with free AI and tighter privacy rules. Company research services are a mature, slow-growing $2.1 billion sector under pressure from AI-native competitors and regulatory compliance costs, with growth in private equity data. Financial data services are a $23.4 billion industry consolidating at the top while alternative data and AI erode the moat.

The investor question

Experian's bet is that it can bundle credit scoring, compliance data, and alternative signals into a thicker platform than pure AI data aggregation. The risk is that privacy regulation (like GDPR and US state laws) fragments data access and that AI-native competitors move faster on alternative signals.

The operator playbook

The operator must treat data freshness and regulatory compliance as equally binding and invest to stay ahead of both. Every data acquisition and model release must assume that privacy regulations tighten, not relax.

The core tension

Experian becomes a commodity data reseller if AI can aggregate equivalent signals from free sources and if privacy regulation narrows the moat. At that point, it competes on update frequency and compliance track record against lighter competitors.

Bottom line: Experian survives only if bundling and regulatory expertise stay ahead of AI commoditization—which requires constant reinvestment and capital discipline.
