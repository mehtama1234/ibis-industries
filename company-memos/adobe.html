Technology & Digital company memo

Adobe

Advantaged
What's happening

Adobe controls design tools (Photoshop, Illustrator), marketing platforms, and publishing software used by millions. The business model is subscription and cloud delivery—high margins, switching costs. But AI agents (like Figma, Canva, emerging generative tools) are commoditizing design and creative workflows. Customers are subscription-fatigued and looking for cheaper alternatives. The $29.5B design software market is fracturing.

The investor question

Adobe's fortress is its installed base and workflow integration; its vulnerability is that AI is lowering the skill barrier to design and reducing willingness-to-pay. The question is whether Adobe can retool as an AI-powered platform faster than specialized competitors can gain share.

The operator playbook

Adobe must rapidly integrate AI into core products to improve productivity and justify subscription price, while simultaneously defending against lighter-weight, cheaper alternatives. The company also needs to explore pricing elasticity—lower price tiers may capture volume before it leaves entirely.

The core tension

If generative AI and lighter-weight tools (Figma, Canva) continue to capture market share, Adobe's pricing power erodes and subscriber churn accelerates. If subscription fatigue persists, even loyal customers migrate.

Bottom line: A software incumbent watching its moat—professional-grade tools with high switching costs—get leveled by artificial intelligence and cheaper competitors.
