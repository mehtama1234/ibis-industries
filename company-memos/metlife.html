MetLife

Advantaged
What's happening

Life insurance and annuities are a mature, well-capitalized industry: annuity sales hit record levels in 2025 as retirees demand guaranteed income, but life insurance enrollment and pricing power are eroding. Dental insurance is in structural decline as dentist networks collapse and plan maximums erode. Disability claims are accelerating. MetLife must underwrite more risk while reinsurance rates climb.

The investor question

MetLife's bet is that it can manage claims costs and reinsurance better than smaller competitors—that scale and data are worth something. But if claims inflation persists (aging populations, chronic disease, labor shortages), scale cannot offset math.

The operator playbook

Exit low-margin lines (dental) and consolidate or divest; focus capital on annuities where margin is still viable; use actuarial data and AI to price disability risk more accurately than competitors.

The core tension

MetLife is caught between high claims costs and constrained pricing power. It can't shrink fast enough to match margin compression and can't grow fast enough to offset it.

Bottom line: MetLife survives in annuities and large-group benefits but must accept that life and disability insurance will generate lower returns than historical experience suggests.
