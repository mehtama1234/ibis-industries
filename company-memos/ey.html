EY

Advantaged
What's happening

EY serves large corporations with audit, tax, consulting, and advisory services. The firm benefits from regulatory compliance demand, complexity in tax and transfer pricing, and capital-markets activity. But AI is automating routine audit and tax work, federal consulting spending (part of revenue) is under pressure, and large clients are building internal capabilities instead of buying expensive consulting hours. The industry is consolidating and consolidating margins are declining.

The investor question

EY's upside is specialized advisory (valuation, forensics, healthcare transformation) and outcomes-based consulting. The downside is commoditization of routine services and client insourcing. The firm must prove it can transition faster than competitors.

The operator playbook

EY should accelerate investment in specialized practices and outcomes-based engagements while managing decline in routine audit and tax work. The firm must also invest in technology and AI tools to improve productivity and justify premium pricing.

The core tension

If large clients successfully insource expertise and reduce consulting spend, EY's high-margin advisory business faces commoditization. If federal spending collapses, a significant revenue stream evaporates.

Bottom line: A diversified services firm trying to graduate from commoditized hours to specialized expertise before clients do it themselves.
