General Mills

Exposed
What's happening

General Mills is facing a structural demand shift: GLP-1 adoption is resetting appetite and portion economics, tariffs are hitting cereal production and baking mix hard, and avian flu spiked egg costs to $6.23 per dozen in March 2025. Cereal is a mature, declining $12.2 billion industry with near-zero growth as health-conscious consumers abandon sugared products. Input cost volatility, tariffs on imported grains, and private-label competition are compressing margins across the entire portfolio.

The investor question

Can General Mills defend margin as GLP-1 shrinks appetites, tariffs raise input costs, and private-label copies undercut price? The answer rests on whether portfolio mix, reformulation, and efficiency can offset structural demand decline and retail power.

The operator playbook

General Mills must reformulate products for health-conscious consumers faster than competitors, cut input costs through efficiency and scale, and defend margin through portfolio mix. Its binding constraint is consumer preference—demand for sugar-heavy cereals is structurally declining, and it cannot force people to eat what they don't want.

The core tension

General Mills is selling indulgence in a world shifting toward health; tariffs are raising input costs and private-label copies are undercutting price. If General Mills cannot reformulate fast enough or defend margin, it could be dragged toward commodity processor economics—losing to both premium health brands and tariff-battered value players.

Bottom line: General Mills is selling packaged indulgence to a market shifting toward health, and tariffs are eroding the one lever it still has: margin through efficiency.
