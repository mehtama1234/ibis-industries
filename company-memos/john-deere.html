John Deere

Contested Middle
What's happening

U.S. agriculture is in crisis: historic drought, record labor costs, and tariff dislocations have collapsed margins for most crops while paradoxically tightening beef supplies. Farm consolidation is accelerating as mid-size family farms are crushed by record debt and commodity volatility, leaving only well-capitalized corporations able to invest in precision agriculture and automation. Tariff costs on imports are forcing domestic tractor and equipment makers to choose between raising prices (losing volume) or absorbing margin compression.

The investor question

Deere wins if large-scale farmers continue to invest in automation and precision agriculture despite margin pressure. The risk is that tariff-driven price increases push smaller farmers toward used equipment and aftermarket channels, collapsing new unit demand.

The operator playbook

Deere must shift from volume-driven tractor sales toward higher-margin autonomous systems, data analytics, and financing. It needs to acquire or develop AI-native autonomous equipment before competitors do, while defending aftermarket revenue as used equipment becomes more attractive.

The core tension

Tariffs are spiking input costs for Deere, but it cannot pass through full price increases without killing unit demand. Simultaneously, shifting to automation and data services requires capital and talent that are in short supply. If tariffs normalize before Deere completes this transition, it loses price advantage and margin expansion opportunity.

Bottom line: Deere transforms from equipment maker to autonomous-systems and data-services company, or gets squeezed by consolidation and tariff volatility.
