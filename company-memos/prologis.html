Prologis

Contested Middle
What's happening

E-commerce fulfillment drive insatiable demand for modern logistics facilities; Prologis has benefited from tenant consolidation and rising rents. However, retail productivity improvements, nearshoring logistics networks, and normalization of e-commerce growth are raising vacancy risk. Remote work persistence is reducing office demand and pressuring mixed-use portfolios.

The investor question

Prologis's thesis is that last-mile supply will remain tight and rental growth will continue. The real risk: if e-commerce normalizes or AI optimizes logistics networks, vacancy rises and rents stagnate.

The operator playbook

Maintain focus on last-mile logistics assets in dense markets; divest or reposition mixed-use or office assets; raise rents aggressively where demand remains strong; build flexibility into lease structures to manage turnover.

The core tension

Prologis' portfolio is optimized for peak e-commerce growth, not stable-state logistics. If growth normalizes, the company has too much square footage and too many assets in high-cost markets.

Bottom line: Prologis remains a cash generator if e-commerce growth stabilizes, but faces capital redeployment challenges if growth slows materially.
