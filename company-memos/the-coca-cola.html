The Coca-Cola Company

Contested Middle
What's happening

Bottled water remains America's top packaged beverage by volume, but growth is decelerating as GLP-1 drugs suppress appetites and health-conscious consumers shift toward functional beverages. Coca-Cola's core carbonated soft drinks face secular pressure; the company has diversified into RTD coffee, tea, and wellness variants, but each niche faces intense competition and input cost volatility. Tariffs and import-driven cost inflation are raising input costs for syrup, packaging, and ingredients faster than pricing power allows.

The investor question

The question is whether Coca-Cola can grow premiumized and functional-beverage volume fast enough to offset carbonated-drink decline while protecting margin against tariff-driven cost inflation. Portfolio breadth helps, but no single category is growing fast enough to offset the base erosion.

The operator playbook

Accelerate innovation in premium, functional, and health-conscious beverage formats—GLP-1-friendly lower-sugar options, adaptogenic drinks, sober-social beverages. Cut low-margin SKUs ruthlessly. Lock in long-term ingredient contracts to hedge tariff volatility. Defend premium brand pricing and channel distribution.

The core tension

GLP-1 adoption is structural and permanent—it is remaking appetite and consumption economics in real time. If functional-beverage growth cannot offset carbonated-drink decline and tariff input costs cannot be passed through, Coca-Cola faces margin and volume compression simultaneously.

Bottom line: A beverage giant defending volume in mature categories while chasing growth in functional niches amid tariff cost pressure.
