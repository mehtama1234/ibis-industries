Verizon

Advantaged
What's happening

Verizon is no longer a voice carrier—that market is mature and margins are thin. Instead, it's competing for enterprise workflows: connectivity, identity, security, compliance. The compute super-cycle (datacenters, AI) creates demand for network capacity and edge services. Regulatory compliance and security keep customers on Verizon's platforms.

The investor question

Verizon's edge is carrier-grade infrastructure and regulatory relationships. The bet is whether it can leverage those into higher-margin software and services before pure-software vendors commoditize connectivity.

The operator playbook

Verizon must accelerate its shift away from consumer wireline (it's dying) and double down on enterprise platforms. The company owns the network layer; the question is whether it can build software layers on top faster than competitors.

The core tension

If pure-play cloud vendors (AWS, Azure) capture the majority of enterprise workflows and lock in data, Verizon becomes a dumb pipe. If 5G/wireless adoption slows, capital-intensive network upgrades don't drive revenue.

Bottom line: An infrastructure incumbent trying to build software leverage before platform vendors make pipes irrelevant.
