What's happening

Petroleum refining is smaller in footprint but running hotter in 2025-2026, with tighter capacity and stronger utilization offset by persistent volatility in feedstock and product pricing. Petrochemical manufacturing is recovering modestly, supported by cheap ethane economics and strong export growth, but remains highly exposed to feedstock cost swings and policy uncertainty. Motor oil demand is forecast to remain steady through the decade, anchored by older vehicle fleets and fleet growth.

The investor question

Can Shell's refining scale and chemical integration insulate it from crude-price volatility and policy uncertainty around fossil-fuel investment? Profitability turns on whether production utilization and margin management can sustain through commodity cycles.

The operator playbook

Maximize refining utilization and operate disciplined capital allocation in petrochemicals; transition downstream toward specialty chemicals and biofuels where policy risk is lower. Lock in feedstock supply relationships to smooth margin volatility.

The core tension

Import-reliant refiners and subscale chemical producers will face consolidation pressure if oil prices collapse or if climate policy accelerates fossil-fuel exit. Stranded assets in legacy refining capacity will mount if EV adoption and energy transition accelerate faster than expected.

Bottom line: Shell wins if refining scale and chemical integration absorb commodity volatility; subscale competitors face acquisition or exit.
