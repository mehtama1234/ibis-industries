Advantaged
What's happening

Grid modernization and data-center investment are driving an $81.4 billion US electrical equipment market growing at 7.8% in 2026. ABB sits in the middle of reshoring and tariff-induced supply rewiring, where domestic specification and long lead times favor incumbents. Power conversion equipment reached $24.1 billion in 2025, shifting demand toward data-center cooling and renewable storage infrastructure.

The investor question

The bet is whether ABB can sustain pricing power as tariffs, supply-chain mandates, and data-center clustering accelerate consolidation among installed-base players. If it can hold specification and fend off commoditization, capex discipline wins. If imports sneak back and customers learn to substitute, margins compress faster than efficiency gains can offset.

The operator playbook

The operator must treat material costs and procurement cycles as non-negotiable constraints, not negotiation variables. Every expansion decision must assume tariffs persist and that scale in electrical-equipment manufacturing is the only durable margin defense.

The core tension

ABB risks becoming an import-reliant assembler if tariff protection erodes and global producers regain margin. If it does, it competes purely on cost and loses the pricing power that tariffs and domestic mandates currently grant.

Bottom line: ABB is riding tariffs and reshoring tailwinds; the real test is what happens when the tariffs recede.
