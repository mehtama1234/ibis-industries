ZF Friedrichshafen

Contested Middle
What's happening

ZF Friedrichshafen operates in an automotive supply market that is fracturing: tariffs and electrification are hollowing out traditional steering, suspension, and conventional engine components, leaving only global tier-ones with scale to survive. Simultaneously, gasoline engine manufacturing is not dying but evolving into a three-fuel ecosystem (gasoline, hybrid-electric, mild-hybrid 48V), creating near-term complexity and capital demands. Auto parts remanufacturing and circular-economy models are gaining share as vehicle age increases and electrification slows new-vehicle sales.

The investor question

The bet is whether ZF can maintain procurement leverage and specification advantages as the automotive supply base consolidates around global players. If it can't—if EV platforms fragment and tariffs force all suppliers to regionalize—scale advantages erode.

The operator playbook

The operator must invest aggressively in hybrid and electrification capabilities while divesting or rightsizing traditional powertrains. Supply-chain nearshoring is urgent; tariff-aware manufacturing footprint redesign is survival.

The core tension

ZF has global scale and automotive relationships, but tariffs are forcing supply-chain regionalization that fragments its advantages. If it can't move fast enough on EV platforms and hybrid powertrains, and if electrification accelerates faster than hybrid demand justifies capex, it gets stranded with obsolete plants and products.

Bottom line: ZF must execute electrification faster than tariffs and complexity hollow out the profitability of transitional platforms.
