Keurig Dr Pepper

Exposed
What's happening

Consumers no longer believe juice is healthy, collapsing structural demand in a once-$13.6 billion category even as tariffs and Florida's decimated citrus harvest squeeze input costs further. Demand is shifting away from pure indulgence toward health-aligned, lower-calorie, and functional options, pressuring classic soda and juice volumes. Retailers have consolidated power and resist price increases, forcing beverage makers to defend profitability through cost efficiency and mix, not volume.

The investor question

Can Keurig leverage relaxation drinks, RTD tea, and functional beverages to offset the structural collapse of juice and maintain pricing power in an oversupplied category? Mix and innovation are the only levers available.

The operator playbook

Shift volume away from juice and commodity soda into health-aligned and functional formats; lock in favorable retailer terms on new innovation. Manage procurement risk by diversifying suppliers and securing long-term input contracts before tariff uncertainty worsens.

The core tension

Single-product brands and commodity processors have no escape route as demand collapses; margin compression will force them out entirely. If tariff prices rise faster than retail will accept, inventory turns can trap cash in unprofitable mix.

Bottom line: Keurig survives if portfolio diversity and retailer relationships let it shift mix faster than demand collapses; commodity-focused competitors face existential squeeze.
