eBay

Advantaged
What's happening

E-commerce in the US remains consolidated, with Amazon setting punishing standards for rivals while tariffs and shrinking margins force smaller sellers to automate or exit. Online auctions and collectibles remain a small but resilient niche at $2.0 billion, supported by collector behavior and specialist platforms. Simultaneously, compliance workflows around fraud, privacy, and trust are thickening, forcing platforms to invest in infrastructure that only scale players can afford.

The investor question

eBay's competitive position hinges on whether it can differentiate through specialization (antiques, collectibles, used goods) rather than compete on breadth with Amazon. If regulatory and fulfillment costs rise faster than pricing power, the platform advantage erodes.

The operator playbook

The operator must choose between scaling fulfillment (expensive, Amazon-like) or leaning into merchant empowerment and specialty categories (higher margin, lower volume). Channel access and pricing power are both constrained; fulfillment intensity is the capital question.

The core tension

If eBay looks like it's losing to Amazon on price, it becomes a mid-market retailer with no clear reason to win. Collectibles and used goods are defensible, but represent a smaller addressable market than general e-commerce.

Bottom line: eBay survives by being the better platform for what Amazon won't do well: specialty categories where merchant empowerment and community matter more than speed and price.
