What's happening

US flexible packaging is a $42.9 billion mature market growing from packaging redesign for recyclability. Cardboard box capacity is down 10% in 2025 with price hikes of $100+ per ton failing to offset weak demand. Plastic bottle growth is modest and scale-driven, with manufacturers investing in lighter and recycled-content designs to offset collapsing single-serve beverage demand. Pharma packaging is the one bright spot—serialization and drug supply chain complexity are pulling volume upward.

The investor question

Amcor is betting that scale in flexible packaging lets it navigate input cost volatility, tariffs, and recycling compliance without losing margin. The risk is that weak underlying demand for packaged goods, combined with regulatory pressure on plastics, commoditizes most of the business.

The operator playbook

The operator must ruthlessly rationalize capacity and accept that utilization will stay low. Every packaging design decision must assume recycling becomes mandatory and that substitution by lighter and simpler formats accelerates.

The core tension

Amcor starts to resemble a subscale producer when pharma (its margin refuge) flattens and general packaging demand keeps falling. At that point, it competes only on cost, and costs are getting worse.

Bottom line: Amcor is a defensive business riding pharma compliance demand, but the runway is shorter than most investors assume.
