What's happening

The pharmacy benefit management model remains demand-positive as prescription spending is projected at $518.7 billion in 2025, but margins are under pressure from rebate-disclosure rules and fiduciary-audit requirements. HMO providers face steep profitability declines despite Medicare Advantage growth. Compounding pharmacies are growing 3.3% in 2026, but automation pressure and consolidation reshape the landscape.

The investor question

CVS's bet is that owning both retail pharmacy and PBM creates a defensible integrated model. The risk is that cost transparency and regulatory tightening eliminate arbitrage opportunities and force CVS into the middle.

The operator playbook

CVS must optimize retail pharmacy productivity through automation and clinical services, invest in PBM data and pricing analytics, and defend independent pharmacy relationships while consolidating redundant capacity.

The core tension

CVS's vertical integration was designed to capture margin from retail and PBM asymmetries. As regulators tighten, those asymmetries evaporate. CVS could end up the most transparent—and least profitable—operator.

Bottom line: CVS is betting transparency and scale compensate for lost arbitrage.
