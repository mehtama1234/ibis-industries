What's happening

The retail audio equipment category is maturing, with online share and premium integrated experiences lifting sales, but consumer electronics substitution and high comparison shopping capping margin upside. The retail headphone market is showing slower growth than hype cycles imply, with channel and feature shifts becoming the primary gain levers. US audio and video equipment manufacturing output was $4.393 billion in 2025, up only 0.3% from 2024, while Census shipments fell 4.6% in 2025 as tariffs, offshore sourcing, and softer hardware replacement cycles pressured volumes. Tariff costs are structural and permanent.

The investor question

Bose's premium positioning is only defensible if experience and brand loyalty can command price premiums that offset tariff costs. If comparison shopping and e-commerce commoditize audio, margin collapse is inevitable.

The operator playbook

Management must invest in experience-led retail (not just online commodity sales) while managing tariff costs through offshore sourcing. Channel access is the key constraint; pricing power depends on brand positioning, not scale.

The core tension

If Bose looks like it's losing to commodity audio (Apple, cheap headphones), it becomes a mid-market audio retailer with no pricing power. Tariff costs make low-cost positioning impossible.

Bottom line: Bose survives by being the premium experience brand, not the cheapest option; tariff-driven cost pressure makes this positioning harder to defend.
