Adidas

Contested Middle
What's happening

Adidas sits in the contested middle of athletic apparel. Tariffs have driven import costs up 34% for athletic goods, and consumers are increasingly split: budget-conscious shoppers buy from discount channels or direct-to-brand, while high-end consumers shift to luxury. Adidas's wholesale channels (Dick's, Foot Locker) are consolidating, giving retailers more leverage. E-commerce is capturing a quarter of all fashion sales, flattening retail margins.

The investor question

Adidas's challenge is cost management: tariffs are squeezing margins faster than pricing can compensate. The investment question is whether the brand can defend its position by shifting mix toward higher-margin products (running, lifestyle) and higher-end channels.

The operator playbook

Adidas must aggressively manage tariff exposure through supply-chain diversification and sourcing mix. The company also needs to accelerate direct-to-consumer and digital channels to avoid wholesale consolidation leverage.

The core tension

If tariff-driven prices push mid-market consumers toward discount brands or direct competitors, Adidas loses volume and gets trapped on the cost side. If luxury consumption slows, the upmarket opportunity vanishes.

Bottom line: A brand caught in the tariff squeeze, fighting for shelf space and pricing power with neither the cost structure nor brand power of tier-one players.
