Retail company memo

PetSmart

Contested Middle
What's happening

Pet ownership remains high (95 million households), but sales channels have shifted decisively to e-commerce and subscription delivery; grooming and boarding remain venue-bound and are growing, but retail shelf space is shrinking. Value pressure is increasing as budget-conscious owners shift to DIY and lower-cost alternatives. Premium fresh pet food is growing but is increasingly available DTC.

The investor question

PetSmart's retail footprint is an anchor dragging it down, not a moat protecting it. The company must prove it can compete on convenience and service, not convenience and selection.

The operator playbook

Shrink retail footprint to high-traffic, grooming-anchored locations; build grooming and boarding as high-margin service businesses; e-commerce is table-stakes, not a driver of value. Use retail presence as a pickup hub for online orders, not a destination.

The core tension

PetSmart owns thousands of expensive leases optimized for pet-product browsing—a customer behavior that has moved online. Converting locations to service centers requires capital and cannibalization.

Bottom line: PetSmart survives if it can make its retail presence a service hub for grooming and boarding; fails if it remains a retail destination for commodity pet food.
