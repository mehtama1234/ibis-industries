Baker Hughes

Advantaged
What's happening

Baker Hughes supplies drilling equipment, completions technology, and production systems to oil and gas operators. Energy transition is real, but oil demand is not disappearing—it's evolving. Middle East production is growing, deepwater projects require specialized equipment, and AI-driven efficiency gains increase oil recovery per well. Simultaneously, renewable energy and grid modernization create demand for Baker Hughes' gas-turbine and power-generation equipment.

The investor question

Baker Hughes' thesis is that oil and gas capital intensity will migrate to difficult-to-extract reserves (deepwater, hostile environments) and that traditional energy demand stays high enough to justify continued investment. Renewable energy exposure provides diversification.

The operator playbook

Baker Hughes should lean into deepwater and offshore expertise while rapidly scaling renewable-energy and efficiency equipment sales. The company must also invest in automation and digital services to improve operator economics and deepen customer relationships.

The core tension

If energy transition accelerates and oil demand peaks sooner than expected, capital spending on oil projects collapses and traditional revenue streams dry up. If renewable capex stays moderate, diversification doesn't compensate.

Bottom line: An energy incumbent doubling down on fossil fuels while hedging with renewables exposure, betting the transition is slower than the rhetoric.
