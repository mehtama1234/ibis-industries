International Paper

Advantaged
What's happening

U.S. paper-mill demand is shifting decisively toward packaging and tissue while printing and communication paper demand continues to compress. Capacity cutbacks of 10% in 2025 have spiked cardboard box prices by $100+ per ton, and tariffs have exposed weak underlying demand growth as consolidation accelerates. Coated paper makers are caught between shrinking print demand and rising input costs, offset only by steady food packaging growth.

The investor question

International Paper wins if it can rationalize unprofitable capacity faster than competitors and shift mix toward packaging formats with stronger sustainability credentials. The risk is that tariff-driven reshoring floods the market with cheap imports once tariffs ease.

The operator playbook

The operator must accelerate mill closures in low-return segments, consolidate production, and shift capital toward food-packaging and specialty-coated grades. Tariff uncertainty makes capital allocation risky, so the focus must be on improving utilization and reducing complexity in existing mills.

The core tension

Tariffs are currently propping up pricing by cutting off import supply, but this is temporary. Once tariffs normalize or supply recovers, prices will revert, and all the capacity reductions happening today will look insufficient. Conversely, cutting capacity too aggressively leaves money on the table if packaging demand accelerates.

Bottom line: International Paper survives by consolidating capacity and shifting entirely toward packaging and specialty grades.
