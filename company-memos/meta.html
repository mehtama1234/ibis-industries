What's happening

AI is reshaping data processing and hosting from a growth-stage, outsourcing-driven market into a power-constrained, hyperscaler-dominated infrastructure arms race where talent and electrical capacity are more scarce than capital. U.S. social networking site economics remain anchored by $117.7 billion in social media ad revenue, up 32% in 2025. Digital advertising agencies are losing commodity work to platform automation and targeting capability to privacy law, forcing a painful transition toward strategic services.

The investor question

Meta wins if it can sustain monopoly power in social ad targeting while absorbing massive compute capital expenditure for AI infrastructure. The question is whether regulatory pressure (antitrust, privacy) fragments its audience and targeting advantage before AI monetization compounds.

The operator playbook

Meta must build out power-efficient compute infrastructure and rationalize data-center costs while defending ad targeting moats against privacy regulation. It also needs to develop AI applications (recommendation, creative generation, commerce) that drive higher ad spend and CPMs.

The core tension

Meta is investing tens of billions in AI compute infrastructure while facing regulatory threats to its ad-targeting moats and privacy regulations that limit data access. If ad targeting power erodes or regulation fragments its audience, massive compute investments become stranded assets.

Bottom line: Meta survives only if AI-driven ad efficiency and commerce integration offset regulatory pressure on targeting and user data access.
