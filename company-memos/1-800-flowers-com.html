Retail company memo

1-800-FLOWERS.COM

Contested Middle
What's happening

The US florists industry is shrinking at $7.9 billion with 1-3 florist shops closing daily as tariff-driven costs, supermarket competition, and e-commerce disruption hollow the market. Flower and nursery stock wholesaling is a $22.8 billion market, but its traditional middleman role is collapsing as importers and growers sell directly to retailers, while new tariffs on Ecuador and Colombia have added over $200 million in annual supply-chain costs. Online flower shops remain structurally supported by e-commerce and same-day delivery, but 2025-2026 performance is mixed as weaker discretionary demand and higher fulfillment costs press margins.

The investor question

The investment case is underwater: tariff costs are structural and permanent, discretionary gifting demand is secular decline, and fulfillment costs are rising. Price increases are not an option in a shrinking market.

The operator playbook

Management must cut costs to match the smaller addressable market, which means painful store closures and rationalizing fulfillment. Pricing power is gone; survival is about operational efficiency on a smaller base.

The core tension

If 1-800-FLOWERS looks like it's losing, it becomes a single-channel merchant with mid-market scale and no pricing power, which is the worst position in retail. Tariff costs make this outcome increasingly likely.

Bottom line: A once-dominant brand hollowed by tariffs and discretionary pullback; survival requires accepting a much smaller, more profitable core.
