Goldman Sachs

Advantaged
What's happening

Investment banking and capital-markets activity surged in 2025-2026 on robust M&A and capital-raise volumes. Securities brokering remains concentration-heavy with lower per-trade commissions and shifting toward advisory-style and platform ecosystems. Private equity asset management is consolidating at the top, with mega-funds raising larger pools and competing on deployed-capital returns more than fundraising. Wealth management is fragmenting as solo practitioners leave for scaled advisors or independent teams.

The investor question

Goldman's bet is that it can stay relevant as capital-markets advisor to large corporates and PE sponsors while building recurrence through asset management and advisory. The risk is that if deal volumes flatten or if AI-driven transaction automation accelerates, scale becomes less defensible and margins compress.

The operator playbook

The operator must treat client relationship depth and capital-deployment capability as linked and invest to own both. Every hiring decision and client pitch must move the needle on lock-in and competitive defensibility.

The core tension

Goldman becomes a commodity transaction facilitator if it can't deepen advisory relationships into durable capital-partnership models. At that point, it competes on price and execution against much leaner fintech platforms and decentralized networks.

Bottom line: Goldman survives only if scale and advisory depth compound into lock-in before automation and fragmentation accelerate further.
