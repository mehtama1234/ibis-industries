What's happening

US commercial real estate is experiencing a marked but uneven recovery in 2025-2026, driven by industrial and multifamily strength while office properties face ongoing distress. The commercial leasing market is splitting: thriving segments like prime office, industrial, and AI datacenters are strong, while secondary office and traditional retail face structural decline. Property management and asset management are in selective expansion phases where recurring services and integrated platforms improve durability.

The investor question

Cushman's bet is that it can deepen service layers beyond transaction brokerage—advisory, capital deployment, facilities operations—so that tenants and landlords find it cheaper to stay than to switch. The risk is that if real-estate cycles turn sharply, transactions collapse faster than recurring services can offset the decline.

The operator playbook

The operator must treat utilization and relationship depth as linked: low utilization markets reward service breadth, while high-utilization markets default back to commodity brokerage. Every market entry must assume that the transaction cycle will turn, and build recurring revenue before it does.

The core tension

Cushman becomes a transaction-only intermediary if it can't deepen relationships into recurring services. At that point, it competes on brokerage price and geography against much leaner specialists.

Bottom line: Cushman survives by being too integrated to abandon, not just too convenient to switch.
