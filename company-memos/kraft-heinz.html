Kraft Heinz

Exposed
What's happening

The US baking mix industry contracted 0.3% in 2026 after years of growth, squeezed by 16% tariffs on imports and 39% cumulative wage inflation, with avian flu spikes pushing egg costs to $6.23 per dozen in March 2025. Simultaneously, demand is shifting away from indulgence categories toward health-aligned, lower-calorie, and functional consumption patterns. GLP-1 adoption is resetting consumer appetite and portion economics for legacy processed-food brands.

The investor question

Kraft Heinz survives on scale—using its procurement leverage and distribution muscle to defend shelf space and retailer relationships in everyday staples. The question is whether legacy brands can migrate toward health-aligned formulations fast enough before newer entrants own the premium shelf.

The operator playbook

Kraft Heinz must ruthlessly rationalizing the portfolio—cutting low-velocity SKUs and investing in reformulated versions of core brands that align with health and wellness trends. It also needs to absorb tariff costs through efficiency rather than price, or risk losing volume to private label and value brands.

The core tension

Tariffs and wage inflation are compressing margins, but Kraft Heinz cannot raise prices on commodity products without losing volume to private label and imports. Health reformulation requires R&D and manufacturing line changes that slow cash flow, yet staying with legacy formulations guarantees market share erosion.

Bottom line: Kraft Heinz wins on efficiency and scale, or becomes a legacy supplier to private label and discount retailers.
