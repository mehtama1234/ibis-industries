PepsiCo

Contested Middle
What's happening

Bottled water remains America's #1 packaged beverage by volume even as single-use plastic regulations force the industry toward recycled-content packaging. Meanwhile, GLP-1 adoption is resetting portion economics and appetite patterns, while consumers increasingly split between bargain-hunters and premium buyers—hollowing out the middle.

The investor question

The question is whether PepsiCo's scale across water, RTD coffee, functional beverages, and snacks lets it straddle premium and value simultaneously. If the middle truly collapses, portfolio breadth becomes the hedge.

The operator playbook

Management must defend shelf access while reformulating toward wellness without diluting margins. Private-label incursion forces continuous innovation in premium segments while competitive pressure in RTD and tea keeps pricing tight.

The core tension

PepsiCo's strength is its scale; its vulnerability is that scale depends on mass-market volume. If enough consumers genuinely abandon the middle, even portfolio diversity cannot solve the math.

Bottom line: PepsiCo wins if it can own both the premium and value ends; it stumbles if stuck in a hollowed middle.
