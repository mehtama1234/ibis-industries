Commercial Metals Company

Contested Middle
What's happening

US steel bounced from tariffs in 2025-2026 but faces structural headwinds: global oversupply, flat demand, and permanent shift to lighter vehicles. US rebar manufacturing is protected by tariff walls and fueled by infrastructure spending and data-center demand, running at near-full capacity in 2026 but facing acute labor shortages constraining growth. Fabricated structural metal manufacturing is experiencing a data-center-driven boom overshadowed by tariff-driven cost pressure and labor shortages forcing automation.

The investor question

Commercial Metals' bet is that it can stay ahead of global overcapacity by owning domestic tariff protection and capturing infrastructure/data-center demand. The risk is that if tariffs relent or if infrastructure spending flattens, it faces margin compression from global producers.

The operator playbook

The operator must treat labor availability as the binding constraint and invest aggressively in automation, even at the expense of near-term margin. Every production decision must assume that skilled labor costs only go up.

The core tension

Commercial Metals becomes an import-reliant assembler if tariffs fall and global producers regain margin. At that point, it competes on cost and loses the installed-base advantage that tariffs and domestic mandates currently grant.

Bottom line: Commercial Metals rides tariff protection and data-center demand; without both, margin compression is inevitable.
