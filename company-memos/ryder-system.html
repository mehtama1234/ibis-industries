Advantaged
What's happening

Commercial trucking demand remains stable but is being disrupted by electrification (EV adoption is rising but charging infrastructure and used-EV depreciation are unpredictable), nearshoring (reducing long-haul demand), and carrier overcapacity. Ryder's leasing business is vulnerable to EV adoption timing; logistics and supply-chain services face intense competition from tech-enabled startups and Amazon-owned solutions.

The investor question

Ryder is caught between structural demand stability and technological disruption. The company's value is in the fleet and customers; the risk is that both become commoditized if EV and autonomous tech neutralize Ryder's logistics expertise.

The operator playbook

Aggressively transition fleet to EV; secure long-term contracts to lock in utilization; diversify into supply-chain software and visibility tools to reduce dependence on truck and driver scarcity.

The core tension

Ryder's installed fleet is both asset and liability: it's valuable if adoption is gradual but obsolete if EV transition accelerates and depreciation is steep.

Bottom line: Ryder survives if EV adoption remains gradual and supply-chain complexity keeps logistics valuable; struggles if electrification accelerates or supply chains simplify.
