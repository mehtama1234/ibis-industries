### Dentists in the US (Healthcare)
- Market size: $196.1 billion (2026) | growth: 1.9% in 2026, 1.2% CAGR (2021-2026) | margin: 15-16% estimated (down from 15.3% in 2022 due to cost pressures) | businesses: 180,000 dental practices in the US | employees: 1.2 million (estimated from 2022 baseline of 1.197M and modest growth)
- 2022 baseline: $162.1 billion (2022)
- Takeaway: The US dental market, worth $196 billion in 2026, is consolidating rapidly as reimbursement stagnation and workforce shortages push independent practitioners into DSO networks while technology and aging populations provide modest growth.
- Current dynamics: Consolidation is the defining force reshaping the industry. DSO affiliation nearly doubled from 16.1% of dentists in 2024 to 39% of practices by 2026—accelerated by new graduate debt (average $297,800 in 2025), stagnant reimbursement rates, and rising operational costs. Staffing is becoming a structural bottleneck: 62% of dentists cite hiring as their top concern, dental hygienist shortages persist nationwide, and McKinsey projects 36,000 missing professionals by 2031. Reimbursement is deteriorating; 60% report rates have stagnated since 2021, 25% say they've fallen, only 7% got increases—compressing margins despite higher patient demand from an aging population (one in five Americans will be 65+ by 2030). Technology is becoming a make-or-break differentiator: AI-powered imaging, teledentistry (30% of consultations now virtual, up from near-zero in 2020), and automation are now table-stakes to compete for staff and handle capacity constraints.

- Market size: $33.2 billion in 2026 | growth: 3.0% in 2026; 5.1% CAGR over 2021-2026 | margin: 6.7% (2022 baseline); under pressure from low Medicaid reimbursement and wage inflation | businesses: Approximately 8,800+ establishments (as of 2022); fragmented, predominantly small regional operators | employees: ~225,000 employees (2022 baseline); workforce growing slower than demand
- 2022 baseline: $20.8 billion in 2022
- Takeaway: The $33 billion US residential mental health and substance abuse treatment sector is caught between surging disorder prevalence and an acute 327,000-person clinical workforce shortfall, with private equity consolidation accelerating amid regulatory chaos and public funding cliffs that are compressing already-thin margins.
- Current dynamics: The industry is being pulled in three directions at once. Demand is booming—mood and anxiety disorders alone drive 34.4% of the residential market; eating disorders affecting young adults are surging; and while opioid overdose deaths dropped from 110,000 (2023) to 75,000 (2025), illicit fentanyl still drives 75% of all OD deaths and keeps demand for treatment high. At the same time, the workforce crisis is acute: HRSA projects a shortfall of 327,000 behavioral health professionals by 2038, with critical gaps in licensed counselors and psychiatric nurses. This has pushed wages up but keeps margins compressed since Medicaid (45.6% of revenue) reimburses below cost in many cases. Regulatory chaos—SAMHSA terminated $2 billion in grants in late January 2026, then reversed the decision within hours—has shaken provider confidence. - Current dynamics: DaVita and Fresenius are locked in a 2-3% volume growth race, with both guiding for modest revenue growth but expanding operating margins through cost control and mix shift.
