### Discount Department Stores (Retail)
- Market size: $107.2 billion (2026) | growth: -0.8% (2025), +0.1% projected (2026) | margin: 6.9% (2026) | businesses: 47 major operators (2026) | employees: ~500,000+
- 2022 baseline: $98.4 billion (2021 IBISWorld)
- Takeaway: The $107.2 billion US discount department stores industry is stabilizing after years of decline, driven by inflation-fueled consumer trade-down behavior and off-price players aggressively expanding, though tariffs and wage pressures are squeezing margins.
- Current dynamics: Inflation eroding purchasing power (PCE up 4.1% year-over-year as of May 2026) is driving a clear bifurcation in consumer behavior. Affluent households absorb higher prices and keep spending on full-price retail; lower and middle-income households are trading down to discount stores at five times faster rates in 2026. Off-price retail (TJX, Ross) is winning market share from mid-tier department stores, which are caught in the middle. Tariffs in 2025 are squeezing retailer margins (down to estimated 3.6% from 6.1% in 2024 if absorbed), forcing price increases and raising questions about pass-through to consumers. Wage pressures from 21 state minimum wage hikes and warehouse labor costs rising 7.4% year-over-year (2024) are further compressing profitability. E-commerce still erodes foot traffic, but the "treasure hunt" experience—curated off-price inventory changing weekly—remains defensible against online shopping, explaining why discount stores outperform traditional department stores.
- Recent: May 2025: Rite Aid filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy a second time, illustrating sector consolidation pressures. | June 2025: At Home filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy, closing ~100 stores in home goods space. | 2025: Trump's reciprocal tariff policy imposed 10% baseline tariff plus 34% on China, 20% on EU, pressuring import-heavy discount retailers. | Q3 2025: Ross Stores reported comparable store sales up 17% and announced ~110 new store openings for 2026.
