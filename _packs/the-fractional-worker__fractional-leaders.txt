### Executive Search Recruiters (Business Services)
- Market size: $10.2 billion in 2026 | growth: Declined 3.3% CAGR from 2021-2026; modest recovery expected 2026-2031 | margin: 10-15% typical for profitable firms; 4.5% average loss for unprofitable firms; 40% of firms saw revenue growth in 2024-2025 despite headwinds | businesses: 5,293 firms with no single player above 5% share | employees: Approximately 40,000-42,000 (as of 2026)
- 2022 baseline: $8.8 billion in 2022
- Takeaway: The US executive search recruiters market, valued at $10.2 billion in 2026, contracted 3.3% annually from 2021-2026 due to rising interest rates and weak hiring for senior roles.
- Current dynamics: The industry is caught between two opposing forces. Clients demand shortlists in under one week, yet executive search fundamentally requires time for rigorous vetting and board-level due diligence. This gap favors large firms deploying AI-augmented workflows and global networks but squeezes smaller players. CEO succession hit an 8-year high in 2025 at 12.5% of S&P 500 seats, versus 9.8% in 2024, and external CEO hires nearly doubled from 18% to 33% of successions, creating tailwind for the industry. However, fractional and interim executives are mushrooming (120,000+ by 2024, up from 60,000 in 2022), representing a structural shift away from permanent placement. Internal HR departments and LinkedIn have eroded the firms' monopoly on executive intel, forcing specialization. Competition from Recruitment Process Outsourcing providers now extends into director and selective C-suite roles at lower margins, squeezing traditional players from below. Firms diversifying into coaching (35% of top performers) and advisory consulting (30%) report better survival odds.
