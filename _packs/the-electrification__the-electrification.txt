### Automobile Engine & Parts Manufacturing in the US (Manufacturing)
- Market size: $43.8 billion in 2026 | growth: 2.5% in 2026, 1.5% CAGR 2021–2026 | margin: 8.1% (2022) | businesses: 774 establishments, 633 enterprises (2022) | employees: Approximately 62,000–63,000 workers (2022 baseline: 62,106; 2027 projection: 63,419)
- 2022 baseline: $37.3 billion
- Takeaway: Gasoline engine manufacturing is no longer dying; it is evolving into a three-fuel ecosystem (gasoline, hybrid-electric, mild-hybrid 48V) while tariffs and supplier bankruptcies force consolidation and nearshoring.
- Current dynamics: The sector faces a paradox: electric vehicles are growing (hybrids up 10+ percent year-on-year), yet gasoline engine demand has *not* collapsed. Instead, OEMs are stretching ICE platform life by investing in turbocharging, variable compression, and 48-volt mild-hybrid architecture. Tariff regimes—US 25% on auto parts effective April 2025, Mexico 25–50% tariffs on non-FTA parts starting January 2026—are crimping margins at suppliers and forcing nearshoring decisions. CAFE standards have been reset in June 2025 to exclude EV credits from compliance calculations, giving OEMs more breathing room to sell gasoline vehicles without penalty. Bankruptcies and restructurings among smaller suppliers (First Brands, Accuride, Tayco) signal consolidation pressure. Denso, Bosch, and Continental are doubling down on EV electronics and thermal systems, while traditional engine makers are repositioning as powertrain integrators—producing not just gasoline blocks but hybrid modules and control systems.
