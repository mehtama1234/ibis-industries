### Dollar & Variety Stores in the US (Retail)
- Market size: $123.5 billion (2026) | growth: 1.4% annually 2021-2026 | margin: 4.7-5% (2026) | businesses: 9,400 companies operating ~39,000 stores | employees: Approximately 600,000+ workers
- 2022 baseline: $101.3 billion (2022)
- Takeaway: Dollar stores remain a resilient middle-tier retail format attracting consumers across income levels in an inflation-stricken economy, but growth is slowing as tariffs, organized theft, and Walmart competition compress margins and saturate the market.
- Current dynamics: The industry is locked between two opposing forces. On the demand side, persistent inflation and wage stagnation have made dollar stores the default destination for low-income households and an attractive "trade-down" venue for middle-income shoppers—a behavioral shift that proved structural in 2024-2025. On the supply side, tariffs (especially on Asian imports), organized retail crime (ORC), and labor-cost inflation are eating margins. Simultaneously, Walmart and Target have weaponized their size to undercut dollar stores on price while offering fresher produce and prepared foods, pulling away both price-sensitive and aspirational shoppers. Dollar stores are responding by remodeling stores, expanding fresh produce (DG Fresh now in ~7,000 locations, 2025), and ramping up omnichannel (e-commerce partnerships, retail media). Yet consumer sentiment collapsed to 48.2 in May 2026 (a record low), signaling that shoppers' financial strain is worsening rather than improving. The industry is stable but no longer growing—it's optimizing for a stagnant or slowly declining consumer base.
- Recent: May 2026: Consumer sentiment index hit record low of 48.2; Dollar General CEO reports customers say their financial situation has worsened. | May 2026: 87% of Dollar Tree stores are within five miles of a Walmart; tariffs and inflation eroding value perception. | Q1 2025: Dollar General reports 5.2% sales growth and +61 basis point gross margin expansion from $150 million labor and store remodeling investment. | 2025-2026: DG Fresh produce rollout continues to ~7,000 stores total (slow pace relative to 2023 momentum); 450 new DG stores and 4,250 remodels planned for 2026.

### Chain Restaurants in the US (Food & Drink)
- Market size: $230.6 billion (2026) | growth: -1.0% in 2026; +1.7% in 2025; +2.2% CAGR 2021-2026 | margin: Low single-digit margins under pressure; 42% of operators unprofitable (2025) | businesses: 142,206 chain restaurant establishments (2025) | employees: 15.8 million total restaurant industry workers in 2026; chain restaurants represent substantial portion; 100,000 new jobs planned for 2026
- 2022 baseline: $50.9 billion (2022)
- Takeaway: Chain restaurants are caught between structural wage inflation, tariff pressures, and a consumer base choosing value-driven casual dining over premium sit-down experiences.
- Current dynamics: Chain restaurants are caught in a structural cost-margin trap. Labor costs have jumped 39% since pre-pandemic levels, with minimum wages now $16-$20 per hour in major states (California FAST Act $20 for national chains; Washington $16.66; Florida heading to $15 by September 2026). Food inflation sits 34% above pre-pandemic, and tariffs have forced 62% of operators to raise menu prices. Consumer spending at restaurants is growing only 1.3% in real terms, while 60% of operators report deteriorated business conditions in 2025 and 42% are unprofitable. Fast-casual's weakness has paradoxically strengthened casual dining: Chili's and Texas Roadhouse thrive by offering $12 sit-down deals that undercut $15 limited-service entrees. However, consumers are also defecting to grocery and convenience stores, takeout apps, and at-home experiences, shrinking traffic across all formats.
