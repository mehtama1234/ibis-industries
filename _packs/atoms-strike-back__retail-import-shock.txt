### Women's Clothing Stores in the US (Retail)
- Market size: $66.4 billion (2026) | growth: -0.3% annually (2021-2026) | margin: 2.3-5% net (2024-2025) | businesses: 88,112 (2026) | employees: 177,748 in online sales; total brick-and-mortar employment declining (2025)
- 2022 baseline: $40.4 billion
- Takeaway: The US women's clothing retail sector is bifurcating into thriving luxury-and-specialty online and physical retail while mass-market brick-and-mortar chains face accelerating closures, squeezed by Shein and Temu from below and tariff-driven price pressure from all sides.
- Current dynamics: The industry is bifurcating sharply. Premium and specialty retailers—focused on affluent women, niche segments like plus-size and athletic wear—continue to grow, while mass-market and discount chains face accelerating closures. The tariff exemption's end in August 2025 has triggered broad price increases across imported goods, with estimated costs of $136 per family. E-commerce now accounts for nearly half of women's clothing sales (online $61.8B in 2025 vs brick-and-mortar $66.4B in 2026), but ultra-low-cost online platforms like Shein and Temu are eating share from traditional retailers. Store closures hit 7,323 in 2024 and roughly 6,000 in the first half of 2025 alone. Retailers are responding by expanding omnichannel offerings, developing localized warehousing (Temu and Shein), and focusing on higher-margin segments. Employment in online women's clothing grew 5.5% annually 2019-2024, while brick-and-mortar employment continues declining.
- Recent: August 29, 2025: De-minimis tariff exemption ends globally; all imports under $800 now subject to duty, forcing price increases on imported apparel | March 2025: Forever 21 files Chapter 11 bankruptcy; plans to liquidate all 354 U.S. stores | February 2, 2025: Liberated Brands (Volcom, Billabong, Quiksilver, Roxy, RVCA, Spyder) files Chapter 11; liquidates 122 stores | January 2025: Macy's announces closure of 66 stores (part of 150-store exit over three years)

### Furniture Stores in the US (Retail)
- Market size: USD 195.7 billion (2025) | growth: 3.07% CAGR 2026–2034 | margin: 3% (2022); compressed by tariffs and price competition | businesses: 38,627 establishments (2022) | employees: 238,195 (2022; current data unavailable)
- 2022 baseline: USD 75.7 billion (IBISWorld, furniture stores only)
- Takeaway: The US furniture retail market is contracting in physical showroom footprint as online channels (28% of sales, growing 5–10% annually) and tariff pressures squeeze traditional operators, while housing-market weakness and consumer retrenchment trigger waves of bankruptcies and strategic repositioning toward DTC, sustainability, and omnichannel logistics.
- Current dynamics: The furniture sector is caught between opposing forces. Housing demand—the core engine—weakened in early 2026 after a brief 2025 rebound; new home sales dropped 17.6% month-over-month in January 2026 and 11.3% year-on-year, directly suppressing furniture demand. Tariffs have become a structural drag: 25% duties on upholstered furniture, cabinets, and vanities (effective October 2024) forced retailers to either absorb costs, eroding already-thin 3% margins, or pass increases to consumers. Online channels now claim 28% of sales and grow 5–10% annually, while Wayfair alone holds ~14% of the 2025 US furniture market share, signaling a permanent shift to e-commerce and DTC models. Inflation-weary consumers are trading down to lower-priced goods, accelerating failures among independent and mid-market retailers. At the same time, sustainable and modular furniture is emerging as a premium niche (76% of consumers willing to pay 9.7% more for eco-friendly goods), and ergonomic/gaming-focused seating is gaining traction among remote workers.
- Recent: American Signature Inc. filed Chapter 11 bankruptcy November 2025, closing Value City Furniture stores | Furniture tariffs delayed: 25% rate on upholstered goods and cabinets held for one year (through late 2026) instead of jumping to 30–50%; however, new Section 301 tariffs (10–12.5%) on Vietnam and 60+ countries took effect July 24, 2026 | Walker Edison Furniture and affiliates filed Chapter 11 August 2025, sold to Twin-Star International for $20 million | Online furniture channels reached 28% of total market share in 2025; DTC brands projected to hit $51 billion US revenue by 2026
