Labor supply is the biggest structural constraint—the trade needs roughly 20,700 workers per year just to replace retirements and attrition, yet total employment is only inching up 2–3% per decade. ### Carpenters in the US (Construction)
- Market size: $63.5 billion (2026) | growth: 0.3% decline in 2026; 2.5% CAGR 2021-2026 | margin: 3-4% (squeezed by wage inflation and material tariffs) | businesses: 195,000 establishments; down 0.9% CAGR 2021-2026 | employees: 923,000 carpenters employed across US (2026)
- 2022 baseline: $40.8 billion (2022)
- Takeaway: The $63.5B US carpentry industry is shrinking in headcount and establishment count while facing simultaneous wage inflation, tariff-driven material cost spikes, and housing demand weakness that has made residential work thin.
- Current dynamics: The carpentry industry is experiencing a rare divergence: demand weakness in residential housing combined with a stubborn labor supply crisis. Housing demand has shifted from supply-constrained to demand-constrained in 2026, with only 11.2% of households relocating in 2024 and existing homeowners locked into mortgages below 4%. Yet 65% of builders still cannot fill carpenter positions, indicating structural skills shortage rather than cyclical downturn. Tariffs have become a major margin killer: lumber tariffs (10% October 2025) stack on 35% prior Canadian duties, while steel hit 50% tariffs in June 2025 and copper exceeded 22% year-over-year price increases. Material costs are up 2.8% in 2025 (nonresidential 3.2%), eroding the 3-4% profit margins already compressed by rising wages. Modular prefabrication is reshaping the work: components move off-site while on-site carpentry shifts to finishing, custom details, and site-specific challenges. The number of carpentry establishments declined 0.9% annually 2021-2026, meaning small operators are exiting faster than new ones enter.

### Concrete Contractors in the US (Construction)
- Market size: $110.1 billion (2026) | growth: 4.2% CAGR 2021-2026; 0.1% in 2026 alone | margin: 6.9-7.1% (compressed from historical levels) | businesses: 90,964 establishments (2026) | employees: 285,000+ workers (2026)
- 2022 baseline: $73.3 billion (2022)
- Takeaway: Federal infrastructure spending and data center buildout have driven growth to $110 billion in 2026, but tariffs, material costs, and labor shortages are now compressing already-thin margins by 3-5 points.
- Current dynamics: Concrete contractors face a paradox: infrastructure demand is solid from the Bipartisan Infrastructure Law, data centers, and semiconductor fab sites, but residential work is weakening as affordability crises and rising interest rates depress housing starts (1.18 million units in May 2026, down 15.4% from the prior month). Ready-mix concrete costs have climbed to $179-$195 per yard in 2025-2026, with Portland cement up 3-7% year-over-year. Tariffs have become a major headwind: steel and aluminum tariffs doubled from 25% to 50% in June 2025, raising rebar prices 5-10%; a 25% tariff on cement from Canada and Mexico also took effect in 2025. Labor shortages remain acute (94% of firms report open craft positions), forcing contractors to adopt technology like telematics and automation. Margins have compressed 3-5 percentage points in the past 18 months, eroding profitability. Consolidation is accelerating as large players acquire regional specialists to build scale and pass through costs, but the fragmented market structure (no dominant firm) keeps competition fierce.
