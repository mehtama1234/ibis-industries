- Current dynamics: Medical loss ratios (MLRs) are the core crisis: 17% of MA contracts ran above 100% MLR in 2023, and another third sit in the 90–95% range with no margin cushion. Prescription drugs, especially GLP-1s like Ozempic, are driving surprise costs. - 2022 baseline: $43.1 billion (2022)
- Takeaway: The $46.5 billion US ambulatory surgery center market is consolidating rapidly as hospitals and private equity acquire independents, while reimbursement growth lags labor cost inflation and new procedure opportunities fail to offset margin pressures.
- Current dynamics: The industry is polarizing into scaled platforms and boutique specialists. Hospitals and PE firms are aggressively acquiring, with Ascension's $3.9 billion AmSurg acquisition (2024) and Cleveland Clinic's Regent Surgical partnership exemplifying a shift toward health-system integration over pure physician ownership. Reimbursement growth of 2.4-2.9% annually is failing to match wage inflation and operational cost increases, compressing margins for smaller independents while large platforms leverage scale to absorb pressures. Anesthesia shortages are acute, with locum tenens use up 17% since 2024 and projected to climb 5% more in 2026; nursing shortages are equally severe as hospitals compete for staff. CMS dramatically expanded procedure coverage in 2026, adding 560 new procedures to the ASC Covered Procedures List (including cardiac ablations and lumbar fusion), but most of these high-acuity procedures carry thinner margins at ASCs than at hospital outpatient departments due to site-of-service payment differentials.
