### Hiking & Outdoor Equipment Stores (Retail)
- Market size: 6.3 billion dollars (2026) | growth: -2.6% decline in 2025; negative annual rate through early 2026 | margin: 3.5-4.3% (2025-2026) | businesses: 1,382 establishments (2025) | employees: 20,834 people (2025)
- 2022 baseline: 7.1 billion dollars (2022)
- Takeaway: The US hiking and outdoor equipment retail industry is contracting sharply after pandemic peaks, with consolidation accelerating as REI and Dick's Sporting Goods fight Amazon and big-box retailers for share among consumers spending cautiously on discretionary goods.
- Current dynamics: Post-pandemic normalization is hitting hard. During 2020-2022, lockdowns and remote work drove demand for camping gear, hiking boots, and outdoor apparel, but as travel resumed and indoor activities reopened, sales stalled. Retailers faced massive inventory write-downs and margin compression. Simultaneously, Amazon is moving upmarket into premium outdoor gear with private-label tents and sleeping bags, while Walmart dominates on affordability through its national footprint. Dick's Sporting Goods is launching elevated outdoor retail concepts to compete directly with REI. Outdoor participation remains strong at 183 million Americans in 2025—but consumers are browsing online, comparing prices on Amazon, and buying less frequently. REI's January 2025 layoffs (180 full-time and 248 part-time positions) and October 2024 retail cuts signal that even the strongest incumbent cannot sustain pandemic-era unit economics. Consolidation is accelerating: smaller independent shops continue to close, and chains are pruning unprofitable store footprints while betting on e-commerce.
- Recent: January 2025: REI laid off 180 full-time and 248 part-time employees and shuttered its unprofitable Experiences division (tours and classes), serving only 40,000 customers out of 23 million co-op members. | October 2024: REI cut 257 retail jobs as it restructured physical store footprint. | January 2024: REI laid off 357 employees (2.2% of workforce) amid worsening outdoor retail conditions and prior-year losses of 311 million dollars (2023) and 164.7 million dollars (2022). | 2024-2025: Dick's Sporting Goods launched elevated outdoor retail concepts and House of Sport/Field House formats to compete with REI; Dick's Public Lands outdoor brand collapsed from eight stores to three.
