### Dentists in the US (Healthcare)
- Market size: $196.1 billion (2026) | growth: 1.9% in 2026, 1.2% CAGR (2021-2026) | margin: 15-16% estimated (down from 15.3% in 2022 due to cost pressures) | businesses: 180,000 dental practices in the US | employees: 1.2 million (estimated from 2022 baseline of 1.197M and modest growth)
- 2022 baseline: $162.1 billion (2022)
- Takeaway: The US dental market, worth $196 billion in 2026, is consolidating rapidly as reimbursement stagnation and workforce shortages push independent practitioners into DSO networks while technology and aging populations provide modest growth.
- Current dynamics: Consolidation is the defining force reshaping the industry. DSO affiliation nearly doubled from 16.1% of dentists in 2024 to 39% of practices by 2026—accelerated by new graduate debt (average $297,800 in 2025), stagnant reimbursement rates, and rising operational costs. Staffing is becoming a structural bottleneck: 62% of dentists cite hiring as their top concern, dental hygienist shortages persist nationwide, and McKinsey projects 36,000 missing professionals by 2031. Reimbursement is deteriorating; 60% report rates have stagnated since 2021, 25% say they've fallen, only 7% got increases—compressing margins despite higher patient demand from an aging population (one in five Americans will be 65+ by 2030). Technology is becoming a make-or-break differentiator: AI-powered imaging, teledentistry (30% of consultations now virtual, up from near-zero in 2020), and automation are now table-stakes to compete for staff and handle capacity constraints.

- 2022 baseline: $5.967 billion franchise revenue (2021 baseline from IBISWorld); projected $9.0 billion by 2026
- Takeaway: The US residential senior care franchises industry is in a demographic sweet spot—73 million baby boomers entering retirement with guaranteed occupancy demand—but labor shortages are clamping profit margins even as private equity floods in to roll up fragmentation.
- Current dynamics: The industry is being remade by three forces. First, the supply-demand crunch is severe: assisted living beds are growing just 1-2% annually while the over-80 population grows at 4%, forcing residual demand into franchised home and community-based care where operators enjoy wider margins. Second, staffing is the binding constraint; registered nurses, CNAs, and dining staff report 40% turnover; over half of home care agencies report ongoing shortages, pushing wages up 31% cumulatively since 2019 and forcing operators to choose between losing margins or rationing service. Third, consolidation is accelerating as private equity firms see demographic tailwinds as a safe bet: 2025 saw 871 M&A transactions (20.8% above 2024's record), with assisted living making up 44% of deals; on an annualized basis, 2026 is surpassing both prior years. Baby boomers' entry into 80+ cohort starting in 2027 will intensify all three pressures simultaneously.
