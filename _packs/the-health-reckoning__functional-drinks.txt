### Energy Drink Production (Food & Drink)
- Market size: $23.9 billion (2025) | growth: 9.7% CAGR 2020-2025 | margin: 10.3-11.3% (estimated 2025) | businesses: 251 establishments (2025) | employees: 38,954 (2025)
- 2022 baseline: $11.7 billion
- Takeaway: The US energy drink industry has doubled in size since 2022 to $23.9 billion (2025) as sugar-free positioning becomes mandatory and acquisition wars intensify to capture female and health-conscious consumers previously outside the category.
- Current dynamics: The industry is in the midst of a dramatic category shift. Sugar-free drinks surpassed full-sugar varieties in dollar sales for the first time, driven by 70% of consumers seeking to limit sugar intake. Red Bull launched Red Bull Zero in November 2024. Consolidation is accelerating: Celsius paid $1.8 billion for Alani Nu (female-focused Gen Z brand) in April 2025, then secured Rockstar through a distribution deal with PepsiCo in September 2025, signaling acquisition wars to capture younger women and health-conscious consumers. Employment grew 10.3% annually 2020-2025 to nearly 39,000 workers, while the number of businesses climbed 10.6% year-over-year to 251, showing continued new entrants. Convenience stores remain dominant at 65.5% of sales but losing share to warehouse clubs (19%) and supermarkets (10.9%) as bulk-buying and price-sensitive consumers trade impulse purchases for economy.

### Juice Production in the US (Food & Drink)
- Market size: $13.6 billion (2026) | growth: 0.5% in 2026; 1.3% CAGR 2021–2026 | margin: ~11% EBIT margin (2022 baseline; current likely lower) | businesses: 318 establishments; 242 enterprises | employees: ~23,500 (2026, est.)
- 2022 baseline: $13.0 billion (2022)
- Takeaway: A maturing $13.6 billion industry facing structural demand collapse—consumers no longer believe juice is healthy—while hit by tariffs, regulatory pressure on sugar, and Florida's decimated citrus harvest.
- Current dynamics: Sugar-phobia is the core dynamic. From 2019 to 2024, Americans cut juice consumption by 20 percent, viewing it as just another sugary drink despite being 100 percent fruit. The 2025–2030 federal Dietary Guidelines now caution on 100 percent juice, and in August 2025 the FDA proposed lowering the minimum sugar standard for orange juice from 10.5 percent to 10 percent Brix—the first standard cut since 1963, driven by both health pressure and Florida's crop failures. Cold-pressed and premium segments are the only growth story, expanding at 8 percent annually (2025–2030) as brands like Naked Juice and newer players capture health-conscious buyers willing to pay more for minimal processing and no added sugar. Yet the mass market is collapsing: 100 percent orange juice fell from 67 percent beverage share in 2022 to 62 percent in 2024, while cheaper orange-flavored drinks steal share.
