### Coal Mining in the US (Energy & Environment)
- Market size: $23.2 billion in 2026 | growth: 1.5% CAGR 2021-2026; +4.3% in 2026 (driven by geopolitical price spike) | margin: Not disclosed in recent reports; historically 2-3% (severely pressured 2023-2025) | businesses: 712 companies in 2026 (4.6% CAGR decline 2021-2026) | employees: 34,900 as of 2026 (down from ~38,000 in 2025; 50% decline since 2011)
- 2022 baseline: $18.3 billion in 2021 (last year in IBISWorld 2022 report)
- Takeaway: US coal mining is a declining commodity commodity business where the core fuel (thermal coal for power) is being replaced by cheaper natural gas and renewables, while narrow-margin exports of metallurgical coal to Asia keep the largest producers afloat on geopolitical volatility and tariff games.
- Current dynamics: Thermal coal (used for power generation) is in secular decline—it fell to 19.8% of US electricity mix in 2020 and continues shrinking as utilities retire plants faster than new coal capacity is added. In 2025 alone, only 2.6 GW of coal capacity was retired (the lowest in 15 years), but that was because the Trump administration forced delays; 6.4 GW are planned for retirement in 2026. Metallurgical coal (for steelmaking) is the only bright spot, sustained by infrastructure spending in India and Southeast Asia, but export margins are razor-thin and vulnerable to tariffs—US coal exports fell 16 million short tons in 2025, with China imports down 92% after Beijing imposed retaliatory tariffs. Production costs are rising due to equipment wear and wage inflation, pushing smaller miners into losses during 2023-2025 until the mid-2026 price spike. Coal's competitiveness hinges almost entirely on commodity prices and geopolitical shocks, not operational fundamentals.
- Recent: March 2025: US coal prices peaked at $138/MT on thermal, $197.50/MT on metallurgical (March-January range), then fell back through 2025 as inventory built and global demand softened. | April 2025: Trump administration issued executive orders forcing ~3.2 GW of coal plants to delay 2025 retirements to 2026 or later; only 2.6 GW retired in 2025 vs. 8.5 GW initially planned. | May 2026: Geopolitical tensions (Iran conflict) spiked coking coal to ~$240/ton before cooling back to $128-130/ton by July 2026. | January 2025: Arch Resources and CONSOL Energy completed merger, forming Core Natural Resources (Arch shareholders got 1.326 Core shares per Arch share).
