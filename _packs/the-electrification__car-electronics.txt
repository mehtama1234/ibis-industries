### Automobile Electronics Manufacturing in the US (Manufacturing)
- Market size: $29.6 billion (2025) | growth: -3.0% (2025) | margin: 4.7% (2022 baseline); margins under pressure from tariffs and fixed contracts | businesses: 560 establishments, 511 enterprises (2022 baseline); declining due to consolidation | employees: ~58,000-60,000
- 2022 baseline: $25.0 billion
- Takeaway: US automobile electronics manufacturing is expanding in EV and autonomous-systems content but shrinking in total revenue and employment, squeezed by tariffs, nearshoring to Mexico, Chinese competition, and supplier bankruptcies—a painful transition favoring large, well-capitalized firms.
- Current dynamics: The industry is experiencing a bifurcated pull. Demand for advanced electronics—particularly for EV battery management, autonomous driving sensors, in-cabin AI systems, and vehicle connectivity—is accelerating and will drive billions in new orders by 2030. At the same time, structural headwinds are brutal. Tariffs (25% on Chinese components, escalating further in January 2026) are making it expensive to import; but domestically, companies operate under fixed-cost contracts with automakers, so they cannot pass tariff costs through without renegotiation. Smaller Tier 2 and Tier 3 suppliers are filing for bankruptcy at record rates (2025 filings exceeded any year in the past decade); Fisker, Accuride, and First Brands Group all collapsed in 2024-2025. Major players like Lear and Visteon are still profitable but are being forced to restructure, automate, and consolidate around EV and autonomous-systems capabilities to compete against lower-wage Mexico and increasingly sophisticated Chinese exports. Mexico is absorbing $9.26 billion in automotive investment in 2025 alone, with nearshoring

### Circuit Board & Electronic Component Manufacturing in the US (Manufacturing)
- Market size: $61.3 billion (2026) | growth: 1.0% CAGR 2021-2026; -0.5% in 2026 alone | margin: ~6% (unchanged from 2022, under pressure from input costs and price competition) | businesses: ~2,442 establishments (down 0.5% annually) | employees: 169,973 in 2022; decline continues at ~0.5% annually
- 2022 baseline: $55.4 billion (2022)
- Takeaway: High-value segments (AI, 5G, defense) are firing—driving automation, reshoring, and consolidation—while commodity PCBs and assembly remain under pressure from tariffs, capacity bottlenecks, and decades of cost competition.
- Current dynamics: The industry is caught between three forces. First: tariffs have made imports expensive (25-35% on Chinese PCBs, plus 10% global baseline), but US domestic capacity is so bottlenecked that buyers wait days just for quotes, with many shops refusing new orders. This has created a window where US manufacturers can raise prices without losing share—but only if they can expand. Second: AI servers and 5G infrastructure are driving explosive demand for high-complexity, high-density boards (layer counts jumped from 16-20 to 28-36 layers, with AI servers rising from 15% of demand in 2025 to over 25% in 2026). Third: bankruptcies and plant closures (TTM closing Anaheim and Santa Clara in 2024-2025, Camston Wrather liquidating) show that mid-tier players can't easily scale into the advanced segments without massive capital. The result: bifurcation. Large players backed by CHIPS Act grants and private capital (TTM's Syracuse Ultra-HDI facility in June 2026, Jabil's $500M expansion, Ventec's new Thailand plant) are investing aggressively. Everyone else is treading water, competing on price agains
- Recent: June 2026: TTM Technologies opens new Ultra-HDI manufacturing facility in Syracuse, NY (215,000 sq ft, up to 400 new jobs) for aerospace and defense | June 2025: Jabil announces $500M multi-year expansion for US cloud computing and AI data-center hardware, operations beginning mid-2026 | August 2025: Ventec International plans new $17M facility in Thailand (2026 launch) to serve automotive and aerospace across US and Asia | July 2025: TTM acquires 750,000-sq-ft facility in Eau Claire, Wisconsin for advanced PCB production targeting data-center networking
