### Chain Restaurants in the US (Food & Drink)
- Market size: $230.6 billion (2026) | growth: -1.0% in 2026; +1.7% in 2025; +2.2% CAGR 2021-2026 | margin: Low single-digit margins under pressure; 42% of operators unprofitable (2025) | businesses: 142,206 chain restaurant establishments (2025) | employees: 15.8 million total restaurant industry workers in 2026; chain restaurants represent substantial portion; 100,000 new jobs planned for 2026
- 2022 baseline: $50.9 billion (2022)
- Takeaway: Chain restaurants are caught between structural wage inflation, tariff pressures, and a consumer base choosing value-driven casual dining over premium sit-down experiences.
- Current dynamics: Chain restaurants are caught in a structural cost-margin trap. Labor costs have jumped 39% since pre-pandemic levels, with minimum wages now $16-$20 per hour in major states (California FAST Act $20 for national chains; Washington $16.66; Florida heading to $15 by September 2026). Food inflation sits 34% above pre-pandemic, and tariffs have forced 62% of operators to raise menu prices. Consumer spending at restaurants is growing only 1.3% in real terms, while 60% of operators report deteriorated business conditions in 2025 and 42% are unprofitable. Fast-casual's weakness has paradoxically strengthened casual dining: Chili's and Texas Roadhouse thrive by offering $12 sit-down deals that undercut $15 limited-service entrees. However, consumers are also defecting to grocery and convenience stores, takeout apps, and at-home experiences, shrinking traffic across all formats.

### Hotels & Motels in the US (Consumer Services)
- Market size: 286.5 billion dollars in 2025 | growth: 0.5% in 2025, projected 0.6% in 2026 | margin: Estimated around 17-18% at large operators, lower at economy segment | businesses: Approximately 142,000+ establishments in the US, with continued growth in conversion projects | employees: 2.15 million in 2024, rising to 2.17 million in 2025
- 2022 baseline: 258.1 billion dollars in 2022
- Takeaway: The US hotel industry is a mature, labor-constrained market bifurcating into high-margin luxury and extended-stay growth versus low-margin economy decline, disrupted by AI workflow automation and haunted by Sonder's spectacular failure.
- Current dynamics: The industry is experiencing a stark divergence between segments. Luxury boutique hotels achieved a six percent revenue gain in 2025 with average daily rates of 452 dollars, while independent luxury boutiques hit RevPAR of 307 dollars—well above the broader luxury average of 263 dollars. Extended-stay hotels grew 4 percent in supply in 2025, the largest increase in four years, driven by project-based employment and hybrid work patterns; they now occupy 12.4 percent above industry average occupancy. Conversely, economy and traditional midscale properties are in contraction. The industry is shifting from organic development to conversion projects, which allow faster reopenings with lower capex. Labor costs have become the primary margin destroyer: wages rose 25.6 percent versus 2019, and hotels are running skeleton crews with 70-80 percent annual turnover. On distribution, direct booking remains contested; metasearch and Google Hotel Ads now command 8.2 billion dollars in annual spend, bypassing OTA interfaces.
