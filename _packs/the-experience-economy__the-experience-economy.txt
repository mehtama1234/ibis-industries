### Movie Theaters in the US (Media & Entertainment)
- Market size: $8.6–8.8 billion (2025) | growth: −5% to −10% year-over-year 2024–2025; remains 23–27% below 2019 pre-pandemic peak | margin: ~7.3% industry average (2022); AMC Q2-2026 EBITDA margin 20% on rebounds, Cinemark 18.6% (2025) | businesses: ~2,200 enterprises (2022: 2,193); AMC, Cinemark, Regal dominate; iPic, LOOK, CMX filing bankruptcy 2024-2026 | employees: ~193,000 estimated (2022 baseline 193,789; likely flat to declining 2025-2026)
- 2022 baseline: $8.233 billion (2022)
- Takeaway: US movie theaters recovered to $8.6B (2025) from pandemic lows but face permanent attendance loss—event-driven hits now carry the load while casual moviegoing has died, forcing chains to close unprofitable locations, raise prices on premium formats, and negotiate longer theatrical windows to compete with streaming.
- Current dynamics: Attendance is stuck in two modes: event movies (Inside Out 2, Deadpool & Wolverine) draw crowds; everything else fails. The 2019–2025 drop of 34% in total ticket volume (1.225B down to 780M) masks this bifurcation. Streaming has become the structural competitor, with 1.1 billion SVOD subscribers now permanent (2x pre-pandemic), yet studios are reversing course: at CinemaCon 2026, every major studio committed to 45-day (or longer) theatrical windows. The data shows a 26-45 day window maximizes both box office and downstream streaming, forcing a détente. Ticket prices rose 5.7% in 2025 to offset attendance loss, but volume-price tradeoff is tightening. Consolidation accelerated: AMC shuttered 200+ theaters; iPic, LOOK, CMX filed Chapter 11; Cinemark and Regal are the survivors with lower debt and better fundamentals. Theater closures, market concentration, and premium format proliferation are now the defining moves.
