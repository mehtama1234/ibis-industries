### Cable Providers in the US (Media & Entertainment)
- Market size: $135.7 billion (cable TV segment), $366.6 billion (global broadcasting and cable) - 2025 | growth: -2.6% annual decline in cable TV subscribers (2025-2026 period) | margin: 8.6-9.8% (2022 baseline); under pressure in 2025-2026 | businesses: 4,281 establishments (2022 baseline); consolidation ongoing | employees: 237,657 (2025)
- 2022 baseline: $99.3 billion revenue
- Takeaway: Cable TV is collapsing into streaming irrelevance while cable broadband—the business that was supposed to replace it—is now losing subscribers to fiber and 5G home internet for the first time in its 30-year history.
- Current dynamics: Cable is caught in a vicious cycle: rapid video subscriber losses force rate hikes, which accelerate cord-cutting, especially among younger, price-sensitive households where only 16% still subscribe versus 64% of those over 65. Simultaneously, broadband—long the growth engine—turned negative in 2025 for the first time, losing ground to fiber (now ~27% of broadband market) and especially 5G home internet (T-Mobile added 1.8M FWA customers in 2025 alone, AT&T added 900K). The industry is responding with aggressive consolidation (Charter-Cox merger) and technology upgrades (DOCSIS 4.0 deployment ramping in 2026), but these require huge capital while margins stay thin due to rising content costs and competitive pressure. Smaller regional providers face existential threats; over 50 cable TV companies are expected to cease operations or exit TV services in 2026.
- Recent: Charter-Cox merger ($34.5B) approved by FCC in March 2026; expected close summer 2026; creates largest broadband provider with 38M+ customers across 41 states | Cable broadband experienced net subscriber losses for first time in history during 2025 (first year of decline after decades of growth) | Comcast and Charter began commercial DOCSIS 4.0 deployments in 2024-2025 to compete with fiber and FWA; full rollout accelerating into 2026 | T-Mobile reached 8M+ fixed wireless customers by late 2025 with 1.8M added in 2025 alone; AT&T added 900K FWA customers; fixed wireless now serves 7-8% of US broadband market

### Vending Machine Operators in the US (Retail)
- Market size: $7.9 billion (2026) | growth: -1.6% CAGR through 2026; -0.5% in 2026 alone (2025-2026) | margin: 25-35% net margin typical (2025-2026); pressured by supplier costs | businesses: 14,801 establishments (2026); declined 3.8% CAGR from 2021-2026 | employees: 58,096 workers (2026); -0.7% change 2025 to 2026
- 2022 baseline: $10.2 billion (2022)
- Takeaway: US vending machine operators generate $7.9B (2026) in a consolidating, technology-driven market where smart/cashless machines and AI-powered fleet management compete for survival against micromarkets, work-from-home trends, and declining soda consumption.
- Current dynamics: The industry is in structural decline masked by technology adoption. Mechanical push-button machines are being replaced by cashless, AI-enabled units that predict inventory, offer dynamic pricing, and enable real-time fleet management. Cashless payment now commands 58% market share (2025) vs. cash 42%, raising average transaction value from $1.78 to $2.24. However, core demand is shrinking: per capita soda consumption falling; health-conscious consumers avoiding traditional snacks; return-to-office stalled (hybrid work remains ~44% in 2025-2026, not pre-pandemic norms). Micromarkets—self-checkout unattended retail kiosks—are cannibalizing high-value locations, generating $20/month per customer vs. $7 at traditional vending. Consolidation accelerated (top 20 global operators acquired 40+ regional players 2023-2025). Profitability pressured by inflation in input costs, location commissions, and logistics.
