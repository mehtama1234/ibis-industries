- Market size: 11.3 billion USD (2026) | growth: 0 percent to -0.3 percent projected annually 2025-2030 (flat stagnation after post-pandemic rebound) | margin: 22 percent EBIT margin (2026) | businesses: 15,000-plus establishments; 88 percent independently owned, 2 percent REITs, 10 percent multi-facility operators | employees: 61,856 (2026); grew 4.0 percent annually 2021-2026
- 2022 baseline: 7.1 billion USD (2022)
- Takeaway: The US campgrounds and RV parks industry has shifted from growth-through-expansion to profit-through-optimization as consolidation, constrained supply, and younger, higher-spending campers reshape a once-sleepy sector.
- Current dynamics: Campground revenue rose 5.2 percent in 2025, but total reservations fell 1.0 percent—the first annual decline in platform history—revealing a shift from volume growth to pricing power and yield optimization. Average nightly rates for medium parks jumped 16.6 percent to 83 dollars. Glamping was the only accommodation type to grow, up 43.6 percent, attracting premium-paying younger demographics. Supply remains the industry's secret weapon: new development requires 24-48 months to permit and build, costing 60,000 dollars per site, which deters new entrants and protects existing operators from oversupply. However, operators face a structural profitability squeeze: institutional cap rates sit at 8.0 percent while loan constants run 8.7-9.3 percent, leaving negative day-one leverage and forcing returns to depend on amortization and repositioning rather than immediate cash flow.
