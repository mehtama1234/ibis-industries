### Major Household Appliance Manufacturing in the US (Manufacturing)
- Market size: 24.2 billion USD in 2026 (manufacturing output); broader home appliance retail market 144.83 billion USD in 2026 | growth: 0.7% in 2025; projected 0.3-0.6% through 2026 (essentially flat) | margin: Whirlpool 2.8%, Electrolux -2.9%, LG -2.7%, Samsung negative (Q4 2025) | businesses: 5 major players (Haier/GE Appliances, Whirlpool, Electrolux, LG, Samsung) control roughly 60% of market; 136+ smaller manufacturers | employees: Approximately 21,000 at Whirlpool; 46,600 total US appliance manufacturing (2021 baseline)
- 2022 baseline: 23.4 billion USD in 2021
- Takeaway: Tariffs imposed in June 2025 (50% on steel-equivalent content in appliances) crushed margins for all major players in Q4 2025, forcing companies to absorb costs they could not pass to consumers in a weak, promotional market.
- Current dynamics: Tariffs have replaced housing demand as the dominant industry force. A 50% steel-equivalent tariff on imported appliances (imposed June 2025) added approximately 300 million dollars to Whirlpool's annual costs and forced Electrolux to absorb unpassed tariff costs, destroying Q4 2025 margins. Housing demand is mixed: single-family starts fell 6.9% in 2025 to 943,000 units, but multifamily surged 17.4%, creating demand for compact and stackable configurations. Import penetration persists at 55% of domestic demand despite tariff protection. The industry remains deeply promotional, with manufacturers unable to raise prices. Competition has shifted toward smart appliances (AI-powered, connected to smart home ecosystems), which offer better margins. All major players announced cost reduction programs (150 million USD or more) to offset tariff impacts heading into 2026.
- Recent: June 2025: US government imposed 50% tariff on steel-equivalent content in imported refrigerators, freezers, washers, dryers, dishwashers, and ovens, adding approximately 300 million USD to Whirlpool annual costs. | Q4 2025: All major manufacturers posted negative or razor-thin operating margins (Whirlpool 2.8%, Electrolux -2.9%, LG -2.7%, Samsung negative); tariff costs could not be passed to consumers. | August 2025: GE Appliances (Haier subsidiary) announced 3 billion USD investment in US operations over five years, including modernization of 11 US plants and expansion in Kentucky, Alabama, Georgia, Tennessee, South Carolina. | October 2025: Whirlpool announced 300 million USD investment in US laundry manufacturing (Clyde and Marion, Ohio plants), creating 400-600 jobs by end of 2026/early 2027.
