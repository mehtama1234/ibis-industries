### Video Post-production Services in the US (Media & Entertainment)
- Market size: $6.8 billion (2025) | growth: 5.9% (2025 vs. 2024) | margin: 6.5% (2022)
- Takeaway: The US video post-production sector is recovering from 2023–2024 strike and streaming-budget trauma but now faces an existential AI video generation threat that could automate 20–30% of routine editing work by decade's end.
- Current dynamics: The market faced a cliff in 2023–2024 from the dual writers and actors strikes, which halted production and delayed over 47 TV and film projects into 2024. While studios resume work, streaming giants (Disney, Netflix, Paramount) cut content budgets aggressively in 2023–2024 to improve profitability, reducing downstream demand for post-production. Recovery is underway: Netflix raised content spend to $18 billion in 2025 and targets $20 billion in 2026. However, generative AI video tools (Sora, Runway, Kling) now threaten lower-value editing work, and major studios continue pushing in-house capabilities to cut outsourcing costs. Smaller shops face margin pressure from both directions: fewer projects and lower per-project budgets.
- Recent: May–September 2023: Writers Guild and SAG-AFTRA strikes halt production; motion picture and video industry sheds thousands of jobs. | 2023–2024: Disney, Netflix, and Paramount cut content spending by $2–5 billion combined in response to streaming profitability pressure. | 2024: Motion picture and video sector employment falls to 405,400 (down 21% from 2016, per BLS).
