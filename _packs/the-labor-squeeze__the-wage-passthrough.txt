- 2022 baseline: $30.6 billion (2022)
- Takeaway: The US beer industry is contracting sharply—production down 5.7% in 2025, craft breweries down to 9,344 in June 2026, and 481 closures in 2025—while large producers consolidate, shift to premium and non-alcoholic products, and face tariff-driven cost inflation as consumers increasingly choose alternative beverages.
- Current dynamics: The craft beer sector is experiencing its most severe correction in years. Production fell 5.1% in 2025 and another 4% in the first half of 2026, with only 54% of surveyed breweries reporting growth while 43% reported declines. However, the data shows early stabilization: consumers who identify as craft beer drinkers are drinking more frequently (85% monthly consumption, up 10 points), brewery visits are rising (5.5 times annually), and market share by volume held steady at 13.4%. The industry is bifurcating: large players with automation and scale are surviving and gaining share, while smaller independents are either closing or pivoting hard into experiential models (taproom-first, food programs, local sourcing).
