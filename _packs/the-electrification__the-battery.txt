### Battery Recycling (Energy & Environment)
- Market size: $6.76 billion (2025) | growth: 9.81% CAGR (2026-2035); lithium-ion specifically growing at 22.24% CAGR | margin: Tight; NMC packs $1,700/pack net economics in US (before OPEX), but LFP packs can be -$1,800 to recycle (2026) | businesses: 155+ establishments (2026); growing from 151 in 2021 | employees: ~2,400+ (2026 estimate, up from 2,341 in 2021)
- 2022 baseline: $765 million (2021, most recent IBISWorld)
- Takeaway: Battery recycling is building expensive domestic capacity for an EV recycling wave that won't arrive until 2030-2035, while Chinese competition and unfavorable LFP economics squeeze margins today.
- Current dynamics: The industry is caught between explosive growth in announced capacity and a severe feedstock drought. US recycling capacity hit 144,000 tons in 2024 and is racing to 300,000 tons by 2026—but actual EV battery volumes won't hit 1.3 million units annually until 2030 at earliest, leaving plants running 25-30% full. Chinese battery prices have crashed, forcing Redwood to cut 135 jobs (10% of workforce) in early 2026, while Ascend Elements filed Chapter 11 in April 2026 after losing $480 million in DOE funding. Economics are chemistry-dependent and broken: NMC/NCM batteries (nickel-heavy) yield ~$1,700 per pack in recovered value before operating costs, but LFP batteries (rising fast in EVs) have negative recovery value, turning recyclers into disposal contractors. Second-life batteries are filling the gap—120 GWh of EV packs worldwide reach end-of-vehicle-life in 2026 with 70-80% capacity still usable, shifting from immediate recycling to 5-10 year grid storage deployments.
