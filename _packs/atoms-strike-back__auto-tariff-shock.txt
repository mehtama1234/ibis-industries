FORCE: Atoms Strike Back — Trade war and tariffs force reshoring, nearshoring, and inventory hoarding — physical supply chains get expensive and political, and whoever moves fastest to nearshore wins.
THIS SUBFORCE: The auto tariff shock
ANGLE: 2025 tariffs and the EV transition force the whole auto supply chain to reorder toward Mexico.

EVIDENCE (real 2025-2026 US industry data we researched; use these numbers, each dated):

### Auto Parts Manufacturing in the US (Manufacturing)
- Market size: $74.9 billion (2026) | growth: 5.4% annual (2026) | margin: 5.0% (2026) | businesses: 1,091 (2026) | employees: 140,405 to 587,500 (2025-2026, depending on scope)
- 2022 baseline: $66.9 billion (2022)
- Takeaway: US auto parts makers are caught between 25% tariffs that make imports expensive, an accelerating EV transition that voids traditional component demand, and rapid nearshoring to Mexico that is now the only viable way to stay competitive.
- Current dynamics: The 25% tariff on imported auto parts (effective April-May 2025) has hit the sector hard, forcing suppliers to absorb costs or risk losing customers to rivals. Manufacturers are rapidly reorienting supply chains toward Mexico rather than reshoring to the US; in the first nine months of 2025, new Mexican investment surged 200%. Electric vehicle adoption is slowing demand for traditional exhaust systems, radiators, and engine components, while simultaneously driving demand for battery thermal management, electronics, and lightweight materials. The aftermarket remains a bright spot as the average US vehicle ages and needs repair. Bankruptcy filings among auto parts suppliers hit decade highs in 2025, and employment fell despite stable production, indicating aggressive automation. The industry is consolidating as larger players with R&D muscle acquire smaller specialists unable to invest in EV transition technology.
- Recent: April 3, 2025: Trump administration imposes 25% Section 232 tariffs on auto parts, covering over 150 categories (engines, transmissions, batteries, electronics, brakes, tires); takes effect May 3, 2025 for parts. | H1 2025: Stellantis reports €2.3bn ($2.7bn) net loss partly due to early tariff impacts; JLR profits nearly halve with 4% EBIT margin amid tariff pressure. | H1 2025: BMW, Mercedes, Volkswagen combined $6 billion loss attributed to US tariffs. | 2025: Mexican auto parts investment surges 200% in first nine months as suppliers nearshore production to avoid tariffs.

### Car & Automobile Manufacturing in the US (Manufacturing)
- Market size: $393.3 billion (2026) | growth: −1.0% (2026); +2.3% CAGR (2021–2026) | margin: 4.4% (2026) | businesses: 190 establishments; Big Three (Ford, GM, Stellantis) dominate; 45 major competitors | employees: ~151,000 direct (2026 estimates)
- 2022 baseline: $104.2 billion (2022)
- Takeaway: Tariff shocks, an EV recount, and labor-cost misalignment have flattened 2026 margins despite stable demand.
- Current dynamics: Tariffs are the immediate shock. A 25% import tariff on all vehicles (April 2025) raised consumer car prices by $5,000–$8,900 for imports and $1,600–$2,000 for domestic cars due to steel/aluminum costs. The tariff added $108 billion in costs to the industry, with Ford, GM, and Stellantis absorbing $41.7 billion collectively. China slapped retaliatory 10–15% tariffs on US vehicles, closing that market to Detroit. Meanwhile, EV momentum has stalled—EVs and hybrids were only 20% of new US sales in 2024, and 2025 EV sales actually declined 4% versus 2024, with Tesla's market share slipping from 49% (2024) to 45% (2026). Hybrid demand is robust, but sedans and compact cars—the industry's defined core—remain unprofitable. UAW contracts signed in 2023 cost GM $9.3 billion and Ford $8.8 billion through 2028, locking in labor rates that are uncompetitive versus non-union plants (Toyota, Honda, Hyundai facilities average $52/hour). About 29,000 parts manufacturing jobs disappeared in 2025, though assembly plants held steady.
- Recent: April 2025: Trump administration imposes 25% tariff on all auto imports and 25% on steel/aluminum, raising vehicle prices $1,600–$8,900; Chinese retaliation tariffs follow. | May 2025: 70 new battery manufacturing facilities open in US (55 primary supply chain); US reaches 20 GWh/year cell capacity, approaching oversupply. | June 2025: Waymo One robotaxi completes 250,000 paid rides per week; Zoox launches in Las Vegas, plans to charge fares in 2026. | 2025 Full Year: EV sales decline 4% YoY; UAW wages boost labor costs +8.3% YoY; 29,000 parts manufacturing jobs lost despite stable assembly output.

### Automobile Engine & Parts Manufacturing in the US (Manufacturing)
- Market size: $43.8 billion in 2026 | growth: 2.5% in 2026, 1.5% CAGR 2021–2026 | margin: 8.1% (2022) | businesses: 774 establishments, 633 enterprises (2022) | employees: Approximately 62,000–63,000 workers (2022 baseline: 62,106; 2027 projection: 63,419)
- 2022 baseline: $37.3 billion
- Takeaway: Gasoline engine manufacturing is no longer dying; it is evolving into a three-fuel ecosystem (gasoline, hybrid-electric, mild-hybrid 48V) while tariffs and supplier bankruptcies force consolidation and nearshoring.
- Current dynamics: The sector faces a paradox: electric vehicles are growing (hybrids up 10+ percent year-on-year), yet gasoline engine demand has *not* collapsed. Instead, OEMs are stretching ICE platform life by investing in turbocharging, variable compression, and 48-volt mild-hybrid architecture. Tariff regimes—US 25% on auto parts effective April 2025, Mexico 25–50% tariffs on non-FTA parts starting January 2026—are crimping margins at suppliers and forcing nearshoring decisions. CAFE standards have been reset in June 2025 to exclude EV credits from compliance calculations, giving OEMs more breathing room to sell gasoline vehicles without penalty. Bankruptcies and restructurings among smaller suppliers (First Brands, Accuride, Tayco) signal consolidation pressure. Denso, Bosch, and Continental are doubling down on EV electronics and thermal systems, while traditional engine makers are repositioning as powertrain integrators—producing not just gasoline blocks but hybrid modules and control systems.
- Recent: First Brands (FBG) files Chapter 11 bankruptcy (September 2025); portfolio included Raybestos, Centric, Cardone, Autolite, FRAM engines and parts | US imposes 25% tariffs on autos and auto parts, effective April 2025 | Mexico introduces 25–50% tariffs on auto parts from non-FTA countries, effective January 2026 | Stellantis announces $13 billion US investment plan (October 2025); Kokomo engine plant to produce new four-cylinder GMET4 EVO starting 2026 with 100+ new jobs
