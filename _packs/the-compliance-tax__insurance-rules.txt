### Workers' Compensation Insurance (Finance & Insurance)
- Market size: $51.2 billion (2025) | growth: -1.3% (2025) | margin: ~13% underwriting gain + 9-10% investment gains (2024) | businesses: ~1,000 establishments (2021, declining) | employees: ~50,000 (2021, stable)
- 2022 baseline: $46.9 billion (2021)
- Takeaway: US workers' compensation insurance is in a long-running soft market with premiums declining 3-5% annually, yet profitability remains near record highs from a 12-year streak of underwriting gains—but rising medical costs and claim severity are eroding the reserve cushion and starting to bifurcate the market by carrier capacity.
- Current dynamics: The industry sits in a paradoxical soft market: rates and premiums are falling (down 5% in 2026 via NCCI filings), yet profitability remains near all-time highs due to declining claim frequency (down 5% in 2024) and conservative reserves. However, this cushion is eroding. Claim severity jumped 6% in 2024 and remains high; medical costs are accelerating in certain states (Florida up 41% in 2025 after fee schedule changes); and cumulative trauma and presumption-law claims are expanding the definition of compensable injury. California—home to 10% of US employment—posted a 127% combined loss ratio in 2026, forcing carriers to reduce capacity or exit entirely. The market is bifurcating: carriers with strong capital and predictive analytics are profiting; smaller players and those lacking technology are being pressured out.
