### Energy Drink Production (Food & Drink)
- Market size: $23.9 billion (2025) | growth: 9.7% CAGR 2020-2025 | margin: 10.3-11.3% (estimated 2025) | businesses: 251 establishments (2025) | employees: 38,954 (2025)
- 2022 baseline: $11.7 billion
- Takeaway: The US energy drink industry has doubled in size since 2022 to $23.9 billion (2025) as sugar-free positioning becomes mandatory and acquisition wars intensify to capture female and health-conscious consumers previously outside the category.
- Current dynamics: The industry is in the midst of a dramatic category shift. Sugar-free drinks surpassed full-sugar varieties in dollar sales for the first time, driven by 70% of consumers seeking to limit sugar intake. Red Bull launched Red Bull Zero in November 2024. Consolidation is accelerating: Celsius paid $1.8 billion for Alani Nu (female-focused Gen Z brand) in April 2025, then secured Rockstar through a distribution deal with PepsiCo in September 2025, signaling acquisition wars to capture younger women and health-conscious consumers. Employment grew 10.3% annually 2020-2025 to nearly 39,000 workers, while the number of businesses climbed 10.6% year-over-year to 251, showing continued new entrants. Convenience stores remain dominant at 65.5% of sales but losing share to warehouse clubs (19%) and supermarkets (10.9%) as bulk-buying and price-sensitive consumers trade impulse purchases for economy.

### Candy Production in the US (Food & Drink)
- Market size: $55 billion (2025) | growth: Projected to reach $62.2 billion by 2030 (1.4% annualized 2025-2030); global market forecast 4.7% CAGR 2026-2033 | margin: Declining; compressed by tariffs and input costs; 2022 baseline was 4.5-4.7%, now under pressure | businesses: Mature, consolidated industry; Mars (~$38.5B confectionery sales 2026), Mondelēz, Ferrara are top players | employees: Approx. 24,500+ (estimated from 2022 baseline of 24,436; modest 1% annualized growth projected)
- 2022 baseline: $12.1 billion (US candy production industry only; narrower than today's $55B confectionery total which includes chocolate and imports)
- Takeaway: The US candy industry is shifting from high-sugar commodity production toward premiumized, functional, and texture-driven innovations, while facing unprecedented tariff-driven cost inflation and structural competition from chocolate and imported goods.
- Current dynamics: The US candy market is being reshaped by four overlapping forces. First, tariffs have become a structural cost—a 10% baseline on all imports, 20% from the EU, 31% from Switzerland, and 145% on bulk shipments from China have pushed production costs up 30% since late 2024, forcing price increases that 86% of consumers now notice and resent. Second, health consciousness is no longer a niche: 47% of regular candy buyers actively seek healthier alternatives, with sugar-free, vegan, and vitamin-fortified gummies growing at double digits. Third, texture innovation is creating entirely new categories—freeze-dried candy jumped from niche to $1.31 billion globally in 2025 and is forecast to grow 12% annually through 2033, driven by social media visibility and crunch-seeking Gen Z and Millennial consumers. Fourth, chocolate is consolidating its lead (51.7% of all confectionery sales) while traditional hard and soft candies shrink; yet imports still meet roughly 23% of domestic demand, creating a paradoxical squeeze where both tariffs and foreign competition pressure domestic producers.
- Recent: Mars announced definitive acquisition of Kellanov (August 2024), finalized December 2025; combined $36B global snacking portfolio including Pringles, Cheez-It, Pop-Tarts, M&Ms, Snickers | Trump administration imposed tariffs: 10% baseline, 20% EU, 31% Switzerland, 145% China (April 2025 onward); chocolate production costs jumped 30% Dec 2024–Sept 2025 | Ferrara launched freeze-dried SweeTARTS, Lemonheads, Spree (August 2025); M&Ms launched POP'd Caramel (early 2026) | Unilever acquired Grüns functional gummy brand for $1.2B (April 2026)
