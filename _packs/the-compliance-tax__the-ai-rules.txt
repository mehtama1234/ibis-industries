### Credit Bureaus & Rating Agencies in the US (Finance & Insurance)
- Market size: $17.6 billion (2026) | growth: 2.7% CAGR (2020–2025); 0.6% in 2025 alone | margin: ~29% (stable, though rising compliance costs are pressing margins) | businesses: 1,280 establishments (2026), up 1.8% CAGR since 2020 | employees: Approximately 30,000+ (growing with industry expansion)
- 2022 baseline: $14.2 billion (2022)
- Takeaway: The $17.6 billion US credit bureaus and rating agencies industry is growing slowly (2.7% annually) as AI-powered alternative scoring disrupts traditional models, cybersecurity breaches erode consumer trust, and the SEC moves to overhaul rating-agency regulations.
- Current dynamics: The industry is caught between steady demand and accelerating disruption. Credit demand remains solid: consumers are borrowing for homes and cars (supporting bureaus), and businesses are issuing debt (supporting rating agencies). But alternative credit data is eating the traditional moat. Zest AI's research showed its AI model could approve 25% more loans while holding risk constant, and FICO launched Score 11 in early 2026 to expand coverage by incorporating rent and cash-flow data—a move that could bring 9 million previously credit-invisible consumers into the scoring system. Meanwhile, cybersecurity is becoming a liability. The TransUnion breach (August 2025, 4.4M exposed) and the 700Credit breach (5.8M exposed, July–October 2025) have forced all three major bureaus to invest heavily in security and raised consumer awareness of data freezes. The SEC's proposed overhaul of rating agency rules (targeting governance, analyst independence, and methodologies) will increase operational costs and compliance hiring, further squeezing already-thin margins in the rating side.
