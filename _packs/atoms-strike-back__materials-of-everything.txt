This props up demand even as residential construction saturates and nonresidential office slackens. ### Glass Product Manufacturing in the US (Manufacturing)
- Market size: $33.3 billion (2026) | growth: 5.5% CAGR forecasted 2025-2030 | margin: 4.7% (2022 baseline); likely compressed in 2025-2026 by tariffs | businesses: 4,012 establishments | employees: approximately 99,000
- 2022 baseline: $31.6 billion (2022)
- Takeaway: Tariffs in 2025-2026 disrupted financing and supply chains, but a massive shift from plastic to glass packaging is creating new growth in a historically stagnant industry.
- Current dynamics: Tariffs are the dominant shock. February 2025 introduced broad tariffs; April escalated them to Liberation Day levels; June pushed steel and aluminum to 50%, forcing fabricators to pass costs downstream or absorb margin cuts. Import restrictions on Chinese and Malaysian float glass tightened supply but have not triggered large domestic capacity builds. Simultaneously, the plastic-to-glass shift is real and accelerating: 89% of premium brands plan to switch by end-2026, recycled PET now costs 20% more than virgin plastic, and container glass could grow 45% by 2035. Construction demand remains steady but faces project financing headwinds from tariffs and higher borrowing costs. Energy costs are volatile; manufacturers are investing in renewable electricity and biofuel furnaces (O-I completed a 100% biofuel trial in UK in 2024-2025). Regulatory pressure is mounting on emissions, heavy metals in coatings, and VOC compliance, increasing operational costs especially for smaller firms.
- Recent: April 2026: NSG Group went private via Apollo-backed restructuring, consolidating amid market volatility and tariff uncertainty | July 2026: Apogee Enterprises acquired Kalwall Companies (translucent daylighting solutions) for ~$115 million, expanding architectural glass footprint | December 2025: Transom Capital acquired Binswanger Glass (custom glass design, engineering, fabrication), with close expected late 2026/early 2027 | July 2026: ResponsibleGlass published draft International Standard for Glassmaking; public consultation open through Sept 2026 for first global glass industry sustainability standard

### Rubber Product Manufacturing in the US (Manufacturing)
- Market size: $23.7 billion (2026) | growth: -1.1% (2026); +2.1% CAGR (2021–2026) | margin: 5.7% (2022 baseline); under pressure 2025–2026 | businesses: 1,182 establishments (2026); -0.5% annual decline rate | employees: 61,176 (2026); -0.3% average decline 2021–2026
- 2022 baseline: $19.2 billion
- Takeaway: The US rubber product manufacturing sector is shrinking under tariff pressure, import competition, and input-cost volatility while simultaneously having to upgrade for EV demand and meet tightening environmental rules.
- Current dynamics: Tariffs are the dominant headwind: US manufacturers face approximately 12% tariffs on plastics and rubber products (2025), with Japanese imports hit by 15% baseline tariffs retroactive to August 2025. Synthetic rubber prices remain elevated (USA $2,387/MT in Q1 2026) and volatile, squeezing profit margins on commodity goods and limiting pricing power. Automotive demand—still the largest end market—is weakening due to consumer caution and tariff-driven uncertainty, though EV production creates new specialized tire and component demand. Import penetration continues to rise, with nearly 20% of domestic demand met by imports (up from historical levels), especially from Mexico (84% USMCA-compliant, avoiding 25% tariffs) and Asia. Employment has been flat to declining; wages grew 4.4% in 2025 but manufacturers report difficulty filling skilled roles as competition for talent intensifies. Construction activity, a secondary but growing market, remains subdued but not collapsing.
- Recent: October 2025: US imposes 15% baseline tariff on Japanese tires and rubber imports, retroactive to August 2025 (2025) | May 2025: Sumitomo Rubber Industries acquires Dunlop brand to accelerate premium tire sales and reach 60% premium mix by 2035 (2025) | Q1 2026: Synthetic rubber prices at $2,387/MT in USA; supply tightness reflects geopolitical disruptions and 90% Asian production concentration (2026) | December 2024: Carlisle Fluid Technologies acquires Reinhardt-Technik GmbH to expand dispensing/mixing tech in auto and aerospace (2024)
