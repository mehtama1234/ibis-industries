### Frozen Food Production in the US (Food & Drink)
- Market size: $50.7 billion (2026) | growth: 2.5% in 2026, 4.0% CAGR (2021–2026) | margin: ~4.3% (2021); under pressure from input costs and tariffs (2025–2026) | businesses: Approximately 721 establishments, 563 enterprises (2021 baseline; modest consolidation continuing) | employees: 96,000+ (2021); projected 106,000+ (2026)
- 2022 baseline: $35.2 billion (2021, the most recent year in the IBISWorld report)
- Takeaway: The US frozen food manufacturing industry is a mature, tariff-pressured sector navigating labor scarcity through automation while fighting private-label encroachment and repositioning its value proposition from health to outright convenience.
- Current dynamics: Tariffs have become the defining pressure of 2025–2026. Steel and aluminum tariffs alone surged from $1.6 billion (2024) to $7.79 billion (2025), flowing directly into packaging costs for frozen pizzas and packaged meals. Meanwhile, imported seafood, frozen fries, and vegetables face new duties that are spreading costs 12–18 months down the supply chain—meaning true retail impact will peak in late 2026 and into 2027. Labor scarcity is forcing automation at a faster clip than anticipated: cobots at $25–40k with 18-month ROI are now standard, and 40% of large plants run digital twins to optimize throughput. Yet the industry is not shedding workers; rather, roles are climbing the skill ladder toward technicians and engineers. On the flip side, convenience has overtaken health as the primary shopping motivation (61% in 2026 vs. 52% in 2025), meaning frozen meals now position themselves as time-savers first, not just nutritious alternatives—a subtle but critical repositioning that favors packaged brands over fresh-meal prep services.
