### Diagnostic Imaging Centers (Healthcare)
- Market size: $26.3 billion (2026) | growth: 2.1% in 2025; 1.7% CAGR 2021-2026 | margin: Approximately 17-18% (2021-2026 period) | businesses: 5,030 establishments (2026); 3.6% CAGR growth 2021-2026 | employees: Data from 2026 IBISWorld indicates employment in high skilled radiologists and technologists, impacted by chronic staffing shortages
- 2022 baseline: $23.3 billion (2021 IBISWorld baseline, reported as 2021 currency)
- Takeaway: Diagnostic imaging centers face persistent 2% growth, radiologist shortages, Medicare cuts, and hospital competition, with consolidation and AI adoption reshaping the competitive landscape.
- Current dynamics: The industry is splitting into two speeds: larger consolidated players and technology-enabled networks are investing heavily in AI, cloud platforms, and teleradiology to offset labor shortages and improve turnaround times. Smaller independent centers struggle with reimbursement cuts, high labor costs, and the capital burden of AI adoption. Medicare conversion factor dropped 2.83% in 2025, continuing a multi-decade decline in imaging reimbursement—MRI payments have fallen 76.5% since 2003, CT by 69.1%—forcing centers to chase volume and shift to private payers. Hospital-based imaging continues capturing market share from freestanding centers (hospitals hold 41.5% of outpatient imaging claims), though regional variation is stark. Radiologist shortage is acute: 961 diagnostic radiology residency applicants went unmatched in 2025, and it takes 130 days to fill a radiology position. Private equity is actively consolidating the market (13 acquisitions in 2025), betting on AI automation, scale efficiencies, and platform consolidation to restore margins.
