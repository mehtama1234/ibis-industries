### Fruit & Nut Farming in the US (Agriculture)
- Market size: $38.2 billion (2026) | growth: -1.7% (2026); -0.1% CAGR 2021-2026 | margin: 7.5% (2021, latest available) | businesses: 88,433 establishments; 87,649 enterprises (2021) | employees: 231,754 (2021, latest available)
- 2022 baseline: $24.8 billion (2021, latest in 2022 report)
- Takeaway: A capital-intensive commodity sector caught between California's water crisis, labor inflation, tariff volatility, and secular fruit-demand weakness is consolidating around high-margin nuts and organic premium crops.
- Current dynamics: Water is the binding constraint. California almonds use 3.2 million acre-feet annually (50% of Los Angeles' household water) and it takes one gallon of water to grow one almond. The 2022-2025 drought forced 80% of US almond production to face restrictions; growers report only 105,000 new non-bearing almond acres in 2025, down sharply from historical norms. Pollination costs doubled in a decade: beekeeper fees hit $209 per hive in 2025 (up 15% from 2024), with 2 million hives trucked to California each spring. A 62% honeybee loss in 2025 made this worse. Labor is the second crisis. Fruit harvest work is seasonal and unskilled, but growers now lose 10-20% of harvestable crops due to labor scarcity; 21% of respondents saw profitability directly cut by wage pressure.
