### Cable Networks in the US (Media & Entertainment)
- Market size: $127.5 billion (US broadcast and cable TV combined) - 2025 | growth: -3.4% CAGR projected to 2026 | margin: Operating margin ~18.66%, EBITDA margin ~22.19% as of Q1 2026 | businesses: Approximately 1,019 cable network establishments in the US; dominated by Disney, Warner Bros. Discovery, Comcast, and Fox | employees: 54,338 in cable networks; total industry workforce declining
- 2022 baseline: $93.968 billion (cable networks revenue) - 2022
- Takeaway: Cord-cutting has pushed cable TV from serving 88% of US households in 2010 to just 42% by 2026, forcing industry giants to divest cable networks and concentrate on streaming while advertising revenue falls 6-8% annually.
- Current dynamics: Cable subscriptions fell from 96.3 million to 68.7 million by 2024, with Comcast losing 1.15 million TV subscribers in 2025 alone (roughly 3,500 per day). Cable advertising revenue crashed from $22.5 billion in 2024 to $20.2 billion in 2025—a 10% drop in one year—with an additional 6% decline expected in 2026 and 8% in 2027. Live sports remains the last pillar keeping linear cable alive: ESPN launched ESPN Unlimited streaming in August 2025 ($30/month), and Fox rolled out Fox One DTC service ($20/month) bundling sports content. The profitability picture masks the underlying crisis: while EBITDA margins remain healthy at 22%, revenue is shrinking so fast that absolute profit dollars are collapsing. Streaming viewership overtook cable in 2025 (47.5% vs 20.2%), and only 52% of Americans watched cable or satellite TV in 2025, down from near-universal adoption a decade ago.
