### Aluminum Manufacturing in the US (Manufacturing)
- Market size: $67.7 billion (2025), projected $71.8 billion (2026) | growth: 6.09% CAGR through 2034 (2025-2026) | margin: Data not available for 2025-2026 | businesses: Four primary smelters (Alcoa and Century Aluminum); Novelis and 100+ secondary processors; 364 enterprises (2022 baseline) | employees: 165,000 direct jobs (2025-2026); secondary/recycling jobs up 20%+ since 2024
- 2022 baseline: $46.5 billion (2022)
- Takeaway: US aluminum manufacturing is supply-constrained and price-booming in 2025-2026, with tariff protection and EV demand driving growth—but kept from expanding by an electricity crisis that makes smelting uncompetitive against AI data centers.
- Current dynamics: Aluminum prices surged 25% year-over-year by August 2026 to $3,273/ton, driven by China's production ceiling (45.5M tons by end-2026) capping global supply, Russia's export collapse, and the 50% US tariff rate enacted June 2025. Automotive demand accelerated as EV makers demand aluminum for battery enclosures and body panels to offset battery weight. However, the primary smelting sector remains half-idled: four US smelters produce only 683,500 tonnes/year with just two at full capacity because electricity now costs $115+/MWh—tech companies outbidding aluminum makers for renewable power. Secondary (recycled) aluminum boomed as energy-efficient alternative: 85% of US-made aluminum is now recycled, up from 20-30% in the 1980s. Novelis, the largest rolled-products maker, lost production due to fires at Oswego, NY (Sept-Nov 2025) but is opening Bay Minette, Alabama (600 kt capacity) in H2 2026. Century Aluminum restarted Mt. Holly capacity in June 2026 after $50M investment.
