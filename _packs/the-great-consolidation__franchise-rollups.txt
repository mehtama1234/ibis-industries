### Residential Senior Care Franchises (Healthcare)
- Market size: $415 billion total senior care services; $110 billion non-medical in-home care franchises (2026) | growth: 6-9% annually, accelerating to high-single-digit as boomers enter 80s in early 2030s (2026) | margin: 14.8% (2021); not separately updated for 2026 but wage pressures intensifying | businesses: 121,000 businesses in elderly/disabled services (2026) | employees: grew from 163,813 (2021) to over 220,000 estimated (2026); 711,700 new home care jobs projected 2022-2032
- 2022 baseline: $5.967 billion franchise revenue (2021 baseline from IBISWorld); projected $9.0 billion by 2026
- Takeaway: The US residential senior care franchises industry is in a demographic sweet spot—73 million baby boomers entering retirement with guaranteed occupancy demand—but labor shortages are clamping profit margins even as private equity floods in to roll up fragmentation.
- Current dynamics: The industry is being remade by three forces. First, the supply-demand crunch is severe: assisted living beds are growing just 1-2% annually while the over-80 population grows at 4%, forcing residual demand into franchised home and community-based care where operators enjoy wider margins. Second, staffing is the binding constraint; registered nurses, CNAs, and dining staff report 40% turnover; over half of home care agencies report ongoing shortages, pushing wages up 31% cumulatively since 2019 and forcing operators to choose between losing margins or rationing service. Third, consolidation is accelerating as private equity firms see demographic tailwinds as a safe bet: 2025 saw 871 M&A transactions (20.8% above 2024's record), with assisted living making up 44% of deals; on an annualized basis, 2026 is surpassing both prior years. Baby boomers' entry into 80+ cohort starting in 2027 will intensify all three pressures simultaneously.
- Recent: BrightSpring Health Services IPO (Jan 2024) via KKR backing validated institutional thesis on home and community-based care at scale | FTC and DOJ opened formal investigation into PE acquisition surge (Mar 2024); no enforcement yet but signals regulatory scrutiny | National occupancy reached 88.7% in Q3 2025, the 17th consecutive quarterly increase, signaling strong demand normalization post-pandemic | 2025 M&A hit 871 deals, 20.8% above prior record of 721 (2024); assisted living dominated at 44% of deals (Q4 2025 data, Jan 2026 reporting)

### Chain Restaurants in the US (Food & Drink)
- Market size: $230.6 billion (2026) | growth: -1.0% in 2026; +1.7% in 2025; +2.2% CAGR 2021-2026 | margin: Low single-digit margins under pressure; 42% of operators unprofitable (2025) | businesses: 142,206 chain restaurant establishments (2025) | employees: 15.8 million total restaurant industry workers in 2026; chain restaurants represent substantial portion; 100,000 new jobs planned for 2026
- 2022 baseline: $50.9 billion (2022)
- Takeaway: Chain restaurants are caught between structural wage inflation, tariff pressures, and a consumer base choosing value-driven casual dining over premium sit-down experiences.
- Current dynamics: Chain restaurants are caught in a structural cost-margin trap. Labor costs have jumped 39% since pre-pandemic levels, with minimum wages now $16-$20 per hour in major states (California FAST Act $20 for national chains; Washington $16.66; Florida heading to $15 by September 2026). Food inflation sits 34% above pre-pandemic, and tariffs have forced 62% of operators to raise menu prices. Consumer spending at restaurants is growing only 1.3% in real terms, while 60% of operators report deteriorated business conditions in 2025 and 42% are unprofitable. Fast-casual's weakness has paradoxically strengthened casual dining: Chili's and Texas Roadhouse thrive by offering $12 sit-down deals that undercut $15 limited-service entrees. However, consumers are also defecting to grocery and convenience stores, takeout apps, and at-home experiences, shrinking traffic across all formats.
- Recent: Denny's acquired by TriArtisan, Treville, and Yadav Enterprises for $620 million (all-cash, $6.25/share); deal expected Q1 2026. After decades public, chain goes private amid operational restructuring. | TGI Fridays and Red Lobster filed Chapter 11 in 2024; both closed 130+ locations. FAT Brands (Smokey Bones parent) filed Chapter 11 Jan 2026; all remaining Smokey Bones locations closed. | On The Border Mexican Grill filed Chapter 11 Mar 2025; Bravo Brio filed Chapter 11 Aug 2025 (second time in five years). Full-service casual dining segment hit hard. | California's FAST Act (2024) mandates $20/hour minimum wage for national fast-food chains 60+ locations; other states follow with increases (WA $16.66, targeting $15+ nationwide by mid-2026).
