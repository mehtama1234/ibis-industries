### Eyeglasses & Contact Lens Stores in the US (Retail)
- Market size: $23.2 billion (2025) | growth: 2–3% CAGR projected 2025–2033 | margin: 5.4% (2022), trending slightly up | businesses: ~14,600 locations, ~6,800 independent enterprises | employees: ~79,000–85,000 (2022–2027 projection)
- 2022 baseline: $13.1 billion (2022)
- Takeaway: A mature $23B retail market anchored by aging demographics and prescription inelasticity, now pressured by tariffs, online disruption, and vertical integration by mega-players, while independents hang on in niches.
- Current dynamics: The industry faces a three-front squeeze. First, tariffs: 2025 saw plastic frames from China hit with combined duties of 64–155%, contact lenses at 154.5%, driving 50% of eye care providers to expect cost pass-through to consumers. Second, channel disruption: e-commerce grows at 6–7% CAGR, driven by Warby Parker's online-first model (guidance: $878–893M revenue, 14–16% growth in 2025) and Zenni's sub-$100 positioning; yet offline still holds 83% of distribution. Third, consolidation under pressure: private equity deal flow slowed in early 2025 due to tariff uncertainty, but the 20-year trend ($17B+ in eye care PE deals since 2019) remains intact. Separately, blue-light-filtering glasses hit $2.9B (2024) and are forecast at $5.8B by 2034 as screen time drives new demand segments. Vision insurance continues to mediate price sensitivity: most copays run $10–20 for exams, but frames still cost $150+ out-of-pocket above insurance allowances, perpetuating the value/premium split.
