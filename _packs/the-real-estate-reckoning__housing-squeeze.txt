- Takeaway: Apartment rents have stopped climbing and flat-lined after years of double-digit growth, exposing oversupply in the Sun Belt while rent-controlled markets squeeze landlords between frozen pricing and soaring costs, driving industry consolidation and bankruptcies.
- Current dynamics: The apartment market is in transition from a supply-constrained, rent-growth decade to one of oversupply and pricing pressures, particularly in the Sun Belt and Mountain West where developers chased 2023–2025 building booms. Vacancy rates have climbed from a five-year low of 6.3% in 2021 to 7.0% in Q2 2025, with luxury segments sitting at 11.4% vacant—forcing operators to offer concessions instead of raising rents. Meanwhile, rent-regulated markets in New York and California face a cruel squeeze: tenants pay record-high percentages of income for housing, yet landlords cannot raise rents fast enough (NYC: ~2.75%–5.25% per Rent Guidelines Board; California: 5% + CPI capped at 10%) to cover surging insurance, labor, and maintenance costs.
