### Cardiologists (Healthcare)
- Market size: $67.2 billion (2025) | growth: 2.5% CAGR (2020-2025); 2.0% growth in 2025 | margin: 13.9% of revenue (2025) | businesses: ~165,000 establishments (2025); down from ~240,000 in 2021 due to consolidation | employees: Not precisely stated in recent reports; workforce shortage expected to worsen by 2031 (−547 cardiologists/year)
- 2022 baseline: $51.3 billion (2021)
- Takeaway: The US cardiologists industry—worth $67.2 billion in 2025 with 2.5% CAGR—is collapsing private practice, absorbing private equity deals, grappling with severe workforce shortages and reimbursement cuts, while AI and remote monitoring transform clinical workflows.
- Current dynamics: The cardiology industry is caught between soaring demand and a shrinking supply of cardiologists. Heart disease remains the leading cause of death in the US (1 in 4 deaths), with obesity prevalence now at 41.9% (2025-2026) and heart failure cases growing from 6.7 to 7.7 million adults in under five years. Yet the profession is in crisis: the cardiologist-to-patient ratio will deteriorate from 1 per 1,087 patients (2025) to 1 per 1,700 patients (2035), while burnout and early retirements are projected to cost the field 547 cardiologists annually by 2031. Private practice has virtually disappeared—90% of cardiologists now work for hospitals or PE-backed consolidators—driven by unsustainable reimbursement (Medicare payments fell 29% from 2005-2021 while hospital payments rose 70%) and administrative burden. For 2026, CMS imposed fresh cuts: stress tests drop from $311.40 to $220.60, amyloid PET imaging was slashed 57%, and left atrial appendage closures were cut 27% despite being critical procedures.
