### Electricians in the US (Business Services)
- Market size: $370.9 billion (2026) | growth: Down 1.1% in 2026 (set to fall); +6.2% CAGR over five years (2021-2026) | margin: Average 5-6% net; top-quartile 10-12%; target 10-20% net | businesses: 269,000 establishments (2026); 3.7% CAGR growth 2021-2026 | employees: 1.1+ million (projected 9% growth 2024-2034)
- 2022 baseline: $225.4 billion (2022)
- Takeaway: The US electricians industry is being pulled between a dead residential market and explosive demand from AI/EV/grid infrastructure, creating labor shortages, wage pressure, and consolidation among the 269,000 small contractors.
- Current dynamics: Data center construction has become the industry's unexpected growth engine in 2026, with spending reaching $50.7 billion annually (up 28% year-over-year), offsetting the collapse in residential construction caused by high interest rates. The electrician shortage is now acute enough that Nvidia and Microsoft CEOs have cited it as a bottleneck for AI infrastructure expansion. Electricians are commanding higher wages (journeymen earning $76K-$110K+), and specialized work in solar, EV charging, and data centers commands premium rates with above-average margins. However, tariff-driven material cost increases—copper up 18%, electrical panels up 22%—are squeezing smaller contractors, while M&A activity is rebounding with PE platforms aggressively consolidating fragmented firms. Roughly one-third of revenue now comes from upgrades and maintenance rather than new construction.

### Home Care Providers in the US (Healthcare)
- Market size: $191.6 billion in 2026 | growth: 3.9% in 2026; 6.7% CAGR (2021-2026) | margin: Near zero to negative in many agencies; strong performers target 15-20% SDE margins; 34% of agencies reported profit concerns in 2025-2026 | businesses: 437,000+ establishments; highly fragmented; private equity owns ~41% of deal volume in 2026 | employees: Over 2.4 million projected in 2026; 17% growth in home health aide positions projected 2024-2034
- 2022 baseline: $109.6 billion in 2021
- Takeaway: The US home care industry is growing due to an aging population preferring care at home, but faces existential margin compression from stacked payment cuts, severe workforce shortages at 75% annual turnover, and regulatory complexity that is driving consolidation into larger platforms.
- Current dynamics: Medicare payment cuts hit home care providers for four straight years, with 2026 seeing a 1.3% aggregate reduction ($220 million) after a 6.4% cut was initially proposed. These cuts are driving a wave of agency closures in rural and underserved areas. Simultaneously, the industry faces a caregiver shortage crisis: 95% of agencies report moderate-to-severe staffing shortages, with agencies turning away up to 25% of patient requests. Caregiver wages rose from $13.07 per hour in 2014 to $16.77 in 2024, but turnover remains at 75% annually due to low pay relative to other healthcare roles. Private equity continues to acquire and consolidate the fragmented market, with 55 home health and hospice deals in the first half of 2026 alone, 41% PE-backed. Technology adoption is accelerating, with cloud-based EMRs and remote patient monitoring becoming mainstream, though integration remains incomplete and creates administrative burden. Value-based care models are shifting financial risk to lower-performing agencies through CMS rebalancing of quality scorecards.
- Recent: CMS finalizes 2026 home health Medicare payment rule with 1.3% aggregate reduction (November 2025), down from 6.4% proposed cut | General Atlantic acquires TEAM Services Group for ~$3 billion (Q2 2026), largest home care deal on record | Kinderhook Industries acquires Enhabit for $1.1 billion take-private (Q1 2026) | Humana snaps up remaining stake in Kindred Healthcare for $5.7 billion to fully own the home care giant (2024)

### General Freight Trucking (Truckload) (Transport & Logistics)
- Market size: $300.1 billion in 2026 | growth: 3.4% growth in 2026; 1.6% growth in 2025 | margin: Less than 1% average for truckload carriers in 2025; only LTL and 1,000+ truck fleets profitable | businesses: 660,000-795,000 establishments; 88.9% are nonemployers (owner-operators) | employees: Approximately 1.3 million (per 2021 data; exact 2026 count not publicly disclosed)
- 2022 baseline: $212.2 billion in 2021
- Takeaway: The US truckload trucking sector is a mature, fragmented market experiencing a shakeout as bankruptcies and capacity exits outpace freight demand, creating selective rate firming but persistent profitability pressure on small carriers caught between record operating costs, driver shortages, and tariff headwinds.
- Current dynamics: Carrier capacity is exiting the market faster than freight demand is growing, creating a selective tightening that began in late 2025 and is expected to push rates 10-20% higher by late 2026. Spot van rates reached $2.41 per mile in late February 2026, up 20%+ year-over-year, while contract rates remained softer, signaling an early but uneven recovery. Operating costs per mile hit an all-time high of $2.34 in 2025, driven by fuel (roughly 21% of total costs), tolls, maintenance, and driver wages; this squeeze is unsustainable for small fleets with thin margins. The US Department of State paused new employment visas for truck drivers in August 2025, tightening the labor market on top of demographic headwinds (average driver age 46, retirement wave expected over the decade). Tariff impacts are material: a 25% tariff on imported heavy trucks started October 2025, and cross-border freight dropped 5% in mid-2025 (Canada-bound down 14.5%), extending the freight recession into 2026 despite selective rate strength.
