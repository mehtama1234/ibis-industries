### Allergists (Healthcare)
- Market size: USD 24.74 billion in 2025, USD 26.56 billion in 2026 | growth: 7.3% CAGR 2025-2026; 8.8-9.1% CAGR projected through 2033-2035 | margin: 14.4-14.5% EBIT, consistent with 2021 baseline despite reimbursement pressure | businesses: 3,417 allergists in 2023 (10% growth from 2015); number of practices fell from 1,782 to 1,585 over the same period | employees: Approximately 10,000-11,000 allergists and support staff (exact 2025 figures not published); 2026 projection is 10,527 workers
- 2022 baseline: USD 4.0 billion in 2021 (2022 specific figure not available from IBISWorld)
- Takeaway: Allergists are consolidating from solo/small group practices into multispecialty networks, facing Medicare reimbursement cuts, at-home testing competition, and margin pressure while the overall allergy care market grows 8-9% annually.
- Current dynamics: Consolidation is reshaping the landscape: practice count fell 11% (2015-2023) while physician count rose 10%, reflecting merger activity and group size growth (mean allergists per practice rose from 7 to 11). Young graduates (post-2010) show 65% adoption of multispecialty groups vs 42% for earlier graduates, signaling structural permanence. Medicare reimbursement declined 3.4% in 2024 (conversion factor fell to $32.74) and another 2.83% in 2025 (to $32.35), creating margin pressure. At-home allergy testing kits (finger-prick samples, $60-200 price range) now compete with office diagnostics, though most lack FDA validation. Telehealth services are expanding (17.4% annual growth), with patients able to conduct initial consultations remotely and receive personalized guidance without in-person visits. Allergy incidence has risen 100% per decade for rhinitis and 21% for pediatric peanut allergies since 2010, supporting stable underlying demand. Physician burnout affects 41.9% of allergists (2025, down from 43.2% in 2024), with staffing shortages and administrative burden as key drivers.
- Recent: October 2024: ALK-Abello received FDA clearance for AllerTest allergy skin testing devices (AllerTest-1, AllerTest-8, AllerTest-10), advancing automated diagnostic options. | 2024: Private equity healthcare M&A hit 1,049 deals with 621 add-on acquisitions to 383 unique platform companies; allergy clinics integrated into dermatology/ENT platforms (Elevate ENT, SENTA Partners) to boost margins. | January 2025: FDA released final guidance on food allergen labeling (Edition 5), requiring risk-based allergen threshold framework discussions. | February-April 2025: FDA held virtual public meetings on food allergen thresholds and potential applications, engaging allergists, manufacturers, and consumer groups.

### Chiropractors in the US (Healthcare)
- Market size: $24.0 billion (2026) | growth: 2.6% annual growth (2026); 2.2% CAGR (2021-2026) | margin: 19.5%-19.7% (stable but compressed by rising costs) | businesses: ~38,000 individual chiropractors; 70,000+ establishments; top 50 companies less than 10% of market | employees: 186,328 (2026); expected to reach ~190,000+ by 2027
- 2022 baseline: $19.5 billion (2022)
- Takeaway: The US chiropractors industry operates at $24 billion in 2026 with 2.6% annual growth, facing disruption from venture-backed digital musculoskeletal startups, insurance pressure, and consolidation among solo practices seeking to survive rising administrative costs.
- Current dynamics: The industry sits at an inflection point between small-practice tradition and corporate consolidation. Franchise models like The Joint are winning on scale and brand (950+ locations, Entrepreneur recognition July 2026) while traditional independents face compounding pressure: insurance reimbursement rates shrink annually, cash patients grow price-sensitive due to high deductibles and cost-of-living stress (a major 2025 trend), and digital competitors are capturing market share by offering objective AI-driven diagnostics, employer contracts at scale, and remote monitoring that traditional chiropractors struggle to match. Private equity is actively hunting platform acquisition targets at 2.8x-3.8x EBITDA multiples, signaling confidence in consolidation profitability. Medicare expansion remains the industry's holy grail: the Chiropractic Medicare Coverage Modernization Act would unlock evaluation, diagnostic imaging, and non-manipulation services (not just subluxation correction), but as of August 2026 it has 167 House cosponsors and stalled—leaving practitioners in a holding pattern.
