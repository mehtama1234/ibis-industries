### 3D Printer Manufacturing (Manufacturing)
- Market size: $30.5 billion (global 2025) | growth: 20.5% year-over-year (2025-2026) | margin: 0.8-2.8% (industry average) | businesses: 182 US manufacturers (2025) | employees: 5,569 (2025)
- 2022 baseline: $5.181 billion (2021, last IBISWorld figure)
- Takeaway: US 3D printer makers are racing to master metal printing and secure defense contracts as reshoring mandates drive domestic demand, while low-cost Chinese competitors erode margins in commodity machines.
- Current dynamics: Metal additive manufacturing has emerged as the fastest-growing segment, projected to reach $102 billion globally by 2035 at 24% CAGR. Aerospace and defense demand is the primary driver, with military orders and reshoring mandates (especially post-FY2026 NDAA restrictions on foreign 3D systems for DoD) creating new requirements for domestic supply. Industry consolidation is accelerating; Stratasys/Desktop Metal merger integrates polymer and metal capabilities into one platform. Simultaneously, low-cost Chinese systems (Bambu Lab, Creality) are gaining industrial relevance despite import restrictions, putting pressure on pricing for entry-level machines. Profitability remains under pressure as new entrants proliferate and material costs fluctuate, though aerospace aftermarket parts are creating profitable margin opportunities. Reshoring investments and supply chain redundancy are now strategic priorities for manufacturers, positioning 3D printing as a de facto reshoring tool.
- Recent: Stratasys announced $1.8B all-stock merger with Desktop Metal (2023, ongoing integration) | Stratasys invests in Tritone Technologies for metal and ceramic 3D printing capability (2025) | 3D Systems and Stratasys ended merger negotiations, with Stratasys prioritizing Desktop Metal combination (2023-2024) | HP presented IF 600HT high-temperature FFF printer and new MJF systems generation at Formnext 2025

### Computer Manufacturing in the US (Manufacturing)
- Market size: $22.3 billion (2026) | growth: 15.3% (2026); 16.3% CAGR 2021-2026 | margin: 4.9% (2022) | businesses: 312 establishments (2022); 304 enterprises (2022) | employees: 17,373 (2026)
- 2022 baseline: $10.4 billion (2022)
- Takeaway: US computer manufacturing rebounds via server buildout but consumer PC demand collapses 13% in 2026 from component shortages and tariff uncertainty.
- Current dynamics: The market is cleaved between consumer and enterprise. Consumer PC shipments hit 18.2 million units in Q4 2025 (3% growth), but Q1 2026 dropped 7% to 15.8 million units as DRAM and SSD costs surged 40–70% year-to-date, with further 60% increases expected in Q1 2026. Predictions now show 13% annual decline for 2026 and no recovery to 2025 levels until 2029. Conversely, AI servers are the bright spot: Dell shipped record $113.5 billion revenue in FY2026 (ending Jan 30) with 40% growth in infrastructure solutions and a $43 billion order backlog. HP's PC revenue grew 11% in Q1 FY2026 despite the broader market weakness. Tariff policy is volatile—computers were exempted from reciprocal tariffs in April 2025 but the Administration plans separate computer tariffs within months; Chinese laptop exports to the US fell 47.6% YoY in April 2025. Semiconductor reshoring is accelerating: Intel's Fab 52 in Arizona now produces 18A chips, TSMC is expanding Arizona operations, and Samsung/SK Hynix are building US fabs, all supported by $52.7 billion in CHIPS Act funding spread across 18 planned factor
- Recent: January 15, 2026: US and Taiwan sign $250B direct-investment semiconductor agreement to strengthen US chip reshoring (Taiwanese firms commit to US fabs) | CES 2026 (January): Intel launches first 18A chip production at Arizona Fab 52; Panther Lake (Core Ultra 300) claims 50% CPU/GPU perf gain; CEO notes AI demand exceeds capacity | April 2025: Trump administration exempts computers (and smartphones, parts) from reciprocal tariffs but signals plans for separate computer tariffs within 1–2 months | Q1 2026 (March): US PC shipments drop 7% to 15.8M units; Omdia forecasts 13% annual decline for 2026 due to memory/storage cost spike of 40–70% YoY

### Electrical Equipment Manufacturing in the US (Manufacturing)
- Market size: $81.4 billion (2026) | growth: 7.8% in 2026, 9.3% CAGR 2021-2026 | margin: ~5.5% (2022, now under pressure from tariffs and material costs) | businesses: ~1,639 establishments (2026) | employees: ~130,000
- 2022 baseline: $46.1 billion
- Takeaway: Strong grid modernization and data-center investment drive an $81.4 billion market (2026, up 7.8%), but tariffs and material inflation squeeze profitability.
- Current dynamics: The industry is caught in a squeeze between strong downstream demand and rising input costs. Copper prices jumped 18% in 2025, steel conduit costs rose 14%, and tariffs can add another 25% to material spend—all hitting an industry with modest 5.5% profit margins (2022). Switchgear lead times have doubled to over 26 weeks, throttling project schedules. On the bright side, grid investment is ramping: US utilities are spending $84.9 billion on transmission and distribution in 2025 and building over 26,000 miles of new lines to connect distant solar and wind farms to cities. Data centers alone are expected to lift annual electricity demand above 2% through 2026, more than twice the historical average. The big manufacturers—Eaton, Schneider Electric, Siemens, ABB—are all investing heavily in US capacity (Siemens' new Fort Worth plant is hiring 480+, ABB is expanding Richmond and Mississippi), hedging against tariffs and betting on sustained demand.
- Recent: Aug 2025: Eaton acquires Resilient Power Systems (solid-state transformer tech), Schneider Electric acquires WattBuy (energy plan platform) | July 2025: Trump administration imposes 50% tariff on copper products effective Aug 1, 2025 (impacts motors, transformers, wiring) | June 2025: Eaton announces $1.55B acquisition of Ultra PCS (UK-based power control systems) to close by end of 2025 | Q4 2025: ABB's Richmond VA facility opens, doubling footprint and adding ~100 jobs immediately, target 200 total; Siemens Fort Worth plant (opened 2025) already hired 480 and targeting 800 total
