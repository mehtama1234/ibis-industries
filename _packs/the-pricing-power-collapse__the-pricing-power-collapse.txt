### Orthopedic Products Manufacturing (Healthcare)
- Market size: $60.6 billion (2025) | growth: 4.3% CAGR (2026-2035) | margin: 5.6% (2022 baseline) | businesses: 480+ establishments (2025) | employees: 26,614+ workers (2022 baseline; growing)
- 2022 baseline: $11.8 billion (2022 US manufacturing revenue)
- Takeaway: US orthopedic products manufacturing is consolidating around robotic surgery platforms while struggling with Medicare reimbursement cuts and margin compression, with an aging population providing steady demand offset by intense pricing pressure.
- Current dynamics: Robotic-assisted surgery is reshaping the market: the robot-assisted orthopedic surgery market grew to $2.6 billion in 2026 and is expanding at 16-17% annually, far faster than the overall 4.3% industry growth. Stryker has taken a commanding lead by making MAKO the standard of care in US knee replacement. Pricing is under siege—Medicare reimbursement fell 26% from 2016 to 2024, with a 39% cut in hip arthroplasty pay since 2017; CMS applied a 2.5% efficiency adjustment in 2026. Consolidation continues aggressively: major companies buy up niche innovators to secure patents and expand product portfolios, and hospital systems are consolidating too, concentrating purchasing power and further depressing prices. The aging US population (10,000 Americans turning 65 daily, with 37% of diagnostic tests and 34% of inpatient procedures now driven by those over 65) provides steady demand tailwind. However, tariffs on imported components, negative pricing pressure (1-2% annually), and rising labor and supply costs are eroding profit margins.

- 2022 baseline: $1.13 trillion (2022)
- Takeaway: The US health insurance market has hit a pivot point in 2025-2026: insurers are regaining pricing power through massive premium hikes, but that power comes with mounting pressure from government cuts to subsidies, Medicaid shrinkage, and new tech entrants challenging their traditional moat.
- Current dynamics: The industry is in the midst of a painful but profitable repricing. After medical cost inflation eroded margins in 2023-2024, the Big Three are taking pricing power back aggressively in 2025-2026. Employer-sponsored premiums are rising 9%—the highest in 15 years—while ACA marketplace premiums jumped 18% for 2026 (and benchmark plans jumped 26%), exceeding what insurers proposed just months earlier. This tightening is being amplified by regulatory shifts: the expiration of enhanced ACA subsidies on December 31, 2025 means that unsubsidized individual premiums will more than double from $888 to $1,904 annually. Medicaid enrollment is shrinking for the first time since COVID (down 7.6% in 2025, expected to grow only 0.2% in 2026), thanks to new work and reporting requirements in the 2025 reconciliation bill. Meanwhile, tariff pressures from the Trump administration are raising the cost of medical devices and supplies, and new entrants like Amazon are siphoning off the most profitable segments—employee health, direct-to-consumer GLP-1 drugs, and primary care.
