- 2022 baseline: $21.8 billion (2022)
- Takeaway: A mature industry that underwrites income protection against disability is slowly accelerating through technology and gig-economy products, but faces tightening claims costs and uneven profitability across occupations.
- Current dynamics: The disability insurance market is bifurcating. On the group/employer side, adoption is steady but slow—68% of US employers offered Supplemental Long-Term Disability in 2024 (up from 64% in 2023), but awareness campaigns by carriers have failed to drive penetration much higher. The real pressure is on individual underwriting and occupational risk: carriers report higher claim incidence in certain fields and divergent claim experiences (some report favorable activity with lower incidence and higher termination rates; others face elevated costs). The gig economy remains a massive white space—70+ million Americans are gig workers but only 5% have short-term disability access, though Prudential's recent launch of a gig-focused product signals market entry. Technology adoption is the operational battleground: Unum partnered with Microsoft in Q3 2024 to deploy AI claims automation; MetLife rolled out an AI platform in early 2025; Prudential partnered with EvolutionIQ.
