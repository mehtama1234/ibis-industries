### Architects in the US (Business Services)
- Market size: $63.5 billion in 2026 | growth: 0.1% in 2026; 1.5% CAGR over past 5 years (2021-2026) | margin: 8-18% net margins typical; top-quartile firms reach 20-22%; smaller firms average 8-12% | businesses: 67785 firms nationwide; declining at 0.2% CAGR | employees: Approximately 240000-250000
- 2022 baseline: $51.8 billion
- Takeaway: The architecture industry is stalling, with market growth near zero, firm billings declining, and smaller practices squeezed while AI tools reshape workflows and PE-backed consolidation accelerates.
- Current dynamics: Billings collapsed because commercial office construction cratered. The Architecture Billings Index hit 47.6 in October 2025 (below 50 means declining), with 38 percent of firms reporting falling revenue and small firms under 250K annually expecting a 7 percent drop. Project pipelines are drying: clients hesitate to sign contracts, deals shrink, and 70 percent of declining firms cite fewer new projects ahead. Small independent practices are hurting worst, facing pressure to merge or close. AI software now automates site planning, specification writing, code-violation detection, and BIM workflows, shifting demand toward firms that integrate these tools while threatening traditional drafting and junior roles. Meanwhile, record office vacancy rates make commercial real estate play double-duty as both a client problem (why build more offices?) and a hard headwind on architecture demand.
