- Market size: $77.1 billion (2026) | growth: 1.6% (2026) | margin: 8.8% (2022) | businesses: 44,600+ establishments (2022 baseline) | employees: 417,098 (2022 baseline)
- 2022 baseline: $73.6 billion (2022)
- Takeaway: The $77 billion US auto parts stores industry (2026) faces slowing growth (1.6% in 2026) as electric vehicles require fewer traditional repairs, e-commerce takes online sales, and tariffs on imported parts raise costs.
- Current dynamics: The industry is being remade by three colliding forces. First, electric vehicles are structurally shrinking the parts basket per vehicle: EVs need no oil changes, fewer spark plugs, simpler drivetrains, and less frequent brake service due to regenerative braking. Second, e-commerce—led by Amazon ($13.7 billion 2026) but also RockAuto, eBay Motors, and Walmart—has captured the price-sensitive DIY buyer; U.S. online auto parts sales (excluding marketplaces) reached ~$23 billion in 2025 and are forecast at 4.6% annual growth, while including Amazon/eBay/Walmart the total channel grows 6.7% annually. Third, tariff volatility (25% on Chinese parts from April 2025, fluctuating with trade negotiations) has raised wholesale costs for retailers and made inventory planning unpredictable. Meanwhile, the average vehicle age climbed to 12.8 years in 2025 (a record), which theoretically should drive parts demand for aging fleets, but this benefit is offset by EV adoption and fewer DIY repairs as modern cars become too complex for amateur mechanics.
