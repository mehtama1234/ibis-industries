- Takeaway: The industry is enjoying strong nominal growth driven by pent-up leisure demand and blockbuster new attractions, but real per-guest margins are being eroded by sticky labor costs, tariff-driven equipment expenses, and mounting pressure from consumers increasingly pricing-conscious about family vacations.
- Current dynamics: The industry is caught between two opposing currents. On one side, pent-up demand for experiential leisure remains robust—Disney's domestic parks topped 76.5 million visitors in 2024 (up 0.6% year-over-year), and Universal's new Epic Universe theme park is projected to deliver 5.2 million visitors in 2025 (its opening year) climbing to 9.2 million by 2026, potentially boosting Universal Orlando's annual attendance by 50%. On the other side, affordability is becoming a visible brake. Disney raised its peak-day ticket prices to $224 at Disneyland and over $200 at Magic Kingdom in October 2025, while simultaneously offering deeper discounts to maintain traffic—a sign that consumers are becoming more price-sensitive. The Cedar Fair-Six Flags merger (completed July 2024) created a combined operator controlling 27 regional parks but facing a $5.5 billion debt load and declining attendance (down 1.4 million in Q2 2025 versus the prior year).
