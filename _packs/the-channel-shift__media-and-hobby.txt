- 2022 baseline: $819.0 million (2022)
- Takeaway: A $1.2 billion specialty retail sector in structural decline, battered by e-commerce, tariffs, and the bankruptcy of a major competitor, where only omnichannel giants and nimble independents can survive.
- Current dynamics: The art supply industry is caught between a collapse of traditional specialty retail and a boom in craft as a consumer activity. Joann Fabrics' bankruptcy and store closures in early 2025 marked a watershed moment—the end of another major chain competitor. Michaels has simultaneously accelerated its dominance, posting 40% earnings growth in Q3 2025 and 12% comparable store sales gains, by leveraging omnichannel (online + physical) capabilities. Meanwhile, tariffs hit hard in 2025: raw materials (Chinese pigments, European linen, Canadian timber) saw costs jump 22%, squeezing margins industry-wide and pushing smaller retailers toward the exit. Online retail now accounts for 56.8% of art supplies market share (2026), with e-commerce growing at 7.4% annually while physical stores shrink. DIY and hobby engagement is genuinely strong post-pandemic—Michaels' results prove demand for creative supplies is real—but consumers exercise price discipline and convenience-seeking, buying online or at Michaels rather than hunting specialty stores. ### Hardware Stores in the US (Retail)
- Market size: $42.8 billion (2026) | growth: 1.3% (2026); 0.2% CAGR (2021-2026) | margin: 3.4% (2022); pressured by competition | businesses: 14,082 establishments (1.2% CAGR 2021-2026) | employees: 154,000 (approximately)
- 2022 baseline: $32.9 billion (2022)
- Takeaway: A $42.8 billion fragmented retail sector in 2026, dominated by big-box chains that control 56% of the market while small independent hardware stores close at record rates, fighting to survive against rising tariffs, labor costs, and the shift to omnichannel shopping.
- Current dynamics: The industry is anchored in a sustained DIY movement: 74% of homeowners do maintenance projects, and 24% either do work themselves or hire labor while buying materials directly. Renovation-over-relocation is the dominant trend—with mortgage rates high and new construction stalled (single-family permits hit a seven-year low in 2025), homeowners are choosing to improve current homes rather than move. Tariffs on imports now run 10-12.5% (as of mid-2026), pushing supply costs up 15-30% for multi-site operations, with lead times stretched from 6-8 weeks to 16-24 weeks. Small independent stores face a brutal squeeze: cooperative consolidation accelerated when Do it Best acquired bankrupt True Value in late 2024, becoming the world's largest hardware cooperative, yet even cooperative members report closures due to inflation, unaffordable rents, and declining foot traffic.
