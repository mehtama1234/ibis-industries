- Takeaway: Airline catering is a low-margin, high-volatility business where catering companies scramble to manage fuel price shocks and tariffs while airlines cut back economy meal service and shift growth to premium cabins, all against a backdrop of seven-year-long labor disputes and emerging AI waste-reduction technologies.
- Current dynamics: Airline catering is being squeezed from all sides in 2025-2026. Fuel costs have become the primary driver—jet fuel spiked 30.9% in just one month (Feb-Mar 2026), and airlines have reacted by cutting economy meals even more aggressively, forcing caterers to chase premium-cabin revenue and fight harder on cost. Simultaneously, tariffs on imported food and packaging materials have arrived, adding new expense pressure that will get passed down the supply chain. Labor has become a flashpoint: LSG Sky Chefs workers have been in contract disputes for seven years with no settlement; Gate Gourmet workers prepared mass strikes at 30 airports in 2025; Flying Food Group faced worker protests at LAX in July 2026 over wage and safety claims. Technology is finally making moves too—Airbus and gategroup have deployed AI to track meal waste and optimize menu planning in 2025-2026, which should help caterers defend their thin margins. But the industry's core problem remains: airlines own the customer relationship and hold all leverage, so catering companies must absorb cost shocks or lose contracts.
