- 2022 baseline: $26.0 billion (2021, per IBISWorld 2022 report)
- Takeaway: Heavy engineering construction faces a 2026 cliff as federal funding authorization expires, while labor shortages and tariffs are squeezing margins.
- Current dynamics: Heavy engineering construction is caught between competing pressures. Federal infrastructure funding from the Biden-era IIJA has buoyed activity—New York committed a record $68.4 billion to mass transit (2025), and the FY 2026 BUILD program has awarded $18+ billion across 18 rounds—but authorization expires September 2026, creating a cliff that has contractors and states scrambling to front-load projects. Labor is the immediate crisis: 92% of firms report difficulty hiring workers, with construction wages rising 8-12% annually and shortages already delaying projects. Steel and aluminum tariffs of 25-30% are adding direct cost pressure, and many contractors are cutting margins (down to 5-7.5%) to hold prices steady rather than pass costs to cash-constrained state and local clients. Technology investment (BIM, AI automation, electrified equipment) is rising as firms try to close skills gaps and improve productivity, but adoption is spotty and capex-hungry.
