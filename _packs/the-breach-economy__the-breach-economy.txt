- Takeaway: Cyber liability insurance in the US is stabilizing prices after two years of decline while simultaneously tightening underwriting and seeing record claim losses, creating a bifurcated market where well-secured firms get cheap rates and weaker ones face denial or triple-cost surplus lines.

### Fraud Detection Software Developers (Technology & Digital)
- Market size: $40.4 billion (2026, US estimate based on global reports) | growth: 18–24% CAGR through 2033 (2025–2026 period) | margin: 8–12% (estimated, up from 5.9% in 2021 as scale improves) | businesses: 200+ active vendors; 50–100 material players; top 10 control ~40% share | employees: estimated 50,000–70,000 across US firms (extrapolated from 2021 baseline)
- 2022 baseline: $18.4 billion (2021 actual per IBISWorld)
- Takeaway: Fraud detection is shifting from reactive rule-based systems to AI-powered predictive intelligence, driven by explosive deepfake attacks, regulatory pressure, and the need for millisecond-speed decisions.
- Current dynamics: Deepfake attacks are reshaping the entire industry. In 2024, deepfake fraud attempts jumped 1,300% (one per month to seven per day). By 2026, 30% of enterprises no longer trust face-biometric-only identity systems. This is forcing fraud-detection vendors to layer in liveness detection, behavioral biometrics, and multi-modal authentication. Simultaneously, AI-powered fraud itself is scaling: over 50% of modern fraud incidents now involve AI-generated manipulation, and 71% of US companies experienced an uptick in AI-powered fraud attempts in 2025. Real-time analytics is displacing older predictive-model thinking. Vendors boast ability to analyze billions of data points in milliseconds. Cloud migration continues at scale, with enterprises moving detection logic into distributed microservices rather than monolithic platforms. Regulation is a structural headwind: GDPR enforcement hit EUR 1.2 billion in fines in 2025 alone; AI Act compliance adds simultaneous documentation burdens; and PCI-DSS requirements are tightening.
- Recent: August 2024: Experian acquired behavioral-analytics firm NeuroID to embed behavioral biometrics into fraud prevention. | June 2025: Valsoft acquired Alessa (AML/fraud prevention platform) to expand compliance tooling. | December 2025: Invictus Growth Partners invested $63 million to acquire controlling stake in Informed.IQ (AI-powered fraud prevention). | 2025: Feedzai acquired Demyst to access external fraud-data sources; raised $2 billion valuation.

### Digital Forensic Services (Technology & Digital)
- Market size: $3.74 billion (2025) | growth: 15.9% CAGR (2025–2026) | margin: Approximately 9% (2020 baseline); margins tightening due to AI/tool development costs and wage pressure | businesses: Hundreds of firms ranging from solo practitioners to public companies; top 5 hold ~38% share | employees: Approximately 7,355 (2020 baseline); updated figures pending
- 2022 baseline: $1.608 billion (2020, per IBISWorld; forecast $2.1 billion by 2025)
- Takeaway: Digital forensics is booming as ransomware and insider threats surge, but consolidation, encryption, regulatory compliance, and the push toward AI-driven investigations are reshaping how investigations work.
- Current dynamics: Cybercrime is not slowing: ransomware, insider threats (83% of companies in 2024), and AI-based attacks (deepfakes, impersonation) are accelerating demand. Regulatory pressure is intensifying—GDPR fines exceeded EUR 5.8 billion by early 2025, CCPA enforcement tightens in 2026, and 21 US states now have comprehensive privacy laws. Cloud adoption and remote work have exploded the volume of data to investigate, but encryption and privacy rules are making access harder. The industry is moving from reactive (investigating after a breach) to proactive (continuous monitoring, threat hunting, evidence preservation). Managed services and outcome-based retainers are replacing one-time tool sales. AI and automation are reshaping investigative workflows—machine learning can now detect deepfakes with 92% accuracy and accelerate log analysis—but courts still demand defensible, transparent evidence, creating friction with "black box" AI models.
- Recent: Cellebrite acquired Corellium (ARM virtualization software) for $170M in December 2025, expanding mobile extraction capabilities. | Cellebrite agreed to acquire SCG Canada (handheld UAV forensics) in February 2026, closed March 1, 2026. | OpenText acquired ADF Solutions for $185M in 2025, consolidating enterprise litigation workflows. | Magnet Forensics integrated Axiom with Microsoft Sentinel in February 2026, automating evidence capture from Azure and Microsoft 365.
