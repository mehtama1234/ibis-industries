### Health Stores in the US (Retail)
- Market size: $60.7 billion (2025) | growth: 3.8% year-over-year in 2025; 7.2% CAGR 2020-2025 | margin: 5.5% (2022 baseline); likely compressing in 2025-2026 due to tariffs and online competition | businesses: 98,303 establishments in 2022; highly fragmented with most regional/independent operators; major branded chains: The Vitamin Shoppe (acquired by private equity 2025), GNC (post-bankruptcy restructuring 2024-2026) | employees: 178,276 in 2022; minimal employment growth forecast through 2026
- 2022 baseline: $19.8 billion (2022)
- Takeaway: The brick-and-mortar health stores sector is stalled—market growth is slowing, major chains are in bankruptcy or being sold to private equity, and tariffs on vitamins and supplements are forcing difficult cost-pass-through decisions in 2025-2026.
- Current dynamics: The industry is caught between irreconcilable forces. Online supplements are growing 11.4% annually while brick-and-mortar stagnates; consumers buy vitamins from Amazon at lower prices, often not differentiating between generic and specialty brands. The two largest regional chains (The Vitamin Shoppe, GNC) filed for bankruptcy, meaning their store closures and asset sales to private equity will reduce physical footprint by 15-25% through 2026. Tariffs impose a 20-30% cost wedge on imported ingredients (China dependency is acute), forcing retailers to choose between raising shelf prices and accepting margin compression. At the same time, upstream market consolidation among supplement manufacturers is creating new private-label entrants that bypass specialty retail entirely (e.g., Target's exclusive personalized vitamin line in 2021-2022). Consumers are migrating toward personalized and convenience formats (gummies, subscription boxes) which specialty stores struggle to stock efficiently; personalized nutrition alone is growing at 15% annually.
