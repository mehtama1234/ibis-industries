### Wine Bars (Food & Drink)
- Market size: $3.4 billion (2026) | growth: -0.7% (2026) | margin: 6.4% (2026 projected) | businesses: ~1,675 establishments (2026 estimate) | employees: ~10,500 (2026 estimate)
- 2022 baseline: $1.9 billion
- Takeaway: Wine bars remain a mature, experience-driven niche for affluent, older consumers, but face structural headwinds from collapsing Gen Z engagement, GLP-1 adoption reducing alcohol spending, and a 90-year low in U.S. drinking rates, forcing industry bifurcation toward premium-experiential operators and margin compression for traditional independents.
- Current dynamics: The industry is bifurcating sharply. Modern wine bars scaled by private equity are growing 6.8% CAGR and investing in experiential elements (staff guidance, events), achieving 22% higher retention than traditional formats. However, the category faces a structural demand wall: Gen Z drinks 30% less than Millennials at the same age; 34% of Gen Z monthly wine occasions have collapsed since 2019; and U.S. adult drinking hit a 54% participation rate in August 2025—the lowest in 90 years. GLP-1 medications (now used by 10–15% of U.S. adults) reduce alcohol spending by 30% on-premise and 22% for home consumption. The 2025 Dietary Guidelines softened alcohol warnings, removing daily drink limits, yet the U.S. Surgeon General simultaneously issued an advisory linking alcohol to nearly 100,000 annual cancer cases. On-premise visits dropped 3.4% in 2025 vs. 2024. Affluent, older drinkers (55+) remain loyal, but that demographic is aging. Tariff volatility and discretionary income tightness add margin pressure.
