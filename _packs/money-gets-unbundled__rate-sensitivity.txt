### Invoice Factoring (Finance & Insurance)
- Market size: $3.0 billion (2026) | growth: -1.9% decline (2025), -4.4% CAGR (2020-2025) | margin: 29.3% (2025) | businesses: 247 factoring companies (2025) | employees: ~5,000-5,200 (2025 est., down from 5,254 in 2022)
- 2022 baseline: $3.9 billion
- Takeaway: US invoice factoring, a $3 billion mature industry facing structural decline from bank competition and rate sensitivity, is being revitalized by AI-powered credit tech and embedded-finance platforms that automate workflows and reach new SMB segments.
- Current dynamics: The industry is bifurcating: transportation factoring remains resilient due to persistent cash flow challenges and freight delays, but general commercial factoring faces severe competition from cheaper bank lending. The 2023-2024 freight recession drove spot rates below operating costs, forcing small carriers out and increasing factoring demand among survivors—yet overall invoice volumes fell 5.7% in 2024 vs 2023. Interest rates have been the swing factor: elevated rates through 2024 made factoring competitive against bank loans, but anticipated rate cuts are reversing that advantage. Technology is now table-stakes: AI and machine learning power real-time credit scoring, fraud detection, and automation, attracting fintech platforms to embed factoring into their APIs. Embedded-finance players (Lendflow, etc.) are bundling factoring alongside loans and merchant services, reaching SMBs who bypassed traditional factors. Regulatory pressure is rising—FDIC consent orders against BaaS sponsor banks in 2024-2025 have raised compliance costs and slowed fintech partnerships.
- Recent: 2023-2024: Freight recession hits transportation factoring; spot rates fall below operating costs; invoice volumes down 5.7% year-over-year by Dec 2024 (Triumph Financial 10-K, CASS freight index) | 2024-2025: Federal Reserve interest rate cuts after May 2024 high; factoring rates adjust downward (1%-4% range narrows toward 1.95%-4.0% for trucking); BaaS sponsor banks receive FDIC consent orders, raising compliance costs for embedded-finance factoring platforms | 2025-2026: Embedded-finance platforms (Lendflow, etc.) begin bundling invoice factoring with loans and payments; AI/ML adoption becomes standard for risk evaluation and fraud detection; global embedded finance market projects $155.96 billion in 2026, with factoring as core component | 2025: Market analysts forecast global invoice factoring to grow 10.5% CAGR (2024-2034), reaching $7.75 billion by 2034, contradicting US-only decline trend—suggesting growth concentrated internationally

### Loan Brokers in the US (Finance & Insurance)
- Market size: $9.1 billion (2026) | growth: -1.9% (2026); -17.1% CAGR (2021-2026) | margin: 18-30% for solo brokers; 10-25% for teams; independent mortgage banks averaged 21 basis points (2025), up from 10 bp (2024); Q1 2026 ~$727 per loan pre-tax | businesses: Thousands of independent brokers and brokerage firms; major players include LendingTree, United Wholesale Mortgage, Guild Mortgage | employees: Approximately 46,000-52,000 (down 39% from peak); modest recovery in 2025
- 2022 baseline: $11.5 billion (2021 actual); $13.2 billion projected for 2026
- Takeaway: Loan brokers are a shrinking middleman trapped between online lenders, direct bank lending, stalled housing demand, and AI automation—despite modest 2025-2026 profitability gains, the industry declined 1.9% in 2026 after collapsing 17.1% since 2021.
- Current dynamics: Mortgage brokers are caught between two crushing forces: first, technology. Online lenders and direct bank channels have become so user-friendly that borrowers no longer pay for intermediation. United Wholesale Mortgage, the largest broker-enabled player, originated 422,120 loans ($164.3 billion) in 2025, but it competes directly against Rocket Mortgage's direct-to-consumer model. Second, the housing market is frozen. Mortgage rates hovered at 6.5% in 2025-2026—painful enough to push home sales down an estimated 400,000 units nationally in 2026. This shrinks the pie: fewer transactions mean fewer commissions, even if a broker captures the same percentage. Third, AI is automating the broker's job. Non-QM (non-qualified mortgage) automated underwriting systems now deliver decisions in under five minutes, removing the human expertise broker networks once provided. Fourth, consolidation is accelerating as small independents realize they have no succession plan and no competitive edge. Bayview Asset Management bought Guild Mortgage in November 2025; Pennymac acquired Cenlar's subservicing
- Recent: Bayview Asset Management acquires Guild Mortgage (November 2025) — strategic consolidation among independent mortgage banks | M&A activity accelerates to 62 deals in 2025 (vs. 37 in 2024) — driven by owners without succession plans and regulatory/profitability pressure | Independent mortgage banks post net production profit of $443 per loan (2025) and $727 per loan (Q1 2026) — profitability recovering but transaction volume remains weak | Online originations reach 45-47% market share (2025-2026) — online lenders and direct bank channels continue displacing brokers
