### Wine Bars (Food & Drink)
- Market size: $3.4 billion (2026) | growth: -0.7% (2026) | margin: 6.4% (2026 projected) | businesses: ~1,675 establishments (2026 estimate) | employees: ~10,500 (2026 estimate)
- 2022 baseline: $1.9 billion
- Takeaway: Wine bars remain a mature, experience-driven niche for affluent, older consumers, but face structural headwinds from collapsing Gen Z engagement, GLP-1 adoption reducing alcohol spending, and a 90-year low in U.S. drinking rates, forcing industry bifurcation toward premium-experiential operators and margin compression for traditional independents.
- Current dynamics: The industry is bifurcating sharply. Modern wine bars scaled by private equity are growing 6.8% CAGR and investing in experiential elements (staff guidance, events), achieving 22% higher retention than traditional formats. However, the category faces a structural demand wall: Gen Z drinks 30% less than Millennials at the same age; 34% of Gen Z monthly wine occasions have collapsed since 2019; and U.S. adult drinking hit a 54% participation rate in August 2025—the lowest in 90 years. GLP-1 medications (now used by 10–15% of U.S. adults) reduce alcohol spending by 30% on-premise and 22% for home consumption. The 2025 Dietary Guidelines softened alcohol warnings, removing daily drink limits, yet the U.S. Surgeon General simultaneously issued an advisory linking alcohol to nearly 100,000 annual cancer cases. On-premise visits dropped 3.4% in 2025 vs. 2024. Affluent, older drinkers (55+) remain loyal, but that demographic is aging. Tariff volatility and discretionary income tightness add margin pressure.
- Recent: August 2025: U.S. adult drinking rate reaches 54%, lowest in survey's 90-year history (Gallup) | January 2026: 2025–2030 U.S. Dietary Guidelines remove explicit daily drink limits and downplay cancer risk, sparking pushback from medical associations (AASLD, Lancet) | 2025: GLP-1 adoption reaches 10–15% of U.S. adults; studies show 30% reduction in on-premise alcohol spending, 22% reduction in home spend; Endocrine Society highlights promise in alcohol-use-disorder treatment | 2025: Gen Z monthly wine occasions down 34% since 2019; 71% cite health/wellness, 56% price sensitivity (Katz Research Group)

### Energy Drink Production (Food & Drink)
- Market size: $23.9 billion (2025) | growth: 9.7% CAGR 2020-2025 | margin: 10.3-11.3% (estimated 2025) | businesses: 251 establishments (2025) | employees: 38,954 (2025)
- 2022 baseline: $11.7 billion
- Takeaway: The US energy drink industry has doubled in size since 2022 to $23.9 billion (2025) as sugar-free positioning becomes mandatory and acquisition wars intensify to capture female and health-conscious consumers previously outside the category.
- Current dynamics: The industry is in the midst of a dramatic category shift. Sugar-free drinks surpassed full-sugar varieties in dollar sales for the first time, driven by 70% of consumers seeking to limit sugar intake. Red Bull launched Red Bull Zero in November 2024. Consolidation is accelerating: Celsius paid $1.8 billion for Alani Nu (female-focused Gen Z brand) in April 2025, then secured Rockstar through a distribution deal with PepsiCo in September 2025, signaling acquisition wars to capture younger women and health-conscious consumers. Employment grew 10.3% annually 2020-2025 to nearly 39,000 workers, while the number of businesses climbed 10.6% year-over-year to 251, showing continued new entrants. Convenience stores remain dominant at 65.5% of sales but losing share to warehouse clubs (19%) and supermarkets (10.9%) as bulk-buying and price-sensitive consumers trade impulse purchases for economy.
