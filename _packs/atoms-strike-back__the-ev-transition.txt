- Takeaway: Transmission manufacturers are shrinking as EVs eliminate transmission demand, hybrid sales offer only temporary relief, vehicle demand falters, and rising tariffs and input costs compress profitability.

On the other side, battery electric vehicles do not need conventional transmissions at all; BEVs dropped to 5.9% of market share by July 2026 after federal tax credits expired in September 2025, but long-term EV adoption remains a systemic threat. Light vehicle sales are skittish, hitting 16.3 million units annualized in July 2026, down 1.4% year over year, and tariffs, geopolitical tensions, and volatile input prices (steel, crude oil) have squeezed margins and triggered manufacturing job losses. ### Automobile Steering & Suspension Manufacturing in the US (Manufacturing)
- Market size: $17.7 billion (2025) | growth: -3.8% (2025); projected CAGR 1.6% baseline 2022-2027 | margin: 2.6% (2022); under pressure from tariffs and OEM price demands | businesses: 250 US companies (2025) | employees: 35,493 (2022 baseline); likely lower in 2025 due to revenue contraction
- 2022 baseline: $14.1 billion (2022)
- Takeaway: Tariff shock, electrification, and steer-by-wire are hollowing out the domestic US steering and suspension manufacturing base, leaving only global tier-ones with scale to survive.
- Current dynamics: Tariffs are the acute shock. The average effective US tariff rate spiked from 2.2% at end-2024 to 17.0% by April 2025, with China specifically targeting US steering racks and control arms in retaliatory duties. This forces manufacturers into a crunch: absorb the cost and destroy thin margins, or nearshore to Mexico and Europe to avoid duties, which collapses domestic US volume. Supply chain disruption ranks as the top concern across respondents (45% cite it), with inventory planning now unreliable under changing duty regimes. Simultaneously, the industry is undergoing forced obsolescence. Electric power steering is now standard across most compact and mid-size vehicles in North America and Europe by 2025, replacing hydraulic systems. Steer-by-wire (fully electronic steering with zero mechanical backup) reached production milestones in 2026 with Mercedes-Benz (ZF supply) and a Chinese EV (Nexteer supply), signaling the beginning of the end for mechanical steering columns.
