### Dairy Product Production in the US (Food & Drink)
- Market size: $153.0 billion (2026) | growth: 1.8% year-over-year (2026) | margin: 2.8% (pressured by input costs and price deflation) | businesses: 1,303 processing establishments; ~5,100 dairy farms in Wisconsin alone | employees: ~143,000 workers
- 2022 baseline: $145.4 billion
- Takeaway: Rising production and falling prices are forcing farm consolidation and bankruptcies while labor shortages, tariffs, and plant-based competition threaten traditional commodity producers.
- Current dynamics: Milk prices have collapsed — from a high in 2022, all-milk prices fell to $21.05 per hundredweight in 2025 and are forecast at just $19.25/cwt for 2026. This deflation is squeezing producer margins even as production rises (1.3% projected for 2026). Simultaneously, dairy farm bankruptcies surged 46% year-over-year in 2025, with 315 Chapter 12 filings and another 185 in the top 24 dairy states alone. Wisconsin, the nation's second-largest dairy state, has lost 76% of its herds over recent decades; consolidation is now structural. Labor remains the hidden crisis: the US shed 155,000 ag workers between March-July 2025, and robotic milking covers only ~5% of US farms versus 20-25% in Denmark/Netherlands. Tariffs arrived in August 2026 — 50% duties on certain Canadian dairy imports (whey, milk powders, casein) while exempting cheese and fluid milk. Cheese demand is resilient (24.5% market share), but plant-based alternatives are growing 13.8% CAGR and captured 62.8% of the organic segment by 2026, fragmenting the market.
