### Waste-to-Energy Plant Operation (Energy & Environment)
- Market size: $1.4 billion (2025) | growth: -1.3% CAGR (2020-2025); +2.0% in 2025 alone | margin: 18-25% EBITDA (2025), compared to 8.7% net profit margin (2021 baseline) | businesses: 9 private enterprises (2025) | employees: 1,870 workers (2025)
- 2022 baseline: $1.36 billion (2021, IBISWorld baseline)
- Takeaway: The US waste-to-energy plant operation industry—a $1.4 billion, nine-company sector operating 64 plants across 21 states—is at a turning point: data center demand for renewable baseload power and updated methane regulations (2027) create growth opportunities, but direct competition from landfill gas capture and advanced pyrolysis technologies, combined with electricity price volatility and construction slowdowns, constrain margins to 18-25% EBITDA and employment to 1,870 workers (down 5% over five years).
- Current dynamics: The industry stands at an inflection point. On the demand side, data center electricity consumption surged 17% in 2025 and AI-focused data centers are tripling power use, creating new contract opportunities for reliable baseload power; the tech sector signed 40% of all corporate renewable energy PPAs in 2025. On the supply side, landfill gas capture is intensifying competition—598 landfill gas systems now produce 64% of US renewable natural gas (2025), with 20 new projects launched in 2025 adding 39.9 Bcf biogas capacity; an updated methane rule (effective 2027) will push 705 additional landfills toward capture. Reworld's 2024 rebrand signals industry consolidation and shift away from pure incineration toward materials recovery and alternative fuels; the company processes 20.8 million tons annually and targets 25% more recycling/reuse by 2025. However, electricity price volatility persists—EBITDA margins range 18-25% when indexed (vs. landfill margins of 25-30%), and rising interest rates have slowed construction waste flows.
