### Gas Pipeline Transportation in the US (Transport & Logistics)
- Market size: $44.2 billion (2026) | growth: 0.9% (2026); CAGR 0.8% (2021-2026) | margin: ~39% (EBIT margin, stable from 2022) | businesses: 102 companies (down 2.7% CAGR since 2021) | employees: ~27,000+
- 2022 baseline: $37.4 billion (2022)
- Takeaway: Gas pipeline revenue stays flat at $44 billion but capacity is expanding at the fastest pace in 18 years, driven by LNG export ramp-up and data-center power demand.
- Current dynamics: Pipeline operators do not own the gas they move; FERC sets rates based on cost-plus methodology, insulating revenue from price swings but tying it to regulatory decisions. LNG export growth is the defining tailwind: US LNG capacity will grow from 11.4 Bcf/d (early 2024) to 28.7 Bcf/d by 2029. New pipeline capacity online in 2024 and 2025 totaled ~12.8 Bcf/d, with another 44.9 Bcf/d planned for 2026–2027 (70% already under construction, mostly from Texas shale). Data centers are the emerging wild card: AI compute facilities now consume ~40% of electricity demand in regions where they cluster, and natural gas supplies that dispatchable power 24/7. Major M&A activity reflects scale-seeking: Kinder Morgan posted record earnings in 2025 ($16.9B revenue, up 12%), and deal-making intensified (EQT-Equitrans merged at $35B; ONEOK formed JVs with MPLX and Enbridge on new Permian pipelines).
