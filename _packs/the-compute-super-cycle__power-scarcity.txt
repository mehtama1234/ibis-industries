- Market size: 536.2 billion dollars (2025) | growth: 1.4% to 2.7% annualized for transmission and distribution combined; 1.4 trillion dollars total transmission investment planned for 2025-2030 | margin: 9.7% to 20.8% varies by region; Florida Power & Light 27.4%, Mid-American Energy 27.6%, AEP Texas 22.2% (2025-2026) | businesses: 8,992 establishments (2022); highly concentrated around 5-7 major vertically integrated holding companies | employees: 404,877 (2022); growing at approximately 1% annually to 2026
- 2022 baseline: 476.1 billion dollars
- Takeaway: A constrained transmission grid chasing unprecedented electricity demand from AI infrastructure and renewables is forcing utilities into the largest capital spending and consolidation wave in decades while pushing rates and profit margins to record levels.
- Current dynamics: The industry is fundamentally constrained by grid capacity, not money or technology. Data centers will consume 75.8 gigawatts of power by 2026—nearly 90 gigawatts of total peak load growth—forcing utilities to accelerate transmission buildout and tech giants to invest directly in generation assets. The interconnection queue has become toxic: project approval delays cost money and threaten viability, with permitting and interconnection being the primary choke point. FERC is responding with landmark transmission planning reforms (Order No. 1920) and new rulemaking on large load interconnection (deadline April 30, 2026), yet compliance filings will take years. Wholesale power costs in PJM spiked 49% in 2025 with capacity costs up 262%, driven by data center demand outrunning supply. Battery storage is exploding—24 gigawatts planned for 2026 versus 15 gigawatts added in 2025—reshaping how transmission operators manage intermittent renewables.
