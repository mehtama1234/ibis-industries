Evidence industries
  • Dialysis Centers Healthcare
    The US dialysis centers industry treated roughly 516,837 patients on dialysis in 2025, generated $36 billion in revenue with a 3.5% annual growth rate, and faced mounting wage pressures and regulatory reimbursement limits despite demographic tailwinds from an aging, diabetic population.
  • Orthopedic Products Manufacturing Manufacturing

  • Ambulatory Surgery Centers Healthcare
    The $46.5 billion US ambulatory surgery center market is consolidating rapidly as hospitals and private equity acquire independents, while reimbursement growth lags labor cost inflation and new procedure opportunities fail to offset margin pressures.
  • Cardiologists Healthcare
    The US cardiologists industry—worth $67.2 billion in 2025 with 2.5% CAGR—is collapsing private practice, absorbing private equity deals, grappling with severe workforce shortages and reimbursement cuts, while AI and remote monitoring transform clinical workflows.

Longevity Turns Into a Financial Product Problem

Living longer creates demand not only for care, but for insurance, annuities, wealth planning, estate logistics, and services that price extended life expectancy.

retirement duration extending balance-sheet riskcare financing pushing households toward structured productsinheritance and planning services expanding with age complexityinsurers repricing risk around longevity and chronic disease
The Graying MarketMoney Gets Unbundled
Evidence industries
  • Life Insurance & Annuities in the US Finance & Insurance
    A mature, well-capitalized $1.1 trillion industry growing at 0.6% in 2025 but contracting 1.4% in 2026 due to interest rate normalization, though annuity sales hit record $464 billion in 2025 on the back of four million annual retirees and rising consumer demand for guaranteed income.
  • Long Term Care Insurance Finance & Insurance
    The US long-term care insurance industry is a $25.6 billion (2025) mature market dominated by Genworth and Prudential, struggling to balance aging-population demand against persistent affordability crises, regulatory tightening, and competition from public Medicaid programs.
  • Portfolio Management in the US Finance & Insurance
    The industry remains large and profitable in 2025-2026, but growth is increasingly captured by low-cost index, ETF, model-portfolio, private-market and wealth-platform providers rather than traditional active mutual fund managers.
  • Financial Planning & Advice in the US Finance & Insurance
    A fragmented industry facing historic M&A activity, technology-driven competition, and generational shifts in how clients expect to interact with advisors.
